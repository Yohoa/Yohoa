CHAPTER 9
Repairing the Past: Embracing Our Selves

第9章
修复过去：拥抱我们的自我

"At the moment of the trauma, the [child] is utterly helpless. Unable to defend herself, she cries for help, but no one comes to her aid. … The memory of this experience pervades all subsequent relationships. The greater the [child's] emotional conviction of helplessness and abandonment, the more desperately she feels the need for an omnipotent rescuer … [But] because [she] feels as if her life depends on her rescuer, she cannot afford to be tolerant; there is no room for human error. …"

"在创伤发生的那一刻，[孩子]完全无助。她无力自卫，发出求救的呼喊，却无人前来相助。……这段记忆渗透进此后所有的关系之中。[孩子]对无助与被遗弃的情感确信越深，她对全能救援者的渴求就越迫切……[但]正因为[她]感到自己的生命依赖于救援者，她无法做到宽容；不允许任何人为错误的存在。……"

(Herman, 1992, p. 137)

Regardless of whatever cruelties or losses they encounter, human beings are born with the capacities necessary to "keep on keeping on," even to live a full, rich, meaningful life—in spite of adversity. We come into the world with innate drives to attach, explore, laugh and play, bond with our social group, and nurture the young. Even as young children, we have a developing brain that offers us such resources as curiosity, compassion, creativity, and wonder (Schwartz, 2001). We also have the mental ability of imagination: if all is lost, we can still dream, still imagine a life we've never known.

无论遭遇怎样的残酷或失去，人类天生就具备"继续撑下去"的能力，甚至能够在逆境中过上充实、丰盛、有意义的生活。我们来到这个世界，带着依恋、探索、欢笑与玩耍、与社会群体建立纽带、养育幼者的先天驱动力。即便作为幼小的孩子，我们也拥有一个正在发展的大脑，它为我们提供好奇心、慈悲、创造力和惊奇等资源（Schwartz，2001）。我们还拥有想象的心智能力：即便一切都失去了，我们仍然能够梦想，仍然能够想象一种我们从未经历过的生活。

But, under chronic conditions of neglect, trauma, or frightened and frightening parenting, our bodies organize to prioritize the mobilization of animal defense survival responses and anticipation of danger (Ogden et al., 2006; Van der Kolk, 2014). The "luxuries" of normal attachment, exploration and learning, play, even sleeping and eating, take a back seat to hypervigilent attention to potential triggers and a readiness for defensive reactions. As important as it is to have parts whose hypervigilence and preparedness for action support survival under threat, it is equally important to have a part able to "make do" in even the worst of circumstances. The normal life child self smiles for family photographs and events, cares for siblings (or even for parents), goes to school, finds pleasure or mastery in the normal developmental "tasks" of childhood (playing with other kids, exploring nature, engaging in athletic or academic competition, getting lost in books, learning a musical instrument, and "adopting" supportive surrogate parents, such as teachers, neighbors, grandparents).

然而，在长期的忽视、创伤，或充满恐惧且令人恐惧的养育环境下，我们的身体会优先调动动物防御生存反应并时刻预判危险（Ogden等，2006；Van der Kolk，2014）。正常依恋、探索与学习、玩耍，乃至睡眠与饮食等"奢侈"需求，都让位于对潜在触发因素的过度警觉和随时准备防御反应的状态。拥有那些以过度警觉和行动准备支撑威胁下生存的各部分固然重要，同样重要的是拥有一个能够在最恶劣情境下"将就应对"的部分。正常生活的孩子自我在家庭照片和活动中微笑，照料兄弟姐妹（甚至照料父母），去上学，在童年正常发展"任务"中寻找乐趣或掌控感（与其他孩子玩耍、探索自然、参与体育或学业竞争、沉浸于书籍、学习乐器，以及"收养"支持性的代理父母，如老师、邻居、祖父母）。

Often, in taking a client's history, the therapist is drawn to the details of neglect, abuse, and family dysfunction that "need to be addressed" in therapy. Never trained to listen for the indicators of the child's drive for normality, their peer relationships, school life, or what kept that child "going on," therapist and client unwittingly paint a shared portrait of the client as a wounded victim, not a ingenious survivor. Annie provides us with a good example of what happens when the therapist neglects to be curious about the side of the child who "kept on keeping on" and focuses the therapy on the wounds and wounded parts. Despite the severity and chronicity of her neglect and trauma, despite coming from the "wrong side of the tracks," or perhaps because of these things, Annie's normal life self was always strongly driven to make the most out of whatever "normal" she could create or emulate.

在了解来访者历史时，治疗师往往被需要在治疗中"加以处理"的忽视、虐待和家庭功能失调的细节所吸引。治疗师从未被训练去聆听孩子追求正常的驱动力的迹象——他们的同伴关系、学校生活，或是什么让那个孩子"继续撑下去"——于是治疗师和来访者不知不觉地共同描绘出一幅来访者作为受伤受害者而非机智幸存者的共同画像。Annie为我们提供了一个很好的例子，说明当治疗师忽视对那个"一直撑下去"的孩子的一面保持好奇，而将治疗聚焦于伤口和受伤部分时会发生什么。尽管她所受忽视和创伤的严重程度和慢性程度如此之深，尽管来自"错误的那一侧"，或许正是因为这些，Annie的正常生活自我始终被强烈地驱动着，要从她能够创造或模仿的任何"正常"中获得最大收益。

An eager, bright sociable little girl, Annie's normal life self naturally evoked the support and attention of her teachers. Physically active and good at athletics, she discovered early on that owning a baseball mitt or bat was the best ticket to social connection with her peers. Although handicapped in peer relationships by the stigmata of neglect and poverty (dirty hair, unwashed second-hand clothes that didn't fit or match), she managed to compensate with the help of an exuberant personality and sports equipment purchased with her paper route and babysitting money. By the age of 8, as her mother's alcoholism worsened, she was put in charge of running her family's entire household and caring for two younger siblings, a burden to which she submitted for fear of physical abuse but which also earned her a repertoire of precocious competencies in cooking, cleaning, and childcare. Caring for two younger siblings who adored her and to whom she was safely attached provided an important normal developmental experience, one that most likely contributed to her later ability to raise two securely attached children and then "adopt" a series of surrogate children who found safe haven in her home. As an adult, friends, relatives, and neighbors consistently sought refuge and support in Annie's home. She often felt confused about why they would seek her out. The fight parts tried to tell her it was only because they wanted to use her since she was such an easy mark. The person she identified as "I" would never have drawn others to her, would never have been a "wise woman" to whom they would come for safety and support.

Annie是一个热情、聪明、善于交际的小女孩，她的正常生活自我自然地唤起了老师们的支持和关注。身体活跃、擅长体育，她很早就发现拥有一只棒球手套或球棒是与同龄人建立社交联系的最好通行证。尽管因忽视和贫困的烙印（脏乱的头发、未洗的二手衣服，既不合身也不配套）在同伴关系中处于不利地位，她还是凭借开朗的个性和用送报纸、做保姆赚来的钱购买的体育器材设法加以弥补。8岁时，随着母亲酗酒情况恶化，她被安排负责打理整个家庭并照顾两个弟弟妹妹，这一重担她因害怕身体虐待而顺从承担，但也为她赢得了烹饪、清洁和育儿方面超乎寻常的能力。照顾两个深爱她、与她安全依恋的弟弟妹妹提供了一次重要的正常发展体验，这很可能促成了她后来养育两个安全依恋孩子、并继而"收养"一系列在她家找到安全港湾的代理孩子的能力。成年后，朋友、亲戚和邻居不断地在Annie家寻求庇护和支持。她常常困惑于为何他们会寻找她。战斗部分试图告诉她，那只是因为他们想利用她，因为她太好说话了。那个她认同为"我"的人，永远不会吸引他人靠近，永远不会是那个他们为了安全和支持而前来寻找的"智慧女性"。

Despite the strengths and resources of her normal life self, Annie found her day-to-day experience dominated by traumatized child parts, with dramatic ramifications. Although she wished for more contact with others (and so did her attach part), the fight and flight parts held her back from acting on that wish. Instead, she was internally compelled to isolate, avoid going outside, cancel dates with potential friends, and never, ever answer the door or the telephone. Only those individuals seeking caretaking in her home were allowed to have contact with her. Ironically, caretaking had been the safest role she knew in childhood, and many parts did not want to give it up despite the scathing criticism of judgmental protector parts. Although Annie's normal life self wanted to have a routine that balanced regular eating and sleeping habits, household responsibilities and pleasurable activities, following a schedule was impossible. She and I constructed schedule after schedule to no avail. Any type of structure was assiduously avoided by her parts—a phenomenon often seen in clients with histories of neglect, even those with graduate degrees and professional careers. The case of Josh illustrates another example of what happens when the parts' implicit memories and perspectives dominate the individual and pre-empt functioning of the prefrontal cortex, disempowering the normal life self. Without the cortical ability to differentiate his grown-up self from trauma-related parts, he could not see his life or himself in perspective.

尽管她的正常生活自我拥有诸多优势和资源，Annie发现自己的日常体验被被创伤的孩子部分所主导，产生了深远的影响。尽管她渴望与他人有更多接触（她的依恋部分也同样渴望），战斗和逃跑部分却阻止她付诸行动。相反，她在内部被迫孤立、避免外出、取消与潜在朋友的约会，并且永远、永远不接门或接电话。只有那些到她家寻求照料的人才被允许与她接触。具有讽刺意味的是，照料他人曾是她在童年所知的最安全角色，尽管受到评判性保护部分的严厉批评，许多部分仍不愿放弃这一角色。尽管Annie的正常生活自我希望有一个能平衡规律饮食和睡眠习惯、家务责任和愉快活动的日常规律，遵循日程表却是不可能的。她和我制订了一个又一个日程表，均无济于事。任何类型的结构都被她的各部分坚决回避——这一现象在有忽视历史的来访者中很常见，即便是那些拥有研究生学位和专业职业的人也不例外。Josh的案例说明了另一个例子：当各部分的内隐记忆和观点主导个体并抢占前额叶皮层的功能时，会发生什么，从而削弱了正常生活自我的力量。没有皮层能力来区分他成年的自我与创伤相关部分，他无法对自己的生活或自身有整体的视角。

Josh was a very competent, successful, well-liked man in his forties. Respected by his colleagues, loved by his wife and daughters, accepted as "one of the guys" by his Wednesday night basketball buddies, a combination of intelligence, kindness, humility and humor endeared him to most people in his life. At times, he could see glimpses of this man others knew so well, but more often, the positive responses he evoked in others triggered a child part holding anxiety and self-doubt. "It's hard to take in anything positive, not when you know you're inadequate—when you know you come from nothing—or worse. I know the positive things are true of me, but I can't believe them." Attending a positive psychology weekend for couples, he found himself skeptical and at times even cynical: "It's so woo-woo. Say something nice to yourself and you'll feel better. But what if it's not true? I couldn't tolerate saying it and then finding out it's not true." Each time he had a glimpse of something beyond the inadequacy endorsed by his child parts, his cynical part would convince him there was no data to support any positive qualities in himself.

Josh是一位四十多岁的男性，非常能干、成功且深受欢迎。受到同事的尊重，被妻子和女儿们所爱，被周三晚上篮球伙伴们视为"自己人"，智慧、善良、谦逊和幽默的结合使他深受生活中大多数人的喜爱。有时，他能瞥见他人如此熟知的那个他，但更多时候，他在他人身上唤起的积极反应会触发一个持有焦虑和自我怀疑的孩子部分。"很难接受任何积极的东西，尤其是当你知道自己不够好——当你知道自己一无所有——甚至更糟的时候。我知道那些关于我的积极事物是真的，但我就是无法相信它们。"参加一个为夫妻举办的积极心理学周末活动，他发现自己持怀疑态度，有时甚至带着讽刺："这太玄乎了。对自己说些好话，你就会感觉好多了。但如果那不是真的呢？我无法忍受说出来然后发现它不是真的。"每次他瞥见超越孩子部分所认定的那种不够好的状态之外的什么，他的愤世嫉俗部分就会说服他，没有任何数据支持他自身存在任何积极品质。

Dawn also found her perspective distorted by whichever parts' intense emotions and reactivity "hijacked" her body and drove her impulsive behavior.

Dawn也发现，无论哪个部分的强烈情绪和反应性"劫持"了她的身体并驱动了她的冲动行为，她的视角都会因此而扭曲。

Dawn was 12 when she was taken out of the foster care system and sent to a Department of Child Mental Health residential placement. That was OK with her. Life had hardly been normal up to that point, nor had it been safe, and even though it didn't always feel that way, residential programs meant safety. Now 22, she had spent the intervening ten years in and out of residential care and hospitals. There, her chronic self-injury and eating disorder were treated as symptoms of borderline personality disorder and evidence of manipulative, attention-seeking behavior. Each time, her normal life part took a step forward, wanting more out of life, it triggered parts frightened of being seen, harmed, or abandoned. Without a window of tolerance, Dawn had no way of managing either her post-traumatic hyperarousal symptoms or her loneliness and emotional pain, which then tended to trigger vigorous efforts by the drug-seeking and eating disordered parts to alter her state. And if that didn't work, her self-injurious part would use cutting to mobilize adrenaline and thereby counteract the fear and vulnerability. Each time these parts hijacked her body and drove her to relapse or self-harm, it confirmed for the young terrified child part that there was no safety anywhere, increasing the panic that the fight and flight parts tried to shut down. Shown a diagram of the structural dissociation model by her new therapist, Dawn was surprised to feel an instant sense of recognition as the normal life self was described to her. "Oh yeah, I know that part! That's the part of me that used to promise myself that someday I'd get out of 'the system' and have a real home and family of my own. That's the part that kept me trying to get better all this time." Dawn, unlike Josh, immediately identified with her normal life part and felt relieved to give it a name and a purpose. It even began to feel real to her that maybe, after all, she could have a normal life!

Dawn 12岁时被带出寄养系统，被送到儿童心理健康部门的住宿安置机构。这对她来说还好。在那之前，她的生活几乎算不上正常，也不安全，尽管感觉并非总是如此，住宿项目意味着安全。现在22岁了，她在此后十年间在住宿护理机构和医院之间进进出出。在那里，她的慢性自伤和饮食障碍被视为边缘性人格障碍的症状，以及操控性、寻求关注行为的证据。每当她的正常生活部分向前迈一步，渴望从生活中得到更多，就会触发那些害怕被看见、被伤害或被遗弃的部分。没有耐受之窗，Dawn既无法管理她的创伤后过度唤醒症状，也无法应对孤独和情感痛苦，而这些往往会触发寻求毒品和饮食失调部分的强烈努力，以改变她的状态。如果这也不起作用，她的自伤部分就会用割伤来调动肾上腺素，从而抵消恐惧和脆弱感。每次这些部分劫持了她的身体并驱使她复发或自我伤害，都印证了那个年幼恐惧的孩子部分的认知：无处是安全的，增加了战斗和逃跑部分试图关闭的恐慌。新治疗师向Dawn展示了结构性解离模型的图示，当正常生活自我被描述给她时，她惊讶地感到一种即时的认同感。"哦对，我知道那个部分！那是我的那个曾经承诺自己有一天要走出'系统'、拥有自己真正的家和家人的部分。那就是一直让我努力好转的部分。"与Josh不同，Dawn立刻认同了她的正常生活部分，并因为能给它命名和赋予目的而感到释怀。她甚至开始感到，也许终究她能够拥有正常的生活！

Each of these clients had normal life selves with inherent inborn strengths and resources. Despite having never known "normality," even Dawn had the same drive to be her best self, the same curiosity and compassion for others, the same determination despite all the obstacles she had faced. All three normal life selves were strong-willed, creative, and instinctively knew what "normal life" signified for each of them. When their left-brained normal life selves were in charge, their personalities, identities, and values were well-defined. Sadly, because the normal life self blended so easily with the traumatized parts, yet also related to them as "not me" aspects, there was little communication or collaboration with the right brain-related parts. Feeling a sense of defectiveness because of their symptoms and difficulties, they were distracted from seeing how blending with their parts' trauma-related implicit memories and survival defenses was impacting their lives and sense of identity.

这些来访者每一位都拥有具有先天优势和资源的正常生活自我。尽管从未了解"正常"，即便是Dawn也拥有同样的成为最好自我的驱动力、同样对他人的好奇心和慈悲、以及面对所有障碍时同样的坚定。三位正常生活自我都意志坚强、富有创造力，并本能地知道"正常生活"对各自意味着什么。当他们左脑主导的正常生活自我掌控时，他们的个性、身份和价值观都清晰明确。令人遗憾的是，由于正常生活自我如此容易与被创伤的各部分发生融合，同时又将它们视为"非我"方面，与右脑相关部分之间几乎没有沟通或协作。因为症状和困难而感到有缺陷，他们分心于看到与各部分的创伤相关内隐记忆和生存防御的融合如何影响他们的生活和身份感。

Instead, the inner worlds of each tended to be more like battlegrounds: Annie's normal life self wrestled with parts terrified of the world they imagined lay outside her home, a world she had deliberately chosen for a normal life because it was so benign and gave her access to nature. Believing it would be as malevolent as the traumatic environment of childhood, her parts wanted none of it. Josh's intelligent, thoughtful normal life part lost conflict after conflict to critical and ashamed parts holding the conviction that no accomplishment or acceptance could erase the stain of having been "less than." Dawn's normal life self was quite literally on "lockdown," in and out of specialized programs, no match for the violence of her addicted, self-injuring, and eating disordered parts. The traumatic or disorganized attachment they had each experienced early in life created especially intense internal conflicts between the attach part's fear of abandonment and yearning for care versus the fight part's determination to defend against any and all vulnerability. Subsequently, traumatic events of all kinds (from bullying to human trafficking, homelessness to sexual abuse to domestic violence) had exacerbated these fears and further polarized the parts: parts holding implicit memories of fear, dread, and shame triggered parts driven by impulses to flee or fight for life—and vice versa. It was an endless cycle. Each part's belief system, reactivity, defensive responses, and emotions implicitly described its place in their histories and embodied the defensive responses needed at the time. Their conscious childhood memories were the tales of how they were victimized, never seen or never loved. Each failed to mention how they survived. Josh's story was that he would never outlive the humiliation, poverty, abuse, and neglect that characterized his childhood: he would never belong. Dawn's story was similar: she had never been wanted, never been safe, and she never would be. She might as well be dead. Not only were their normal life parts missing in their daily consciousness but also missing from their childhood memories. Flooded with the traumatized parts' emotions, impulses, and beliefs, each normal life self had automatically identified with the parts' implicit memories. Each was disconnected from a felt sense of ownership of the aspects of life he or she had deliberately created to support "keeping on" despite it all. Blended with the parts, all three identified with the traumatic lives of their childhoods rather than with their consciously created "life after trauma."

相反，每个人的内心世界往往更像是战场：Annie的正常生活自我与那些对想象中家门外世界感到恐惧的部分搏斗——而那个世界是她刻意为了正常生活而选择的，因为它如此温和并让她接近自然。这些部分相信那里将像童年的创伤环境一样充满恶意，完全不想要那一切。Josh聪慧、体贴的正常生活部分在与批评和羞耻部分的一次次冲突中败下阵来，那些部分坚信任何成就或接纳都无法抹去"不够好"的污点。Dawn的正常生活自我实际上处于"封锁"状态，在专业项目之间进进出出，完全无法与她那些成瘾、自伤和饮食失调部分的暴力相抗衡。他们每个人在早年所经历的创伤性或混乱型依恋，在依恋部分对被遗弃的恐惧和对关怀的渴望，与战斗部分抵御一切脆弱性的决心之间，制造了尤为强烈的内部冲突。随后，各种各样的创伤事件（从霸凌到人口贩卖，从无家可归到性虐待再到家庭暴力）加剧了这些恐惧，并进一步使各部分两极化：持有恐惧、恐怖和羞耻内隐记忆的部分触发了被逃跑或为生命而战斗的冲动驱动的部分——反之亦然。这是一个无尽的循环。每个部分的信念体系、反应性、防御反应和情绪都隐含地描述了它在各自历史中的位置，并体现了当时所需的防御反应。他们有意识的童年记忆是关于他们如何被害、从未被看见或从未被爱的故事。每个人都没有提到自己是如何幸存的。Josh的故事是，他永远无法摆脱那些标志着他童年的屈辱、贫穷、虐待和忽视：他永远不会有归属感。Dawn的故事也类似：她从未被人需要，从未安全过，也永远不会。她不如死了算了。不仅他们的正常生活部分在日常意识中缺失，在童年记忆中也同样缺失。被创伤部分的情绪、冲动和信念所淹没，每个正常生活自我都自动认同了各部分的内隐记忆。每个人都与那种对自己刻意创造的、用以支撑"继续坚持"的生活各方面的所有权感断开了联系。与各部分发生融合，三人都认同了自己童年的创伤生活，而非有意识创造的"创伤后的生活"。

The parts' disordered attachment and need for the approval and acceptance by others created a vulnerability to depending on others, leading fight and flight parts to perceive that they were still being used and abused—or could be. Their fight parts attacked their bodies, their credibility, even those closest to them—demoralizing the normal life selves and affecting their ability to be effective or to feel effective or both. None of the three could gain perspective without a trusting relationship between the parts and the self who could create safety, stability, and a life after trauma. When Dawn identified the little girl inside who had been left alone and unprotected to become prey for pedophiles, she was finally able to make the connection between her nightfall terrors and unsafe behavior. She felt an immediate protectiveness that impelled her to buy a little girl doll and doll bed so she could "see her and tell her I'm going to take care of her." But when Dawn's normal life part tried to reassure the little girl who got terrified every night as soon as it got dark, there was no emotional impact. The only interventions that eased the panic caused by blending with the child were restricting food intake and drinking alcohol. Not knowing that she would have to befriend the little girl first and win her trust before she would be able to believe the words of reassurance, Dawn was discouraged and, blended with her depressed submit part, she gave up trying.

各部分的混乱型依恋以及对他人认可和接纳的需求，制造了对他人依赖的脆弱性，使战斗和逃跑部分认为他们仍在被利用和虐待——或有可能被如此对待。他们的战斗部分攻击他们的身体、可信度，甚至那些与他们最亲近的人——使正常生活自我士气受挫，影响其有效行动或感到有效的能力，或两者兼而有之。三人中没有任何一个能够在各部分与能够创造安全、稳定和创伤后生活的自我之间建立信任关系的情况下获得视角。当Dawn辨认出内心那个被单独留下、无人保护而成为恋童癖猎物的小女孩时，她终于能够将她的夜幕降临时的恐惧与不安全行为联系起来。她感到一种即时的保护欲，驱使她购买了一个小女孩玩偶和玩具床，以便她可以"看见她，告诉她我会照顾她"。但当Dawn的正常生活部分试图安慰那个每晚天一黑就感到恐惧的小女孩时，没有任何情感上的影响。唯一能缓解因与孩子融合而产生的恐慌的干预措施是限制食物摄入和饮酒。不知道她必须先与小女孩建立友谊并赢得她的信任，然后小女孩才会相信安慰的话语，Dawn感到沮丧，与她沮丧顺从的部分发生融合，放弃了尝试。

When Josh's judgmental, humiliating fight part was triggered by social situations, Josh's prefrontal cortex could not compete with its emotional intensity. Nor could he reassure the ashamed part or the part that felt he didn't belong. He was too blended with them. The normal life self on whom others relied for words of support and wisdom could not "be there" for his child selves. The parts did not seek him out either. The ashamed little boy and depressed 12-year-old parts had a long though troubled relationship with the fight part; they had no relationship at all with Josh's normal life self. How could they know he was there when he was blended with them?

当Josh那评判性、令人屈辱的战斗部分被社交情境触发时，Josh的前额叶皮层无法与其情感强度抗衡。他也无法安慰那个羞耻部分或那个感到自己不被接纳的部分。他与它们的融合太深了。那个他人依赖以获得支持和智慧之语的正常生活自我，无法为他的孩子自我"在场"。各部分也不寻找他。那个感到羞耻的小男孩和抑郁的12岁部分与战斗部分有着漫长而动荡的关系；他们与Josh的正常生活自我根本没有关系。当他与它们融合时，他们怎么能知道他在那里呢？

Although Annie went through the motions of relating to her parts, she had no emotional connection to the words she spoke to them. In fact, she was afraid to feel too much for her young selves, having found it overwhelming in the past. Historically, once she began to feel their fear or sadness, it would escalate into a tsunami wave of emotion. Her normal life self was exceptionally gifted with her surrogate children and students, had a sensitivity to their trauma, and knew just how to help them find their own normal life selves. She took on more surrogate children of an alcoholic mother in the neighborhood and began to teach them how to survive as she had taught herself. She fed them healthy snacks, made them do their homework, and, over time, went to their parent-teacher conferences. As teenagers, she drove them to work or school activities to make sure they got all the skills and capacities they would need for eventual independence. Annie taught me an important lesson about the effects of neglect: seeing that they had no support or structure but also no help in developing the capacities to feel safe on their own, independent of their mother, she could see that without help, they would never be able to leave. To be on their own without any skills for self-regulation or functioning was terrifying and made a big world feel even bigger.

尽管Annie走过了与各部分建立关系的步骤，她对自己对它们所说的话却没有情感上的联系。事实上，她害怕对自己年幼的自我产生太多感受，因为过去曾发现这令人不堪重负。历史上，一旦她开始感受到它们的恐惧或悲伤，就会升级为情感的海啸。她的正常生活自我在对待代理孩子和学生方面异常有天赋，对他们的创伤有敏感性，并且知道如何帮助他们找到自己的正常生活自我。她接纳了邻居中一位酗酒母亲更多的代理孩子，开始教他们如何生存，就像她教自己一样。她给他们提供健康零食，让他们做作业，随着时间推移，还去参加他们的家长教师会议。当他们成为青少年时，她开车送他们去工作或学校活动，以确保他们获得最终独立所需的所有技能和能力。Annie教会了我一个关于忽视影响的重要课题：看到他们既没有支持或结构，也没有帮助发展在没有母亲的情况下独立感到安全的能力，她能看到如果没有帮助，他们将永远无法离开。在没有任何自我调节或功能技能的情况下独立生活是令人恐惧的，并使一个大世界感觉更大。

But Annie couldn't access these insights and abilities on behalf of her own parts, especially when overwhelmed by the emotions of younger parts or the defensive responses of fight and flight parts. The result: when she was triggered, she couldn't forge a relationship with her parts because she blended with them and could not stay present. For the parts, it was as if they had just cried for help, and the person to whom they called out heard their screams and then walked away. The parts felt abandoned, just as they had when their favorite aunt "walked away" back to her own life even after she had seen what was being done to them at home. When she could feel differentiated and separate, Annie's normal life self kept a safe distance from the parts' emotions—aided and abetted by a professional self, the teacher, who preferred a left-brain understanding of the parts to any emotional connection. But to the parts holding memories of yearning for some sign of care or kindness, the teacher's unemotional reassurance that they were safe now was as often alarming as it was reassuring.

但Annie无法代表自己的各部分获取这些洞见和能力，尤其是当被年幼部分的情绪或战斗和逃跑部分的防御反应所淹没时。结果是：当她被触发时，她无法与各部分建立关系，因为她与它们融合，无法保持在场。对于这些部分来说，就好像他们刚刚发出求救呼喊，而他们呼求的那个人听到了他们的呼喊，然后走开了。这些部分感到被遗弃，就像当他们最喜欢的姑妈在见识了家里对他们的所作所为之后，仍然"走开"回到自己的生活一样。当她能够感到有所区分和分离时，Annie的正常生活自我与各部分的情绪保持着安全距离——这由一个职业自我，即老师，所助长，老师更倾向于对各部分进行左脑式的理解而非任何情感联系。但对于那些持有渴望某种关怀或善意迹象记忆的部分来说，老师那种不带情感的"你们现在安全了"的安慰，往往既令人安心又令人警觉。

Midway through a discussion of her fear of leaving the house or yard, I asked Annie to be curious about why some protector parts were still holding her on "house arrest," the term I used for her agoraphobia. I wanted to communicate that it was more than the fears of the young part that wouldn't even let her open the door. She was also being prevented from leaving by some unidentified protector part. As she reassured the frightened part and headed toward the door, her body would suddenly tense up until she was frozen and couldn't move.
Asking "inside" to find out more about why she was on "house arrest," she got the following communication: "They're worried about the bad people outside the house like the ones who grabbed you as a kid."

I ASKED HER: "That makes sense—they were very dangerous people, very dangerous. And you were very little. But didn't anyone ever tell the parts that they're safe from those people now? Did anyone bother to tell them that you took them far away from New Jersey a long time ago and brought them to a place where no one could find them? You did that for them and you. Did anyone ever tell them that you changed your name so you couldn't be followed? Do they know anything about the life you've built here for the past 40 years?"

我问她："这很有道理——他们是非常危险的人，非常危险。而你那时很小。但有没有人曾经告诉过这些部分，他们现在已经远离那些人、安全了？有没有人费心告诉他们，你很久以前就把他们带离新泽西州，带到了一个没有人能找到他们的地方？你为了他们和你自己做到了这一切。有没有人告诉他们，你改了名字，这样就没人能跟踪你了？他们对你在这里过去40年所建立的生活了解任何事情吗？"

ANNIE: "It didn't occur to me that I had to tell them—I thought they'd just know."

Annie："我没有想到我需要告诉他们——我以为他们自然会知道的。"

I challenged her but with a tone that spoke to the parts' needs to be told, not her failure to tell them: "How could they have known? As far as they know, they're still in New Jersey, and they are still little. No one takes care of them, and anyone could take them. They were fair game for any creepy person, and they believe they still are. That's why they're so scared." Reassuring her that she hadn't failed (she hadn't known about the parts then), I appeal to her compassion for children: "You didn't know it, Annie, but they experienced it as you leaving them there. If you didn't tell them you were going, didn't say you were taking them away from all that—if you didn't tell them where they were when you arrived in Maine, then they don't know!"
On another day in a session with Josh, discussing his shame and fear of failure after he'd been triggered by a business meeting with male peers, I asked Josh a similar question: "Josh, does that little boy know that he doesn't live in that place anymore? Does he know that he doesn't have to wear clothes that are too big for him anymore? That no one will make fun of him now? I bet he doesn't even know that people here look up to you! He just remembers being fat, not having the right clothes, not being able to play sports with the other boys—and he remembers the shame of being bullied. This, your life, isn't real to him … only the bullying feels real."

我挑战了她，但语气着眼于各部分需要被告知，而非她未能告知他们："他们怎么可能知道呢？就他们所知，他们仍在新泽西州，仍然是小孩子。没有人照顾他们，任何人都可以带走他们。他们是任何可怕的人的猎物，他们相信自己依然如此。这就是他们如此害怕的原因。"向她保证她没有失败（她那时还不了解这些部分），我诉诸她对孩子的慈悲："你不知道，Annie，但他们体验到的是你把他们留在了那里。如果你没有告诉他们你要离开，没有说你要带他们离开那一切——如果你没有告诉他们你到达缅因州时他们在哪里，那么他们就不知道！"
另一天，在与Josh的一次会谈中，讨论他在被一次与男性同龄人的商务会议触发后感到的羞耻和对失败的恐惧时，我问了Josh一个类似的问题："Josh，那个小男孩知道他不再住在那个地方了吗？他知道他不再需要穿着太大的衣服了吗？知道现在没有人会嘲笑他了吗？我打赌他甚至不知道这里的人对你刮目相看！他只记得自己胖，没有合适的衣服，不能和其他男孩一起打球——他记得被霸凌的羞耻感。这一切，你的生活，对他来说并不真实……只有霸凌才感觉真实。"

Annie and Josh both identified with their ashamed parts early in adulthood, facilitating their ability to be "seen but not heard" and minimizing the abuse in their families. They had a need for a "damage limitation strategy" (Gilbert & Andrews, 1998) to survive that conditioned judgmental parts and ashamed parts to dominate their inner lives and sense of identity for many years. Looking through the telescopic lens of their ashamed parts, neither could recognize, much less integrate, the healthy normality they had created in their lives as adults, the way they drew people to them. Both were inspiring examples of how the inborn drive to be healthy, conscious, compassionate, kind, and do meaningful work could surmount poverty, abuse, socioeconomic status, and post-traumatic stress. Neither, of course, could relate to such descriptions of themselves, so I kept these insights to myself and instead tried to evoke their interest and curiosity in the parts holding the shame. I encouraged each to thank those parts for helping them survive without losing their hearts or their souls.

Annie和Josh都在成年早期认同了他们的羞耻部分，这使他们能够"被看见但不被听见"，并将家庭中的虐待最小化。他们需要一种"损害控制策略"（Gilbert和Andrews，1998）来生存，这使评判性部分和羞耻部分被训练成多年来主导他们的内心生活和身份感。通过羞耻部分的望远镜式视角看世界，两人都无法认识到——更不用说整合——他们作为成年人在生活中创造的健康正常，以及他们吸引他人的方式。两人都是鼓舞人心的例子，说明健康、有意识、慈悲、善良和做有意义工作的先天驱动力如何能够超越贫困、虐待、社会经济地位和创伤后应激。当然，两人都无法与对自己的这些描述产生共鸣，所以我把这些洞见藏在心底，转而试图唤起他们对持有羞耻感的部分的兴趣和好奇心。我鼓励每个人感谢那些部分帮助他们在不失去内心或灵魂的情况下生存了下来。

Dawn, having never known "normality" outside of the mental health system, was especially out of touch with her normal life self's ability to function and to persevere. But, unlike Annie or Josh, she could unblend from her parts and have warm feelings toward the frightened part and respect for her fight and flight parts. Her treatment team took every opportunity to point out signs of the normal life part so Dawn could begin to recognize it as a force in her life despite the up's and down's caused by the parts' strong triggered reactions to her attempts to go forward.

Dawn从未了解过心理健康系统以外的"正常"，尤其与她的正常生活自我的运作和坚持能力脱节。但与Annie或Josh不同，她能够从各部分中去融合，对那个恐惧的部分产生温暖的感受，并对她的战斗和逃跑部分感到尊重。她的治疗团队抓住每一个机会指出正常生活部分的迹象，这样Dawn就能开始将其认识为她生活中的一种力量，尽管各部分对她尝试前进时的强烈触发反应造成了起起伏伏。

Accessing the Resources of a Wise Adult

获取智慧成人的资源

For many trauma survivors, the past as conveyed by the incessantly triggered implicit memories of the parts continues to be experienced as more "real" than the present. When that happens, the parts continue to relive and relive, over and over, the neglect, abuse, rage, fear, and dread—and to re-experience being abandoned by the normal life self just as they were abandoned by their caretakers years and decades ago. Annie, Josh, and Dawn illustrate this phenomenon.

对许多创伤幸存者来说，通过各部分不断被触发的内隐记忆所传递的过去，持续被体验为比现在更"真实"。当这种情况发生时，各部分继续一遍又一遍地重活忽视、虐待、愤怒、恐惧和恐惧——并再次体验被正常生活自我所遗弃，就像多年乃至数十年前被照料者所遗弃一样。Annie、Josh和Dawn说明了这一现象。

To break the cycle requires several steps on the part of the therapist: first, the therapist must challenge the client's automatic beliefs and habitual patterns so that the tendency to identify with the autonomically activated emotions and drives of the parts is inhibited.

打破这一循环需要治疗师采取几个步骤：首先，治疗师必须挑战来访者的自动信念和习惯模式，从而抑制认同各部分自主激活的情绪和驱动力的倾向。

Carla came to therapy in a highly activated state, having just had to take a leave of absence at work after she became too overwhelmed with fear, sadness, and a sense of numb unreality. As she described the past year of her life, it became clear that she had functioned highly for so long because her normal life self was supported by two trauma-related parts, one afraid of failure and one equally determined to succeed. This team drove her to develop a successful professional career and long-term relationship with her partner, but, I explained, "It was held together with rubber bands and chewing gum." Then her partner had an affair; she was mugged at knifepoint in her own neighborhood; and her father died, activating the trauma-related parts and completely incapacitating the normal life part. "No wonder you have been feeling 'not yourself.' Your parts staged a coup d'etat—probably right after you found out about the affair," I commented. "These emotions that have been overwhelming you belong to very young distressed parts."
At the next session, as Carla made connections between her reaction to the affair and a childhood spent parenting her mother and soothing her father to ensure her safety, I translated her narrative into the language of parts: "So that little girl was all on her own—with no one to help her take care of her mother or protect her from her father—she had to grow up very fast, didn't she?" Carla responded immediately to the language of parts: "She did—she couldn't afford to feel lonely and scared—she had to take care of herself because no one else was going to."
"That's how it felt when I found out Amelia was cheating on me. I felt all alone again."
Concerned about how overwhelmed she was by her feelings and their parts, I drew on her strong prefrontal cortex and scientific background to begin deconstructing the situation: we subdivided the stressors, differentiated her reactions to each, and then connected the different sets of feelings to the different parts communicating them. By dividing an overwhelming situation into smaller components, each could be experienced as more manageable. "So not only were the losses hard for the strong normal life part of you to come to terms with," I validated, "they were devastating for this young girl." I added, "Remember: a betrayal would be deeply distressing for any adult, but on top of that, it triggered the worst fears of your younger parts. Children are so much more vulnerable to feeling unimportant and unseen. They fear being abandoned more than they fear attack."

CARLA: "True, but even though the little girl was heartbroken, I put up a wall with Amelia—'you can't hurt me anymore because I am not really connected to you.'"

Carla："确实，但尽管那个小女孩心碎了，我还是对Amelia筑起了一道墙——'你再也不能伤害我了，因为我实际上与你没有真正的连接。'"

ME: "That sounds like a bodyguard part that didn't want that little girl's heart broken again! But if I recall, your normal life self also got on the phone and found a couples therapist right away. It doesn't sound as if you were putting up a wall—just trying to save your relationship. But someone clearly was! If you feel the wall there in your body, some part is responsible for it."

我："那听起来像是一个不想让那个小女孩的心再次破碎的保镖部分！但如果我没记错的话，你的正常生活自我也立刻拿起电话找了一位伴侣治疗师。听起来不像是你在筑墙——只是在努力拯救你们的关系。但显然有人在！如果你在身体里感受到那道墙，某个部分是负责的。"

CARLA: "The wall is still there—I can't take it down—I've tried. But maybe that makes sense if I didn't put it up?"

Carla："那道墙还在——我无法拆除它——我试过了。但也许如果不是我筑起它的，这就说得通了？"

A week later, Carla was back with good news: "I've been thinking a lot about the little girl and meditating—and crying a lot for her. I've been remembering what it was like. Before, I was so focused on my career that I never thought of what she had been through and how lonely she was, which is just what Amelia complained about! [She chuckled with recognition of the irony.] I've been taking good care of the little girl this week, holding her close to me, and the wall is softening a little—just a little. The wall wants me to know that it doesn't trust me yet to protect her."

Carla来到治疗时处于高度激活的状态，她刚刚不得不从工作中请假，因为她被恐惧、悲伤和麻木的不真实感所压倒。当她描述过去一年的生活时，清楚地表明她之所以能长期高度运作，是因为她的正常生活自我得到了两个创伤相关部分的支持，一个害怕失败，另一个同样坚定地要成功。这个团队驱使她发展出成功的职业生涯和与伴侣的长期关系，但我解释说，"这一切是靠橡皮筋和口香糖勉强维持在一起的。"然后她的伴侣出轨了；她在自己的街区被人持刀抢劫；她的父亲去世了，激活了创伤相关部分，完全使正常生活部分丧失了能力。"难怪你一直感觉'不像自己了'。你的各部分发动了一场政变——可能就在你得知那次外遇之后，"我评论道。"那些一直让你不堪重负的情绪属于非常年幼的、痛苦的各部分。"
在下一次会谈中，当Carla将她对外遇的反应与童年花在照顾母亲和安抚父亲以确保自己安全上的时间联系起来时，我将她的叙述翻译成各部分的语言："所以那个小女孩完全是独自一人——没有人帮她照顾妈妈或保护她免受父亲的伤害——她不得不很快成长，不是吗？"Carla立刻回应了各部分的语言："是的——她承受不起孤独和恐惧的感觉——她必须照顾好自己，因为没有其他人会这样做。"
"当我发现Amelia出轨时，那种感觉就是如此。我再次感到完全孤独。"
担心她的感受和各部分让她如此不堪重负，我借助她强大的前额叶皮层和科学背景开始解构这种情况：我们细分了压力源，区分了她对每个压力源的反应，然后将不同的感受组与传递它们的不同部分联系起来。通过将一个压倒性的情况分成更小的组成部分，每一部分都可以被体验为更易于管理。"所以损失不仅对你强大的正常生活部分来说难以接受，"我认可道，"对这个年幼的女孩来说更是毁灭性的。"我补充说，"记住：背叛对任何成年人来说都会造成深深的痛苦，但除此之外，它触发了你年幼部分最深的恐惧。孩子们对感到不重要和不被看见更为脆弱。他们害怕被遗弃甚于害怕被攻击。"
一周后，Carla带来了好消息："我一直在思考那个小女孩并冥想——为她哭了很多。我一直在回忆过去是什么感觉。以前，我太专注于我的职业，从来没有想过她经历了什么，她有多孤独，而这恰恰是Amelia抱怨的！[她会心地笑着，认识到这一讽刺。] 这周我一直在好好照顾那个小女孩，把她紧紧抱在怀里，那道墙正在慢慢软化——只是一点点。那道墙想让我知道，它还不信任我能保护她。"

It took very little for Carla to resonate immediately to her little girl part rather than identify with her. It was as if she had been waiting for someone to give her the language for something she already knew or sensed. Her compassion for the little girl was easily accessible, and perhaps because of her meditation practice, she could experience a felt connection to her mindful observer self while also feeling connected to the little girl and to the protective wall. Over the weeks to come, Carla guided the therapy herself from a wise mindful place, and I had only the job of supporting and commenting on the work she was doing. Sometimes, she chose to work on her relationship to the little girl and sometimes on strengthening her capacity to be calm, curious, accepting, and centered (what Richard Schwartz [2001] calls "self-energy") through her meditation practice. She felt her heart increasingly open and, with that, she was able to observe without judgment how closed it had been before. "I thought of myself as gentle and caring, but I'm sure that's not how other people saw me. They just saw the professional distance that I rationalized as the price I paid for success in my work."

Carla只需很少的引导就能立即与她的小女孩部分产生共鸣，而非认同她。就好像她一直在等待有人给她语言，来表达她已经知道或感受到的事情。她对小女孩的慈悲很容易获得，也许因为她的冥想练习，她能够体验到与正念观察自我的感受联系，同时也感到与小女孩和保护性的墙相联系。在接下来的几周里，Carla从一个充满智慧的正念之处自己引导着治疗，我只需要支持和评论她所做的工作。有时，她选择致力于与小女孩的关系，有时则通过冥想练习加强她保持平静、好奇、接纳和专注的能力（Richard Schwartz [2001]称之为"自我能量"）。她感到自己的心越来越开放，随之，她能够不带评判地观察到之前它曾经多么封闭。"我以为自己温柔而有关怀，但我确信其他人不是这么看我的。他们只看到了专业距离，而我将其合理化为我在工作中取得成功所付出的代价。"

Not all clients have the immediate response to or compassionate understanding of their parts as Carla did—or the intuitive access to her "wise mind" (what would be termed "self" or "self-energy" in the language of Internal Family Systems). Carla, for example, intuitively sensed the need to meditate as a way of developing a mindful relationship to the multitude of painful thoughts, feelings, and body sensations she and her parts were experiencing.

并非所有来访者都像Carla那样对各部分有即时的回应或富有慈悲的理解——或者对她的"智慧心灵"（在内部家庭系统语言中被称为"自我"或"自我能量"）有直觉性的接触。例如，Carla凭直觉感到需要通过冥想来对她和她的各部分所经历的大量痛苦思想、感受和身体感觉发展一种正念关系。

For other clients, simply shifting from a narrative approach to a mindful dual awareness stance might have taken many months. Clients are used to equating therapy with talking, "getting things off my chest," or "venting." Even certain parts can be very attached to narrative therapy because it gives them an opportunity "to be heard" or "heard and believed." Normal life parts may be attached to talking because their priority is problem solving the issues that are most troubling or most triggering on a daily basis and avoiding the "not-me" feelings. Child parts yearning for connection may feel soothed by the quality of the therapist's listening and empathy; parts focused on avoiding the trauma (or the emotions connected to it) might be attached to complaining and/or analyzing. But since the key ingredient in a parts approach is mindful, compassionate noticing of thoughts, feelings, and bodily reactions as communications from parts, the shift from narrating to noticing is crucial for the success of the work. Narrating will yield an understanding of a part's role in the client's trauma history or even in day-to-day life, but it will not heal that part's wounds or "earn" secure attachment. Just think of a small child crying: if the adult responds by saying, "The child is crying," or "I am crying," the child is unlikely to feel any comfort or relief. If the adult hears the teenager's anger and comments, "I am angry about that," or "It made me angry, too," or "Why did that make me so angry?" it is unlikely to have an effect other than to make the adolescent angrier. But these are what the parts hear when their feelings and reactions are narrated by the client as "my feelings."

对于其他来访者来说，仅仅从叙述性方法转变为正念双重觉察的立场，可能就需要好几个月。来访者习惯于将治疗等同于谈话、"一吐为快"或"发泄"。甚至某些部分可能非常依附于叙事治疗，因为它给了它们"被倾听"或"被倾听和被相信"的机会。正常生活部分可能依附于谈话，因为它们的优先事项是解决每日最令人烦恼或最具触发性的问题，并回避"非我"的感受。渴望联系的孩子部分可能因治疗师倾听和共情的质量而感到被安抚；专注于回避创伤（或与之相连的情绪）的部分可能依附于抱怨和/或分析。但由于各部分方法中的关键要素是对想法、感受和身体反应作为来自各部分的信息进行正念、慈悲的觉察，从叙述转向觉察对于工作的成功至关重要。叙述将产生对一个部分在来访者创伤历史中甚至在日常生活中的角色的理解，但它不会治愈那个部分的伤口或"赢得"安全依恋。想想一个小孩哭泣：如果成年人回应说，"孩子在哭"，或"我在哭"，孩子不太可能感到任何安慰或宽慰。如果成年人听到青少年的愤怒并评论，"我也对此感到愤怒"，或"它也让我感到愤怒"，或"为什么这让我如此愤怒？"除了使青少年更加愤怒之外，不太可能有任何效果。但当各部分的感受和反应被来访者叙述为"我的感受"时，这正是各部分所听到的。
