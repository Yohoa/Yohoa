CHAPTER 4
Learning to See Our "Selves": An Introduction to Working with Parts

第4章 学会看见我们的"自我"：与各部分工作的导论

"The essence of trauma is that it is overwhelming, unbelievable, and unbearable. Each patient demands that we suspend our sense of what is normal and accept that we are dealing with a dual reality: the reality of a relatively secure and predictable present that lives side by side with a ruinous, ever-present past."

"创伤的本质在于它是压倒性的、难以置信的，也是难以承受的。每一位来访者都要求我们暂时搁置对"正常"的认知，接受我们正在面对一种双重现实：一个相对安全、可预期的当下，与一个破坏性的、无处不在的过去并存。"

(Van der Kolk, 2014, p. 195)

"['T]he self' who was in the camp wasn't me, isn't the person who is here, opposite you. No, it's too unbelievable. And everything that happened to this other 'self,' the one from Auschwitz, doesn't touch me now, doesn't concern me, so distinct are deep memory and common memory. … Without this split, I wouldn't have been able to come back to life."

"在集中营里的那个'自我'不是我，不是此刻坐在你对面的这个人。不，那太难以置信了。发生在那个'自我'——奥斯维辛的那个——身上的一切，现在与我无关，与我毫不相干，深层记忆与普通记忆就是如此截然不同。……若没有这种分裂，我就无法重新活下来。"

(Langer, 1991, p. 5)

The brain and body are inherently adaptive, instinctively prioritizing survival demands over other drives, such as socialization, exploration, sleep and rest, hunger and thirst, and play (Van der Hart et al., 2006; Schore, 2001). In infancy and early childhood, the attachment drive is an even more powerful mobilizer than fight/flight instincts, reflecting the child's need to physically depend on parent figures. In adolescence, the balance shifts the other way: fight/flight responses are mobilized as or more readily than proximity-seeking behavior. When other defenses fail us or increase the danger, freezing and submission responses are automatic at all developmental stages. In the face of inescapable threat to life, the brain and body instinctively "feign death" (Porges, 2005), shutting down, going unconscious and/or limp, or "pretending to be asleep," as human beings often describe it. The innate ability to neurocept or sense the severity of the threat is accompanied by an instinctive tendency to engage the safest, most effective defensive response in a given context.

大脑和身体天生具有适应性，本能地将生存需求置于其他驱动力之上，如社会化、探索、睡眠与休息、饥渴以及玩耍（Van der Hart等，2006；Schore，2001）。在婴儿期和幼儿期，依恋驱动力甚至比战斗/逃跑本能更为强大，这反映了儿童在身体上依赖父母形象的需求。到了青春期，这一平衡向另一方向倾斜：战斗/逃跑反应的激活与寻求亲近行为一样迅速，甚至更为迅速。当其他防御机制失效或反而加剧危险时，冻结和顺从反应在所有发展阶段都会自动启动。面对无法逃脱的生命威胁，大脑和身体本能地"装死"（Porges，2005），关闭系统，陷入无意识和/或软瘫状态，或如人们常常描述的那样"假装入睡"。这种感知或判断威胁严重程度的先天神经感知能力，伴随着一种本能倾向：在特定情境中启动最安全、最有效的防御反应。

In the same way, human beings instinctively seek psychological distance from traumatic events or from "deep memory" to avoid being overwhelmed by the remembrance. To keep on "keeping on," we must psychically split off from what is happening right now, what did happen a moment ago, and what might happen next. Whether in wartime, in a concentration camp, or in the context of childhood abuse and neglect, some sense of self must be kept separate from the horrifying events around us even if that self just goes through the motions of life. To get up each morning and face death, abandonment, assaults, or imprisonment requires somehow disowning the horror and fear left from the day before and the dread of what is to come. Disowning "the other one" inside is a survival response: the overwhelming feelings are no longer ours; that shame does not belong to us but to "him" or "her;" the white-hot rage and violent impulses certainly aren't "me." By disowning our traumatized parts and/or "not me" self-states (Bromberg, 2011), by disconnecting from them emotionally or losing consciousness of them via dissociation, we preserve our hearts and souls from growing as bitter as our circumstances. We hold out hope for the future and we keep going.

同样，人类本能地寻求与创伤事件或"深层记忆"之间的心理距离，以避免被回忆所淹没。为了继续"撑下去"，我们必须在心理上与正在发生的事情、刚刚发生的事情以及可能发生的事情割裂开来。无论是在战时、集中营，还是在童年虐待与忽视的情境中，某种自我感必须与周围那些令人恐惧的事件保持分离——即便这个自我只是在机械地维持生活的表象。每天早晨起身面对死亡、抛弃、侵袭或囚禁，需要以某种方式否认前一天留下的恐惧与震惊，以及对将来的恐惧。否认内在的"那个他者"是一种生存反应：那些压倒性的感受不再属于我们；那份羞耻不属于我们，而属于"他"或"她"；那炽烈的愤怒和暴力冲动当然也不是"我"。通过否认我们被创伤的各部分和/或"非我"自我状态（Bromberg，2011），通过与它们情感上断开联系，或通过解离而失去对它们的意识，我们保护了自己的内心和灵魂，使之不会像我们的处境那般苦涩。我们对未来抱有希望，并继续前行。

Distancing from the trauma serves another important function in childhood, another way of surviving: it allows us not just to keep going but to keep growing and developing despite whatever befalls us. With distance or disconnection from the trauma, children can focus on mastering age-appropriate developmental tasks and developing a repertoire of functional abilities. When part of the child can concentrate on "normal" activities, such as going to school, can experience new learning and mastery, can play sports and make friends, there is an opportunity for normal development of exploratory and social drives. The going on with normal life self, unaware of what is happening or only dimly aware of it, might become a "parentified child" by day, a good student at school, a lover of nature or horses or books or making things with his hands. The worse the trauma or neglect and the less safety, the more distance will be needed from the knowledge of his or her emotional and physical vulnerability. For example, in times of war, in abusive families, or in a concentration camp, it might also be adaptive to disown normal physical needs, attachment-seeking, or the wish to be comforted. When we disown needs that can't be met or feelings that are unacceptable, we protect ourselves from unbearable disappointment or punishment (e.g., "I'll give you something to cry about!"). One way to accomplish this challenging task is to split the sense of desperate needing and the refusal to need anything between two parts: one part that actively seeks proximity, comfort, or needs-meeting and one that just as actively pushes others away or keeps a hypervigilant, suspicious distance. Disowning one's sad or lonely or needy parts, as well as angry, hypervigilant, or counterdependent parts, prevents self-acceptance and self-care, but it is safer. When the individual must adapt to an environment that punishes or ignores a child's basic needs and feelings, self-compassion too becomes "dangerous." It cannot be "me." Depending upon what best promotes safety and optimal development in each unique environment, children might have to identify with their angry, aggressive, hypervigilant parts and disown their innocent, trusting, attachment-seeking parts, or they might have to reject the parts that bore the brunt of the abuse so that the trauma can be blamed on "their" vulnerability. Alienation from self is often necessary, too, to maintain some semblance of attachment to grossly neglectful and abusive caretakers—an under-rated but important survival instinct when we are young enough to be dependent on our caretakers. If the "good child" is distasteful to attachment figures, it can be more adaptive to disown the "good me" or even the going on with normal life self and identify with the bad, ashamed, disgusting child who doesn't threaten dangerous or neglectful caretakers. Whichever parts endanger the child's adaptation must be walled off; whatever parts are necessitated by the environment must be identified with as "me."

与创伤保持距离在童年时期还发挥着另一个重要功能，是另一种生存方式：它不仅让我们继续前行，也让我们能够在遭受种种不幸的情况下继续成长和发展。通过与创伤保持距离或切断联系，儿童可以专注于掌握与年龄相适应的发展任务，并发展出一系列功能性能力。当孩子的一部分能够专注于"正常"活动——比如上学、体验新的学习与掌握感、参与运动或结交朋友——探索驱动力和社会驱动力就有了正常发展的机会。正常生活自我可能对正在发生的事情毫不知情，或仅有隐约察觉，他/她白天可能成为一个"亲职化的孩子"，在学校里是好学生，热爱自然、马匹、书籍或用双手创造事物。创伤或忽视越严重、安全感越匮乏，就越需要与对自身情感和身体脆弱性的认知保持距离。例如，在战争时期、施虐家庭或集中营中，否认正常的身体需求、寻求依恋的冲动或渴望被安慰的愿望，也可能具有适应性意义。当我们否认那些无法被满足的需求或无法被接受的感受时，我们保护自己免受难以承受的失望或惩罚（例如，"我倒要让你哭个够！"）。完成这一艰难任务的一种方式，是将绝望的需求感与拒绝任何需求的感受分裂成两个部分：一个部分积极寻求亲近、安慰或需求的满足，另一个部分则同样积极地将他人推开，或保持高度警惕和充满疑虑的距离。否认自己悲伤、孤独或有需求的各部分，以及愤怒、高度警惕或反依赖的各部分，会阻碍自我接纳和自我关怀，但这样更安全。当个体必须适应一个惩罚或忽视儿童基本需求与感受的环境时，自我慈悲也会变得"危险"。它不能是"我"。根据在每种独特环境中最有利于安全和最优发展的因素，儿童可能必须认同自己愤怒、攻击性强、高度警惕的各部分，并否认自己天真、信任、寻求依恋的各部分；或者，他们可能必须拒绝那些承受了虐待最主要冲击的各部分，以便将创伤归咎于"它们"的脆弱性。自我疏离往往也是必要的，以便对严重忽视和施虐的照料者维持某种依恋的表象——当我们年幼到足以依赖照料者时，这是一种被低估但十分重要的生存本能。如果"好孩子"令依恋对象感到厌恶，否认"好的我"甚至正常生活自我，并认同那个不会威胁到危险或忽视型照料者的坏的、羞耻的、令人厌恶的孩子，可能更具适应性。凡是危及儿童适应的各部分都必须被隔离；而环境所需的各部分则必须被认同为"我"。

The Cost of Adaptation

适应的代价

Disowning requires selective attention, a focusing away from whatever is "not me." The senses fail to register what is taking place around us; we don't feel our emotional responses, good or bad; we are in a zone. We can't "own" our anger or dependence or fear when we don't feel them. We can't "own" traumatic events that we haven't witnessed. We can't know ourselves as whole human beings because only those qualities valued in a traumatic environment are accessible to consciousness. Segregating intense feelings, though, results in affect intolerance: if we can escape our emotions by automatically and involuntarily shifting into a different part of the self or different feeling state, we never get the opportunity to exercise our "emotional muscles," and all feelings gradually become more and more intolerable. Inner conflicts are never resolved, just distanced.

否认需要选择性注意，即将注意力从一切"非我"的事物上移开。感官无法登记周围正在发生的事情；我们感受不到自己的情感反应，无论是好是坏；我们处于一种恍惚状态。当我们感受不到愤怒、依赖或恐惧时，就无法"认领"它们。我们无法"认领"自己未曾目睹的创伤事件。我们无法将自己认识为完整的人，因为只有那些在创伤环境中被重视的品质才能被意识所触及。然而，将强烈感受隔离的结果是情感不耐受：如果我们能通过自动、非自愿地转换到自我的另一个部分或不同的感受状态来逃避情绪，我们就永远没有机会锻炼自己的"情感肌肉"，所有感受都会逐渐变得越来越难以承受。内部冲突永远无法解决，只能被拉开距离。

When that happens, acting out (self-destructively or addictively) and "acting in" (through self-hatred, self-judgment, punitive introspection) become the only avenues for regulating emotions and autonomic arousal. Splitting or fragmentation must become more complex and creative. For example, some parts may become more autonomous, "emancipated" from cortical control, or so split off from other parts there is no intrapersonal awareness between them. Whereas continuous consciousness across states might be typical of complex PTSD, development of increasingly severe trauma-related disorders (borderline personality disorder, dissociative disorder not otherwise specified [DDNOS], and dissociative identity disorder [DID]) increasingly becomes a risk the more prolonged and severe the traumatic events. (See Chapter 8 for more on Treatment Challenges: Dissociative Systems and Disorders.)

当这种情况发生时，付诸行动（以自我毁灭或成瘾的方式）和"向内行动"（通过自我憎恨、自我评判、惩罚性自省）便成为调节情绪和自主神经唤醒的唯一途径。分裂或碎裂必须变得更加复杂和有创意。例如，一些部分可能变得更加自主，从皮层控制中"解放"出来，或与其他部分分裂得如此彻底，以至于彼此之间没有任何内部意识。持续的跨状态意识可能是复杂性创伤后应激障碍的典型特征，而随着创伤事件持续时间越长、越严重，发展出程度不断加重的创伤相关障碍（边缘性人格障碍、未特定解离障碍[DDNOS]以及解离性身份障碍[DID]）的风险也越来越大。（有关治疗挑战：解离系统与障碍的更多内容，请参阅第8章。）

The therapist's job of normalizing compartmentalization is made easier by the nature of the Structural Dissociation model: it is simple, straightforward, and positive in approach, yet its grounding in neurobiology gives it credibility even when clients are skeptical and intellectually hypervigilant. Using the diagram in Figure 4.1, I explain to clients that human brains are designed to be able to split if things get "too much" or "too overwhelming." Because right and left brains are separate brain structures, it makes intuitive sense to clients that, when they are exposed to trauma, the split between the two hemispheres enables the left brain aspect of self (what Cozolino [2002] calls "the verbal linguistic self") to "keep on keeping on," earning it the title of the "going on with normal life part," while the right brain mobilizes the "corporeal and emotional self" (Cozolino, 2002), with its more physical survival resources, to prepare for the next threat, thus its name, "the trauma-related part." It is also easy for most clients over the age of 12 to understand that no one could survive in just one way—for example, just by fighting. It is easy for them to comprehend that they have additional subparts as well, each of which contributes a different survival strategy. And then, to ensure that the clients can connect personally to the theoretical model, I ask them what they already know about each part. Do they recognize their going on with normal life selves? Do they recognize a part that knows how to ask for help? A part that knows how to fight or get angry? Do they recognize a frightened part? An ashamed or compliant part? Which parts are most difficult for the client to deal with? Which does the client like best?

结构性解离模型的特性使治疗师将分隔化正常化的工作变得更容易：这一模型方法简单、直接且积极，同时其根植于神经生物学的基础赋予了它可信度，即便来访者持怀疑态度且在智识上高度警惕也是如此。借助图4.1中的图示，我向来访者解释，人类大脑天生具备在事情"太多"或"太难以承受"时发生分裂的能力。由于左右脑是独立的脑结构，来访者凭直觉就能理解：当他们遭受创伤时，两个半球之间的分裂使自我的左脑方面（Cozolino [2002]称之为"言语语言自我"）能够"继续撑下去"，因此得名"正常生活部分"；而右脑则动员"身体与情感自我"（Cozolino，2002），凭借其更具身体性的生存资源，为下一次威胁做好准备，因此得名"创伤相关部分"。大多数12岁以上的来访者也很容易理解，没有人能只靠一种方式生存——例如，仅靠战斗。他们也很容易理解，自己还有额外的子部分，每个子部分都贡献着不同的生存策略。然后，为了确保来访者能够与理论模型建立个人连接，我会问他们对每个部分已经了解些什么。他们能认出自己的正常生活自我吗？能认出一个知道如何寻求帮助的部分吗？一个知道如何战斗或愤怒的部分？一个恐惧的部分？一个感到羞耻或顺从的部分？哪些部分对来访者来说最难处理？来访者最喜欢哪个部分？

With the help of the diagram, I try to demonstrate how each part sees the world through a different lens, is driven by a different animal defense imperative, and how it relates to others based not only on its history but on its biological role. Each holds different implicit memories and interpretations of what happened; each has a different job to do.

借助图示，我试图展示每个部分如何通过不同的视角来看待世界，如何受到不同动物防御驱动力的驱使，以及它如何根据不仅是自身历史、还有其生物学角色来与他人建立关系。每个部分持有不同的内隐记忆和对所发生事情的诠释；每个部分都有不同的任务。

Years after the traumatic experiences, clients come to treatment describing their symptoms or issues, not knowing that they are describing traumatized parts and their implicit memories. The animal defense survival strategies that once reduced the level of harm or enhanced their survival have now become split off automatic responses activated by trauma-related stimuli. Divorced from the original events that necessitated them, they are long outdated and often extreme or maladaptive in the client's normal life today (Figure 4.2).

在创伤经历发生数年之后，来访者前来治疗时描述着自己的症状或问题，却不知道他们描述的正是被创伤的各部分及其内隐记忆。那些曾经减轻伤害程度或增强生存能力的动物防御生存策略，如今已成为由创伤相关刺激激活的分裂性自动反应。脱离了最初使其成为必要的事件，这些策略早已过时，在来访者今日的正常生活中往往极端或具有适应不良性（图4.2）。

The paradoxical quality of these symptoms is rarely captured by traditional diagnostic models. Clients report symptoms of major depression (the submit part), anxiety disorders (freeze), substance abuse and eating disorders (flight), anger management or self-harm issues (fight), and they alternately cling to others or push them away (the characteristic symptoms of disorganized or traumatic attachment). Sometimes they come with co-occurring issues: suicidality, chronic pain, obsessive-compulsive disorder, inability to function, or loss of previous functioning. Often, they are diagnosed with borderline personality disorder or, because of their autonomic hypo- and hyperarousal responses, with bipolar II. Only occasionally, when memory gaps and losses of consciousness have been observed, do such clients receive dissociative disorder diagnoses. But whether traumatized individuals have been given one diagnosis (e.g., depression, borderline personality, anxiety disorders) or have been given many, standard treatments rarely have had a lasting impact, and many structurally dissociated clients have spent years in therapy with little to no change in their symptoms. Studies (Foote et al., 2006; Karadag et al., 2005) have consistently shown that the dissociative disorders (dissociative amnesia, dissociative fugue, dissociative disorder not otherwise specified [DDNOS], and DID) are consistently under-diagnosed in both inpatient and outpatient settings relative to the more widely known diagnoses of borderline personality, bipolar disorder, attention deficit disorder, and substance abuse disorders. These studies also indicate that there is a correlation between severity of symptoms and having an undiagnosed dissociative disorder. That is, when symptoms of dissociation are present but not diagnosed or included as an issue in the treatment, we can expect increased rates of high-risk behavior, more frequent relapses, and more instances of suicidal behavior, not just ideation (Karadag et al., 2005; Korzekwa & Dell, 2009). Perhaps because DID remains a controversial diagnosis even to this day, evidence of more common dissociative disorders (especially DDNOS) is often overlooked or the symptoms are interpreted as signs of other diagnoses. It is crucial that therapists know of the well-documented correlation between borderline personality and dissociative symptoms: a series of studies by Korzekwa et al. (2009a; 2009b) and by Zanarini (1998) have consistently found evidence that approximately two-thirds of borderline patients have statistically significant levels of dissociative symptoms, and one-third tend to have sufficient symptoms to justify a diagnosis of DID.

这些症状的悖论性质很少能被传统诊断模型所捕捉。来访者报告的症状包括重度抑郁（顺从部分）、焦虑障碍（冻结）、物质滥用和饮食障碍（逃跑）、愤怒管理或自伤问题（战斗），以及交替出现的依附他人或推开他人（解离性或创伤性依恋的典型症状）。有时他们还伴有共病问题：自杀意念、慢性疼痛、强迫症、功能丧失或既往功能的退化。他们常常被诊断为边缘性人格障碍，或者由于其自主神经系统的唤醒不足和过度唤醒反应而被诊断为双相II型障碍。只有在偶尔观察到记忆空白和意识丧失时，这类来访者才会收到解离障碍诊断。但无论被创伤的个体被给予一个诊断（如抑郁、边缘性人格、焦虑障碍）还是多个诊断，标准化治疗很少产生持久的影响，许多结构性解离的来访者在多年治疗中症状几乎没有改变。研究（Foote等，2006；Karadag等，2005）持续表明，与更广为人知的边缘性人格、双相障碍、注意力缺陷障碍和物质滥用障碍诊断相比，解离障碍（解离性遗忘、解离性漫游、未特定解离障碍[DDNOS]以及DID）在住院和门诊环境中均持续被漏诊。这些研究还表明，症状严重程度与存在未被诊断的解离障碍之间存在相关性。也就是说，当解离症状存在但未被诊断或未被纳入治疗议题时，我们可以预期高风险行为发生率的上升、复发频率的增加，以及自杀行为（而不仅仅是意念）实例的增多（Karadag等，2005；Korzekwa和Dell，2009）。或许正是因为解离性身份障碍（DID）至今仍是一个有争议的诊断，更为常见的解离障碍（尤其是DDNOS）的证据常常被忽视，或者症状被解读为其他诊断的迹象。治疗师了解边缘性人格与解离症状之间有据可查的相关性至关重要：Korzekwa等（2009a；2009b）和Zanarini（1998）的一系列研究持续发现，约三分之二的边缘性人格患者具有统计学意义上显著的解离症状，三分之一的患者往往有足够的症状支持DID诊断。

"Getting to Know You"

"了解你"

Aaron described the reasons for which he had come: "I start out by getting attached to women very quickly—I immediately think they're the 'one.' I'm all over them, can't see them enough … until they start to get serious or there's a commitment. Then I suddenly start to see everything I didn't see before, everything that's wrong with them. I start feeling trapped with someone who's not right for me—I want to leave, but I feel guilty—or afraid they'll leave me. I'm stuck. I can't relax and be happy, but I can't get out of it either."

Aaron描述了他前来求助的原因："我一开始就对女性迅速产生依恋——我立刻认为她们是'命中注定的那个人'。我对她们着迷，怎么都见不够……直到她们开始认真起来或出现承诺的要求。然后我突然开始看到之前没有看到的一切，她们所有的缺点。我开始感到被一个不适合我的人所困——我想离开，但我感到内疚——或者害怕她们会先离开我。我被卡住了。我无法放松和快乐，但我也无法从中脱身。"

Aaron was describing an internal struggle between parts: between an attachment-seeking part that quickly connected to any attractive woman who treated him warmly and a hypervigilant, hypercritical fight part that reacted to every less-than-optimal quality she possessed as a sign of trouble. His flight part, triggered by the alarms of the fight part, then would start to feel trapped with what felt like the "wrong person," generating impulses to get out—an action that his submit and cry for help parts couldn't allow. Guilt and shame for the commitment he'd promised (the submit part's contribution) and fear of loss (the input from his traumatically attached part) kept him in relationships that his fight and flight parts resisted with equal intensity. Without a language to differentiate each part and bring it to his awareness, he ruminated constantly: should he leave? Or should he stay? Was she enough? Or should he get out now? Often, suicide seemed to him the most logical solution to this painful dilemma, yet at the same time "he" dreamed of having a family with children and a loving and lovely wife. "He" didn't approve of his wandering eye, yet "he" couldn't stop trolling for prospective partners. Who was "he"? The suicidal part's threat to end it all was in direct conflict with his wish for a wife and family; the "trolling for women" part was at odds with the person he wanted to be and believed he should and could be.

Aaron描述的是各部分之间的内部斗争：一个寻求依恋的部分迅速与任何温暖对待他的有魅力女性产生连接，而一个高度警惕、极度挑剔的战斗部分则将她每一个不够完美的特质都视为麻烦的迹象。他的逃跑部分被战斗部分的警报触发，开始感到被一个感觉上是"错误的人"所困，产生了逃脱的冲动——而他的顺从部分和求助部分却不允许这种行动。对他所承诺的承诺感到的愧疚和羞耻（顺从部分的贡献），以及对失去的恐惧（来自他的创伤性依恋部分的输入），使他留在了那些战斗和逃跑部分以同等强度抵制的关系中。没有一种语言来区分每个部分并将其带入他的意识，他不断反刍：他应该离开吗？还是应该留下来？她够好吗？还是他现在就应该离开？自杀对他来说常常显得是解决这一痛苦困境最合乎逻辑的方案，然而与此同时，"他"梦想着拥有一个有孩子和一位温柔可爱妻子的家庭。"他"不赞同自己的游荡之心，然而"他"又无法停止寻觅潜在伴侣。"他"到底是谁？自杀部分威胁要结束一切，这与他对妻子和家庭的渴望直接冲突；"觅女"部分与他想要成为、并相信自己应当且能够成为的那种人背道而驰。

Nelly presented herself as "depressed" but when asked to describe her symptoms, instead she recounted a series of beliefs about herself: "I'm disorganized, lazy—I can't seem to get off the ground—I'm ashamed to think of myself as a competent person, though people tell me I am." Her first thought each morning was "another day for me to make a waste of," and then she would feel overwhelmed and pull the covers over her head and sleep until early afternoon. Appointments were forgotten; dishes didn't get washed; there was nothing to eat in the house—confirming her belief that she was a failure and generating a series of harsh judgments that drained her energy and generated the impulse to go back to bed. Now in her fifties, she could recall what it was like to be the underachieving child in an achievement-oriented family, "hiding in plain sight" to avoid angering her abusive narcissistic father who was threatened by his children's accomplishments and wounded by their failures. Her funny, charming personality had won him over, and seeing her as a bright, ditzy, disorganized kid was acceptable to him—at least marginally acceptable. She had promise, even if she didn't have achievements.

Nelly自述"抑郁"，但当被要求描述自己的症状时，她却讲述了一系列关于自己的信念："我杂乱无章，懒散——我好像怎么都无法起步——我为把自己视为一个有能力的人而感到羞耻，尽管别人都这么告诉我。"她每天早晨的第一个念头是"又是一天让我浪费掉"，然后她便感到不知所措，把被子蒙在头上，一直睡到下午早些时候。约会被遗忘；碗碟未洗；家里没有任何可吃的东西——这一切印证了她认为自己是个失败者的信念，并产生了一系列苛刻的评判，耗尽她的能量，并产生了回去睡觉的冲动。如今已年过五十，她能够回想起在一个以成就为导向的家庭中做一个成绩不佳的孩子是什么感觉，她"在众目睽睽之下躲藏"，以避免激怒她那位具有施虐性的自恋父亲——他因孩子们的成就而感到威胁，又因他们的失败而受伤。她有趣、迷人的个性赢得了他的欢心，而把她视为一个聪明、迷糊、杂乱无章的孩子对他来说是可以接受的——至少是勉强可以接受的。她有潜力，即便她没有成就。

Now, all these years later, Nelly found herself confused about who "she" was. For so long, she had been "hijacked" (Ogden & Fisher, 2015) by the underachieving, self-denigrating, quirky child part that had once helped protect her from her father's wrath and win his heart. The dominance of this submissive part's role in Nelly's life was supported by that of an equally familiar critical part whose words and worldview were eerily like her father's. Without a parts model on which to draw, it was easy for her therapist of many years to mistake Nelly's self-loathing for low self-esteem and to interpret her wry sense of humor as "a defense against core emotion."

如今，这么多年过去了，Nelly发现自己对"她"是谁感到困惑。如此之久，她一直被那个成绩不佳、自我贬低、古怪的孩子部分所"劫持"（Ogden和Fisher，2015），而这个部分曾经帮助她免遭父亲的愤怒并赢得他的心。这个顺从部分在Nelly生活中的主导地位，得到了一个同样熟悉的批评部分的支撑，后者的言辞和世界观与她父亲的惊人相似。没有可供借鉴的各部分模型，多年来的治疗师很容易将Nelly的自我厌恶误认为低自尊，并将她的苦涩幽默感解读为"对核心情绪的防御"。

But Nelly felt best when her going on with normal life self broke free, and she could be focused professionally, witty, charming, and self-disparaging in a humorous yet self-compassionate way. Sadly, the critical part succeeded in convincing her that these abilities were just those of a false self whose role it was to hide what a waste of human life she had ended up becoming. Even when she felt good, the critical part found a way to communicate judgment! She needed a "no fault" paradigm for understanding herself and for seeing each part's contribution as a communication from the past. Inside, the submit part was afraid to let Nelly actualize as the talented professional she was for fear she would "get a swelled head;" the critical part hypervigilantly worried about failure; and the freeze part was afraid to leave the house for fear that all human beings would be scary like her father.

但当Nelly的正常生活自我挣脱出来时，她感觉最好，她可以在职业上专注、机智、迷人，并以一种幽默而充满自我慈悲的方式进行自嘲。令人悲伤的是，批评部分成功地说服她，这些能力不过是假自我的能力，其作用是掩盖她最终成为怎样一个人类生命的浪费。即便在她感觉良好时，批评部分也找到了传递评判的方式！她需要一个"无过错"的范式来理解自己，将每个部分的贡献视为来自过去的信息。在内心深处，顺从部分害怕让Nelly作为有才华的专业人士自我实现，担心她会"变得自大"；批评部分对失败高度警惕；而冻结部分则害怕离开家，担心所有人类都会像她父亲那样令人恐惧。

And because all this was happening in one body, Nelly made the logical assumption that these were all "her" feelings—never questioning the paradoxical reactions that revealed evidence of an internal war among parts.

因为这一切都发生在同一个身体里，Nelly做出了符合逻辑的假设，认为这些都是"她的"感受——从未质疑那些揭示各部分之间内部战争证据的矛盾反应。

Developing Curiosity: Who Is "I"?

培养好奇心："我"是谁？

In most psychotherapy models, no differentiation is made between the "I" that feels shame or the "I" that explodes in anger or the "I" that is always afraid. Each emotion is treated as an expression of the individual's whole self. In a parts model, however, each distressing or uncomfortable thought, feeling, or body sensation is treated as a part (Schwartz, 1995). By deliberately and consistently using the language of parts rather than the language of "I," the therapist helps the client to observe each trauma-related feeling or reaction as a message from a part or parts: "Which 'I' feels ashamed and apologetic? And which 'I' is disgusted by all the apologizing?" When we ask these questions, we evoke curiosity and facilitate mindful observing. There is now a very slight distance between the observer and what is being observed. The client can still feel the feeling or reaction but with a decrease in intensity, presumably due to increased activity in the medial prefrontal cortex and reduced amygdala activation. The word "part" introduces new information, generating interest and often curiosity.

在大多数心理治疗模型中，感到羞耻的"我"、爆发愤怒的"我"或总是恐惧的"我"之间没有区分。每种情绪都被视为个体整体自我的表达。然而，在各部分模型中，每一个令人痛苦或不舒适的想法、感受或身体感觉都被视为一个部分（Schwartz，1995）。通过刻意且一贯地使用各部分的语言而非"我"的语言，治疗师帮助来访者将每一个创伤相关的感受或反应观察为来自一个或多个部分的信息："哪个'我'感到羞耻和歉意？哪个'我'对所有的道歉感到厌恶？"当我们提出这些问题时，我们唤起了好奇心，促进了正念观察。此时，观察者与被观察的对象之间有了极小的距离。来访者仍然能够感受到这种感受或反应，但强度有所降低，这可能是由于前额叶内侧皮层活动增加和杏仁核激活减少所致。"部分"这个词引入了新的信息，产生了兴趣，通常还会产生好奇心。

Aaron and Nelly had long lost any curiosity they might once have had about their symptoms: they accepted the input of angry, suicidal, demoralized, lonely, self-critical, and self-loathing parts as "my feelings," ignoring the fact that these were contradictory emotional states. Wanting to attach to someone is in direct conflict with wanting to flee; wanting to feel mastery, competence, and energy is in direct conflict with "flying below the radar" or appearing "less than" to avoid threatening others. But both were so identified with the parts' struggles and conflicts that these paradoxical responses escaped their notice. The first task in therapy was to challenge their assumptions and ignite their curiosity in two ways: first, by using the "language of parts" (Schwartz, 2001), rather than the language of "I," and, secondly, by asking them to use mindful observation, instead of their automatic negative interpretations, to "track" (Ogden et al., 2006) the moment-to-moment signs of their parts' thoughts, feelings, visceral reactions, and movement impulses as they responded to triggers around them with competing survival responses.

Aaron和Nelly早已失去了他们曾经可能对自己症状怀有的好奇心：他们将来自愤怒、自杀、士气低落、孤独、自我批评和自我厌恶部分的输入接受为"我的感受"，忽视了这些是相互矛盾的情感状态这一事实。渴望依附于某人与渴望逃离直接冲突；渴望感到掌控感、能力和活力与"低调行事"或显得"不如别人"以避免威胁他人直接冲突。但两人都与各部分的挣扎和冲突高度认同，以至于这些矛盾反应逃过了他们的注意。治疗中的第一个任务是以两种方式挑战他们的假设并点燃他们的好奇心：首先，使用"各部分的语言"（Schwartz，2001）而非"我"的语言；其次，要求他们使用正念观察而非自动的负面诠释，来"追踪"（Ogden等，2006）各部分的想法、感受、内脏反应和运动冲动的逐时迹象，因为他们以相互竞争的生存反应来应对周围的触发因素。

Mindful Noticing of the Inner Landscape

对内在景观的正念觉察

To the extent that clients continue to ruminate on their trauma-related emotions and cognitive schemas, the more often they are triggered by environmental stimuli, the more dysregulated their nervous systems will become and the more activated the parts. The more the prefrontal cortex is inhibited or shut down by trauma-related dysregulation, the harder it becomes for the normal life self to stay curious and present. Without an observing prefrontal cortex to differentiate past and present, repeated activation of neural networks holding traumatic memory further sensitize these pathways to future triggering, exacerbating trauma-related symptoms (Van der Kolk, 2014). Learning to observe rather than react and to attach neutral labels to what is noticed (e.g., scared child part, angry part, etc.) is the foundation of a parts approach.

来访者继续反刍其创伤相关情绪和认知图式的程度越深，他们被环境刺激触发的频率越高，他们的神经系统就越失调，各部分就越活跃。前额叶皮层被创伤相关失调抑制或关闭的程度越深，正常生活自我就越难保持好奇心和当下状态。没有观察性前额叶皮层来区分过去与现在，持有创伤记忆的神经网络的反复激活会进一步使这些通路对未来触发变得敏感，加重创伤相关症状（Van der Kolk，2014）。学会观察而非反应，并为所注意到的事物附加中性标签（如受惊的孩子部分、愤怒部分等）是各部分方法的基础。

With the help of a therapist who persistently reframes problematic emotions and issues as communications from parts, clients learn to identify the key features that indicate signs of a part's presence. They are taught to observe distressing or uncomfortable physical sensations, overwhelming or painful emotions, negative or self-punitive beliefs, internal struggles, procrastination, and ambivalence. Automatic reactions, the same thoughts repeatedly coming to mind, repetitive responses to triggers, negative reactions to positive events or stimuli, or "over-reactions" should also be flagged as likely signs of parts activity. The practice of being asked repeatedly to be curious and notice all of these phenomena as possible signs of parts activity has a number of benefits. Mindful observation evokes activity in the prefrontal cortex, counteracting trauma-related cortical inhibition and inducing a very slight sense of separation from the feeling, thought, or part. For the first time, clients might notice that they can have a relationship to the feeling rather than be consumed by it or over-identified with it. Another benefit of mindful awareness is its effect on autonomic dysregulation: activation of the medial prefrontal cortex (the part of the brain that engages when we meditate) is associated with decreased activation in the amygdala (the brain structure associated with initiation of the emergency stress response). In addition, when individuals become curious and interested and focused on what they are observing, they intuitively slow their pace and increase concentration to increase observational capacity.

在治疗师持续将有问题的情绪和议题重构为来自各部分的信息的帮助下，来访者学会识别表明某个部分在场的关键特征。他们被教导去观察令人痛苦或不舒适的躯体感觉、压倒性或痛苦的情绪、消极或自我惩罚性的信念、内部斗争、拖延和矛盾心理。自动反应、反复浮现的相同想法、对触发因素的重复反应、对积极事件或刺激的消极反应，或"过度反应"，也都应被标记为各部分活动可能的迹象。被反复要求以好奇心注意所有这些现象作为各部分活动可能迹象的做法有许多益处。正念观察在前额叶皮层中唤起活动，抵消创伤相关的皮层抑制，并引发与感受、想法或部分之间极微小的分离感。来访者可能第一次注意到，他们可以与感受建立关系，而不是被它吞噬或过度认同它。正念觉察的另一个益处是其对自主神经失调的影响：前额叶内侧皮层（我们冥想时参与的大脑部分）的激活与杏仁核（与启动紧急应激反应相关的大脑结构）活动的减少相关。此外，当个体变得好奇、感兴趣并专注于所观察的事物时，他们会本能地放慢节奏，增加专注度，以提高观察能力。

When clients have greater access to their going on with normal life selves (or what is called "self" (Schwartz, 2001) in the Internal Family Systems model), they benefit significantly from the advantages made possible by increased activity in the prefrontal cortex: they can use mindful noticing to separate from the intense reactions of a part; they can have a relationship of curiosity or compassion toward the part's feelings or perspective; they can create ways of soothing or managing the emotions; and they can choose to react differently to foreseeable events or triggers than they have in the past. In contrast, parts under the influence of implicit memory will have the same reactions over and over again: separation anxiety, irritability or anger, shame and hopelessness, fear, self-destructive impulses, etc. Even if available to the client, coping skills and problem solving are less likely to work when parts are triggered because the "problem" to be solved is most often an implicit memory, not a current stress or challenge. And when "the problem" is the result of competing states engaging in a struggle for control, adult coping ability has little to no effect on it.

当来访者能够更多地接触其正常生活自我（或内部家庭系统（IFS）模型中所称的"自我"（Schwartz，2001））时，他们能够从前额叶皮层活动增加所带来的优势中显著受益：他们可以使用正念觉察从某个部分的强烈反应中分离出来；他们可以对该部分的感受或视角持有好奇或慈悲的关系；他们可以创造安抚或管理情绪的方式；他们可以选择以不同于过去的方式对可预见的事件或触发因素做出反应。相比之下，受内隐记忆影响的各部分将一遍又一遍地产生相同的反应：分离焦虑、易怒或愤怒、羞耻和绝望、恐惧、自我毁灭冲动等。即便来访者有应对技能和解决问题的能力，当各部分被触发时，这些方法也不太可能发挥作用，因为需要解决的"问题"通常是内隐记忆，而非当前的压力或挑战。当"问题"是相互竞争的状态为争夺控制权而进行斗争的结果时，成人的应对能力对其几乎没有影响。

Survival-Related Internal Struggles

与生存相关的内部斗争

Certain internal struggles between parts are inevitable and predictable. The cry for help or attach for survival response will automatically evoke flight-related distancing impulses—or fight-related protective reactions such as mistrust, hypervigilance, anger, or judgment. Critical thoughts expressed by the fight part, often experienced by clients as "self-loathing," are likely to trigger the submit part's feelings of shame, hopelessness, and inadequacy. Interpersonal closeness can trigger either the attach part's yearning for more proximity, the freeze part's fear of being harmed, and/or the warning alarms of the fight and flight parts—or all of these reactions simultaneously. Professional or family responsibilities can feel like a repetition of bearing old burdens to the submit part, even when the work is self-initiated by a competent normal life self who enjoys completing chores that once felt overwhelming to a small boy or girl. Sometimes, it is the steps forward in life taken by the normal life part that most alarm the trauma-related parts and even trigger their conflicts and crises. "Being seen" positively (e.g., being given a compliment, praised for something, or evoking attention for one's accomplishments), successful performance, and achievement awards can all evoke the fear of visibility in the freeze part and the expectation of being used or abused in the fight part. We often forget that some kind of special attention or "grooming" often precedes sexual or even physical abuse, making traumatized clients hypervigilently sensitive to being treated kindly as well as to being ill-treated.

各部分之间某些内部斗争是不可避免且可以预见的。求助或为生存而依恋的反应会自动唤起与逃跑相关的疏远冲动——或与战斗相关的保护性反应，如不信任、高度警惕、愤怒或评判。战斗部分表达的批评性想法，通常被来访者体验为"自我厌恶"，很可能触发顺从部分的羞耻感、绝望感和不胜任感。人际亲密可以触发依恋部分对更多亲近的渴望、冻结部分对受伤的恐惧，和/或战斗和逃跑部分的警报——或所有这些反应同时发生。职业或家庭责任对于顺从部分来说可能感觉像是重复承担旧有负担，即便这项工作是由一个胜任的正常生活自我主动发起的，而正常生活自我享受完成那些曾经让小男孩或小女孩感到不知所措的事情。有时，正是正常生活部分在生活中迈出的前进步伐，最让创伤相关部分感到警惕，甚至触发他们的冲突和危机。被积极地"看见"（例如，受到赞美、因某事被表扬，或因成就引起关注）、成功的表现以及成就奖励，都可能在冻结部分中唤起对被人注目的恐惧，以及在战斗部分中唤起被利用或被虐待的预期。我们常常忘记，某种特殊的关注或"诱导"往往先于性虐待甚至身体虐待而发生，这使得被创伤的来访者对受到善待和受到虐待都极度敏感。

As Donald Meichenbaum (2012) reminds us, trauma is an experience beyond words, and the beliefs or stories that individuals attach to a traumatic event bias their meaning-making, leading to what he calls the creation of "self-defeating stories." Which parts might write a self-defeating story? The submit part would be likely to write a shame-based, hopeless story of victimization; the cry for help part a story of how no one came or cared; the fight part would communicate that it's better to die than to continue to be used and abused. The going on with normal life self has access to the wider perspective of the prefrontal cortex and can learn to understand that the belief one is "faking it" might be adaptive in a traumatic environment, while continuing to believe it later in life would be maladaptive. When beliefs are consistently differentiated from emotions, visceral reactions, tension versus relaxation, and actions, when all of these inputs are connected to the parts contributing them, clients begin to get a clearer sense of who they are as a whole and why they have struggled so hard.

正如Donald Meichenbaum（2012）提醒我们的，创伤是一种超越语言的体验，个体附加在创伤事件上的信念或故事会使其意义建构产生偏差，导致他所称的"自我挫败故事"的形成。哪些部分可能会写出自我挫败的故事？顺从部分可能会写出一个基于羞耻的、绝望的受害故事；求助部分会写出一个没有人来或关心的故事；战斗部分会传达宁死也不愿继续被利用和虐待的信息。正常生活自我能够接触到前额叶皮层更宽广的视角，可以学会理解，相信自己是在"装腔作势"的信念在创伤环境中可能具有适应性，而在之后的生活中继续持有这种信念则将是适应不良的。当信念被持续地与情绪、内脏反应、紧张与放松以及行动区分开来，当所有这些输入都与贡献它们的部分联系起来时，来访者开始对自己作为一个整体是谁，以及为何如此艰难地挣扎有了更清晰的认识。

Danny was an overachiever professionally, driven by a judgmental critic and an anxious part that feared failure. Whatever he achieved, it wasn't enough for the critic and didn't reassure the fearful part. A new boss who was never satisfied with his work but who took credit for Danny's accomplishments provided the trigger that opened the floodgates of implicit memory. Suddenly, his fight part was confronting and challenging the boss, the submit part was feeling so ashamed and victimized that it was hard to go to work, and Danny was experiencing a painful sense of not being wanted or valued anywhere, the emotional memories of an attach part. Each held a piece of his survival: the fight part's indignation at the misuse of power by both parents, the submit part's self-defeating, self-blaming story, and the heartbreaking emotions of a young boy who felt an unmet yearning to be special to someone. As Danny "unblended" from these parts and used his meditation skills to observe them as younger selves, he felt an immediate compassion for the young boy who felt so hurt. "That's why I've been pushing myself to achieve—to win praise so that this boy feels special to someone!" Instinctively, he felt protective of the young boy: he could feel a determination in his body: this boy should not be hurt again, he thought. He would have to make this little boy feel special—he would have to communicate the acceptance his parents had withheld.

Danny在职业上是一个过度追求成就的人，被一个评判性的批评者和一个害怕失败的焦虑部分所驱使。无论他取得了什么成就，对批评者来说都不够，也无法安抚那个恐惧的部分。一位对他的工作永不满意、却窃取Danny成果的新上司，提供了打开内隐记忆闸门的触发因素。突然间，他的战斗部分在对抗和挑战上司，顺从部分感到如此羞耻和受害，以至于很难去上班，而Danny则体验到一种痛苦的无处被需要或被珍视的感觉——这是依恋部分的情感记忆。每个部分都持有他生存的一个片段：战斗部分对父母双方滥用权力的义愤，顺从部分的自我挫败、自我责备的故事，以及一个年幼男孩令人心碎的情感——他感受到一种未被满足的渴望，渴望对某人来说是特别的。当Danny从这些部分中"解融合"并运用他的冥想技能将它们作为更年幼的自我来观察时，他立刻对那个感受到如此伤痛的小男孩产生了慈悲。"这就是我一直逼迫自己取得成就的原因——为了赢得赞美，让这个男孩感到对某人来说是特别的！"本能地，他感到要保护这个小男孩：他能在身体里感受到一种决心：这个男孩不应再受到伤害，他想。他将不得不让这个小男孩感到特别——他将不得不传达他父母所拒绝给予的接纳。

Selves-Acceptance

自我们的接纳

"Befriending" one's parts is not simply a therapeutic endeavor: it also contributes to developing the practice of self-acceptance, one part at a time. When clients pause their reactions to "befriend" themselves, to be curious and interested rather than dismissing and reactive, they slow time. Autonomic arousal settles; there is a relaxing of the sense of urgency to do or be anything different. They feel more at peace because their parts can be more at peace. Self-alienation, that is, disowning of some parts and identifying with others exclusively, does not contribute to a sense of well-being, even when it is absolutely necessary in order to survive. Self-alienation creates tension, pits part against part, communicates a hostile environment (often much like the traumatic environment), and diminishes the self-esteem of every part. Befriending means that we "radically accept" (Linehan, 1993) that we share our bodies and lives with these "room-mates" and that living well with ourselves requires living amicably and collaboratively with our parts. The more we welcome rather than reject them, the safer our internal worlds.

与自己的各部分"为友"不仅仅是一种治疗性努力：它也有助于发展自我接纳的实践，每次一个部分。当来访者暂停自己的反应来与自己"为友"，以好奇和感兴趣的态度而非回避和反应性的态度时，时间慢了下来。自主神经唤醒趋于平静；对做任何不同的事或成为任何不同的人的紧迫感得以放松。他们感到更加平和，因为他们的各部分也可以更加平和。自我疏离，即否认某些部分而专门认同其他部分，即便在为了生存而绝对必要时，也无助于产生幸福感。自我疏离制造紧张，使部分对抗部分，传递一种充满敌意的环境（往往很像创伤性环境），并降低每个部分的自尊。与各部分为友意味着我们"彻底接受"（Linehan，1993）我们与这些"室友"共享我们的身体和生命，与自己好好生活需要与我们的各部分和睦、协作地共处。我们越欢迎而非拒绝它们，我们的内部世界就越安全。
