CHAPTER 5
Befriending Our Parts: Sowing the Seeds of Compassion

第5章 与各部分为友：播下慈悲的种子

“Mindfulness is an act of hospitality. A way of learning to treat ourselves with kindness and care that slowly begins to percolate into the deepest recesses of our being while gradually offering us the possibility of relating to others in the same manner. … [The] process simply asks us to entertain the possibility of offering hospitality to ourselves no matter what we are feeling or thinking. This has nothing to do with denial or self-justification for unkind or undesirable actions, but it has everything to do with self-compassion when facing the rough, shadowy, difficult, or uncooked aspects of our lives.”

“正念是一种款待之举。它是一种学会以善意和关怀对待自己的方式，这种善意与关怀会慢慢渗透进我们存在最深处的角落，同时逐渐赋予我们以同样的方式与他人相处的可能性。……这一过程只是要求我们去设想这样一种可能性：无论我们正在感受或思考什么，都向自己提供款待。这与否认无关，也与为不友善或不当行为进行自我辩解无关，而是完全关乎在面对我们生命中粗糙、阴暗、艰难或尚未成熟的那些方面时，对自己怀有慈悲。”

(Santorelli, 2014, p. 1)

Reclaiming Our Lost Selves

重新认领我们失落的自我

While yearning to “like” ourselves, the disowning of traumatic experiences or the vulnerable, ashamed, angry, or depressed parts holding implicit memories of those experiences results in a profound alienation from self: “I don’t know myself, but one thing is clear: I don’t like myself.” The ability to be compassionate or comforting or curious with others, which comes so easily to many trauma survivors, is not matched by the ability to offer themselves the same kindness. What it took to survive has created a bind. It was adaptive “then” to avoid comfort or self-compassion, to shame and self-judge before attachment figures could find them lacking, but now it has come to feel believable that others deserve or belong or are worth more—while, at the same time, it also feels that these “others” are not to be trusted; they are dangerous or uncaring.

在渴望“喜欢”自己的同时，否认创伤经历，或否认那些承载着这些经历内隐记忆的脆弱、羞耻、愤怒或抑郁的各部分，会导致一种深刻的自我疏离：“我不了解自己，但有一点是清楚的：我不喜欢自己。”许多创伤幸存者轻而易举就能对他人怀有慈悲、给予安慰或保持好奇，但他们却无法以同样的善意对待自己。当初为了生存所付出的代价造成了一种困局。在“那时”，回避安慰或自我慈悲，在依恋对象发现自己有所欠缺之前就先行羞辱和评判自己，是具有适应性的；但如今，他们越来越相信他人配得上、属于这里或更有价值——而与此同时，又觉得这些“他人”不可信任；他们是危险的或冷漠的。

It is a well-accepted premise that, to feel safe in any relationship, human beings need compassion both for themselves and for the other. Internal attachment bonds or “earned secure attachment” (Siegel, 1999) give us emotional resilience. The internalization of secure attachment allows individuals to tolerate hurt, loneliness, anxiety, disappointment, frustration, and rejection—all the risks inherent in any close relationship. But in order to unconditionally accept ourselves and “earn” that resilience, we need to develop a relationship to all of us: to our wounded and needy parts, to the parts hostile to vulnerability, to the parts that survived by distancing and denial—to the parts we love, the parts we hate, and even the parts that intimidate us.

有一个被广泛接受的前提：要在任何关系中感到安全，人类既需要对自己怀有慈悲，也需要对他人怀有慈悲。内部依恋纽带或“习得的安全依恋”（Siegel，1999）赋予我们情感韧性。安全依恋的内化使个体能够容忍伤害、孤独、焦虑、失望、挫折和拒绝——这些都是任何亲密关系中固有的风险。但是，为了无条件地接纳自己并“赢得”那种韧性，我们需要与全部的自我建立关系：与我们受伤、有需求的各部分，与那些敌视脆弱的各部分，与那些靠疏远和否认而存活下来的各部分——与我们所爱的各部分、我们所恨的各部分，乃至那些令我们畏惧的各部分。

Embedded in most methods of psychotherapy is the belief that “healing” is the outcome of a relational process: that if we are wounded in an unsafe relationship, the wounds must heal in a context of relational safety. But what if the quality of our internal attachment bonds, rather than our interpersonal attachments, is a more powerful determinant of our ability to feel safe? What if attachment to ourselves is a bigger contributor to the sense of well-being than the attachment we feel to and from others? What if being witnessed as we recall painful events does not heal the injuries caused by those experiences? And what if compassion for the child who lived through these events is more important than knowing the details of what happened? If that is so, and I believe it is, then trauma treatment must focus less on painful and traumatic events and more on cultivating compassion for our disowned selves and their painful experiences. When all parts of us feel internally connected and held lovingly inside, each can experience feeling safe, welcome, and worthy, often for the first time. The first step is to become curious about this “other” inside whom we do not really know.

大多数心理治疗方法都内含这样一种信念：“疗愈”是关系过程的产物——如果我们是在一段不安全的关系中受伤的，那么这些伤口必须在关系安全的情境中愈合。但是，如果决定我们能否感到安全的更强大因素，是我们内部依恋纽带的质量，而非我们的人际依恋呢？如果对自己的依恋比我们对他人、来自他人的依恋更能促成幸福感呢？如果在我们回忆痛苦事件时被见证，并不能疗愈那些经历所造成的创伤呢？又如果对那个经历了这些事件的孩子怀有慈悲，比知道所发生之事的细节更为重要呢？如果情况如此——我相信确实如此——那么创伤治疗就必须减少对痛苦和创伤事件的关注，转而更多地培养对我们被否认的自我及其痛苦经历的慈悲。当我们所有的各部分都感到内在彼此相连、被充满爱意地容纳于内时，每一个部分都能体验到安全、被欢迎和有价值，而这往往是第一次。第一步是对内心这个我们并不真正了解的“他者”产生好奇。

The Role of Mindfulness: How We Can “Befriend” Ourselves

正念的作用：我们如何能与自己“为友”

To observe and identify the signs indicating parts activity requires a witnessing mind capable of focused concentration or “directed mindfulness” (Ogden & Fisher, 2015). Mindfulness has an important role to play in the treatment of trauma because of its effects on the brain and body. Mindfulness practices counteract trauma-related cortical inhibition, regulate autonomic activation, and allow us to have a relationship of interest and curiosity toward our feelings, thoughts, and body responses—or parts. In brain scan studies, mindful concentration has been associated with increased activity in the medial prefrontal cortex and decreased activity in the amygdala (Creswell et al., 2007).

要观察并识别表明各部分活动的迹象，需要一颗能够聚焦专注或进行“定向正念”（Ogden和Fisher，2015）的见证之心。由于正念对大脑和身体的作用，它在创伤治疗中扮演着重要的角色。正念练习抵消创伤相关的皮层抑制，调节自主神经的激活，并使我们能够以兴趣和好奇的态度与自己的感受、想法和身体反应——也就是各部分——建立关系。在脑部扫描研究中，正念专注与前额叶内侧皮层活动的增加和杏仁核活动的减少相关（Creswell等，2007）。

Mindfulness is key to trauma work not only because of its regulating effect on the nervous system but because it also facilitates the capacity for “dual awareness” or “parallel processing,” allowing us to explore the past without risk of retraumatization by keeping one “foot” in the present and one “foot” in the past (Ogden et al., 2006). “Dual awareness” is a habit of mind or mental ability that allows us to simultaneously hold in mind more than one state of consciousness. When the client can stay present in a mindfully aware relationship to both present moment experience and an implicit or explicit memory connected to the past, he or she is in dual awareness. When individuals can connect to a felt sense of the child self’s painful emotion while simultaneously feeling the length and stability of the spine, the in and out of the breath, the beating of the heart, and the ground under their feet, intense emotions can be held and tolerated. Sensorimotor Psychotherapy, Internal Family Systems, and hypnotic ego state therapy (Phillips & Frederick, 1995) are all mindfulness-based methods, as are the other popular treatments for trauma most frequently sought out by clients—Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR) (Shapiro, 2001) and Somatic Experiencing (Levine, 2015).

正念之所以是创伤工作的关键，不仅因为它对神经系统的调节作用，还因为它能够促进“双重觉察”或“平行加工”的能力，让我们能够一只“脚”踏在当下、一只“脚”踏在过去，从而在没有再创伤风险的情况下探索过去（Ogden等，2006）。“双重觉察”是一种心智习惯或心理能力，它使我们能够同时在心中持有不止一种意识状态。当来访者能够以正念觉察的方式，同时与当下时刻的体验以及与过去相连的内隐或外显记忆保持当下的关系时，他或她便处于双重觉察之中。当个体能够连接到孩子自我痛苦情绪的感受体验，同时又感受到脊柱的长度与稳定、呼吸的进出、心脏的跳动以及脚下的大地时，强烈的情绪便能够被容纳和承受。感觉运动心理治疗、内部家庭系统以及催眠自我状态治疗（Phillips和Frederick，1995）都是以正念为基础的方法，来访者最常寻求的其他流行创伤疗法——眼动脱敏与再加工（EMDR）（Shapiro，2001）和身体经验创伤疗法（Levine，2015）——亦是如此。

From Whose Perspective Should We Observe?

我们应当从谁的视角去观察？

Observations of the environment through the telescopic lenses specific to each part often create a distorted perspective for the client. Each part has particular biases that narrow what it picks up as data and what it never sees. The fight part is not scanning for safety cues: it is hypervigilantly oriented toward threat stimuli. Attach sees only the warm smile, the reassuring words, the polite manners—and never sees the danger signals that indicate grooming or seduction. The submit part doesn’t see the respect of his colleagues, the approval of his boss or family members, but he is likely to be hypersensitive to data that confirm beliefs in unworthiness or not belonging. When clients learn to identify the lens through which they are looking (“the little part is hoping to see someone who likes her,” “the depressed part is looking at Susan’s expression and thinking the worst”), they can begin to see the actions and reactions of their parts from a meta-awareness perspective. Rather than being flooded with overwhelming emotions, they learn to separate from the intense reactions of a part, acknowledge the feelings as “his” or “her distress,” and bear witness to the child part’s painful experience. Perhaps for the first time, clients can have a relationship to a distressing feeling rather than being consumed by it or identifying with it as “mine.” The feeling or reaction is still palpable but with a decrease in intensity that is consistent with the ability to sustain curiosity and interest in it, rather than react. Mindful “interest,” rather than “attachment or aversion,” helps individuals tolerate emotions and sensations that may have previously felt frightening, and it supports a neutral stance toward whatever is observed or discovered. In addition, when individuals become curious and interested and focused on what they are observing, they intuitively slow their pace to increase concentration and observational capacity. Being “interested” is the first step toward getting to know another being, even when that other is a part of one’s self. From this new perspective, it is easier for most individuals to find ways of soothing the parts’ emotions and anticipating events or triggers that might overwhelm a child without support from someone older and wiser.

透过每个部分特有的望远镜镜片去观察环境，往往会为来访者制造出一种扭曲的视角。每个部分都有特定的偏见，它们会缩窄它所采集为“数据”的内容，以及它永远看不见的内容。战斗部分并不在扫描安全线索：它高度警觉地朝向威胁刺激。依恋部分只看见温暖的微笑、令人安心的话语、礼貌的举止——而永远看不见那些表明诱骗或勾引的危险信号。顺从部分看不见同事的尊重、上司或家人的认可，但他很可能对那些印证“自己不配”或“不属于这里”的信念的数据极度敏感。当来访者学会识别他们正透过怎样的镜片在看（“那个小部分希望看到一个喜欢她的人”，“那个抑郁部分正盯着Susan的表情往最坏处想”）时，他们便能够开始从元觉察的视角来看待自己各部分的行动与反应。他们不再被压倒性的情绪所淹没，而是学会从某个部分的强烈反应中分离出来，承认那些感受是“他的”或“她的痛苦”，并见证孩子部分的痛苦经历。也许是第一次，来访者能够与一种令人痛苦的感受建立关系，而不是被它吞噬或将其认同为“我的”。这种感受或反应依然清晰可感，但强度有所降低，这与对它保持好奇和兴趣、而非作出反应的能力是相一致的。正念的“兴趣”，而非“依附或厌恶”，帮助个体容忍那些以往可能令人恐惧的情绪和感觉，并支持对所观察或所发现的一切持中立立场。此外，当个体变得好奇、感兴趣并专注于所观察的事物时，他们会本能地放慢节奏，以提升专注度和观察能力。“感兴趣”是了解另一个存在的第一步，即便那个“他者”是自身的一部分。从这一新的视角出发，对大多数个体而言，更容易找到安抚各部分情绪的方法，并预见那些可能在没有更年长、更智慧之人支持的情况下淹没一个孩子的事件或触发因素。

In an Internal Family Systems approach (Schwartz, 1995; 2001), the observer role is ascribed to “self,” an internal state that draws upon eight “C” qualities: curiosity, compassion, calm, clarity, creativity, courage, confidence, and connectedness. “Self” is not just a meditative state or dependent upon having positive experiences in life: each quality is an innate resource available to all human beings no matter what their past or present circumstances. Most importantly for the purposes of psychotherapy, access to these states creates an internal healing environment.

在内部家庭系统的方法中（Schwartz，1995；2001），观察者的角色被赋予“自我”，这是一种内部状态，它依托于八种“C”品质：好奇（curiosity）、慈悲（compassion）、平静（calm）、清明（clarity）、创造力（creativity）、勇气（courage）、信心（confidence）和连接感（connectedness）。“自我”不仅仅是一种冥想状态，也不依赖于生活中拥有积极的经历：每一种品质都是所有人与生俱来的资源，无论他们过去或现在的处境如何。对心理治疗而言最重要的是，接触到这些状态会创造出一个内部的疗愈环境。

In the model I am describing here, integrating approaches drawn from Sensorimotor Psychotherapy and Internal Family Systems, there is also a fundamental assumption that these “C” qualities are accessible to any human being. They are never lost, no matter how sadistic and prolonged the traumatic experiences. However, I find that, for many lower-functioning clients with habitually inhibited precortical activity, it may be necessary to practice in order to access these states consistently. Some individuals may have to learn how to regulate autonomic activation sufficiently to keep the prefrontal cortex online before they can connect even to their curiosity. Some clients may be triggered by feelings of compassion, calm, courage, even curiosity. In these instances, I ask the client to pick one of the “C” qualities from Internal Family Systems and just focus on the practice of accessing that quality.

在我此处所描述的、整合了感觉运动心理治疗与内部家庭系统的模型中，也有一个基本假设：这些“C”品质对任何人都是可触及的。无论创伤经历多么残虐、多么漫长，它们都不会丧失。然而我发现，对于许多功能较低、皮层活动习惯性受抑制的来访者来说，可能需要加以练习才能稳定地接触到这些状态。有些个体可能必须先学会如何充分调节自主神经的激活，以保持前额叶皮层处于在线状态，然后才能连接到哪怕只是他们的好奇心。有些来访者甚至可能被慈悲、平静、勇气乃至好奇的感受所触发。在这些情况下，我会请来访者从内部家庭系统中挑选一种“C”品质，只专注于练习接触那一种品质。

Sarah at first picked compassion but quickly found that she felt so overwhelmed when she opened her heart to the young parts that, in the end, she couldn’t feel for them because she was too blended: she felt only the tidal wave of their emotions. Then she chose calm as the quality she wanted to cultivate but found that triggering, too. “I think it’s too close to having to be quiet and not move,” she realized. Finally, as her third choice, she picked curiosity. That didn’t trigger the parts, but because she blended so quickly with their intense reactivity, she often missed the chance to be curious. For her, it was easiest simply to observe her body responses: to mindfully notice when she was triggered and observe the activated thoughts, feelings, and body sensations as “things of interest,” rather than interpret or describe them in a narrative.

Sarah起初挑选了慈悲，但很快发现，当她向那些年幼的各部分敞开心扉时，她感到如此不堪重负，以至于最终她无法为它们而感受，因为她过度融合了：她只感受到它们情绪的滔天巨浪。接着她选择平静作为想要培养的品质，却发现这也会触发她。“我想这太接近于必须保持安静、不能动弹了，”她意识到。最后，作为第三个选择，她挑选了好奇。那并没有触发各部分，但因为她极快地与它们强烈的反应性融合在一起，她常常错过保持好奇的机会。对她来说，最简单的方式就是单纯地观察自己的身体反应：以正念注意自己何时被触发，并将被激活的想法、感受和身体感觉当作“值得关注之物”来观察，而不是用叙事去诠释或描述它们。

Adopting an attitude of mindful noticing or dual awareness also allows clients to slow down their thoughts, feelings, and physical reactions enough to listen more attentively to the parts. The therapist at first must support them by prompting them to “notice what’s happening,” “notice ‘who’ is here with us,” and by observing each thought or feeling as a separate communication: “I hear a part of you speaking today that feels overwhelmed and scared—do you notice that part, too? Can you be curious about what’s scaring her?”

采取一种正念觉察或双重觉察的态度，也能让来访者充分放慢他们的想法、感受和身体反应，从而更专注地倾听各部分。治疗师起初必须给予支持，提示他们“注意正在发生什么”，“注意‘谁’此刻和我们在一起”，并将每一个想法或感受作为一则独立的信息来观察：“今天我听到你的一个部分在说话，它感到不堪重负、很害怕——你也注意到那个部分了吗？你能对是什么让她害怕产生好奇吗？”

“See how that ashamed part immediately interpreted the chaos in your apartment as her fault! Maybe because the critical part was having such a fit about it …”

“看看那个羞耻部分立刻就把你公寓里的混乱解读成是她的错！也许是因为那个批评部分对此大发雷霆……”

“There’s quite a battle going on today inside you, huh? A lot of thoughts focused on whether or not to commit to your boyfriend—a lot of emotions and tears coming up. Notice the perspectives of both sides: what part wants you to stay connected no matter what? What part is afraid of him leaving? What part thinks you should get out while the going is good?”

“今天你内心正进行着一场相当激烈的战斗，是吧？很多想法都集中在要不要对你的男友许下承诺——很多情绪和泪水涌上来。注意双方各自的视角：哪个部分想要你无论如何都保持连接？哪个部分害怕他离开？哪个部分认为你应该趁着情况还好赶紧抽身？”

“The hopeless part is really struggling today, isn’t he? He doesn’t want to feel this way—he doesn’t want to be stuck in the past—but hopelessness and shame are his ‘safe place,’ and he’s afraid it’s not safe to have hope.”

“那个绝望部分今天真的很挣扎，不是吗？他不想要这样的感受——他不想被困在过去——但绝望和羞耻是他的‘安全之地’，而他害怕怀抱希望是不安全的。”

Differentiating Observation versus Meaning-Making

区分观察与意义建构

As Donald Meichenbaum (2012) reminds us, trauma is an experience beyond words, and the beliefs or stories that individuals attach to a traumatic event bias their meaning-making, leading to what he calls the creation of “self-defeating stories.” Which parts might write a self-defeating story?

正如Donald Meichenbaum（2012）提醒我们的，创伤是一种超越语言的体验，个体附加在创伤事件上的信念或故事会使其意义建构产生偏差，导致他所称的“自我挫败故事”的形成。哪些部分可能会写出一个自我挫败的故事？

The submit part would be likely to write a shame-based, hopeless story of victimization; the cry for help part a story of how no one came or cared; the fight part would communicate that it’s better to die than to continue to be used and abused. Only the going on with normal life with its access to the wider perspective of the prefrontal cortex has the ability to conceptualize at a higher level, to make meaning out of the apparently paradoxical feelings, beliefs, and instinctual reactions of the whole system. It requires higher order cognitive processing to comprehend that the belief one is “faking it” might be adaptive in a traumatic environment, while continuing to believe it later in life would be maladaptive. When beliefs are consistently differentiated from feelings, visceral reactions from perceptions, tension from relaxation (Ogden & Fisher, 2015), and when all of these inputs are connected to the parts contributing them, clients begin to get a clearer sense of who they are as a whole and the trauma logic underlying their actions and reactions.

顺从部分很可能会写出一个基于羞耻、绝望的受害故事；求助部分会写出一个无人前来、无人在乎的故事；战斗部分则会传达出宁可死去也不愿继续被利用和虐待的信息。只有能够接触到前额叶皮层更宽广视角的正常生活部分，才有能力在更高层次上进行概念化，从整个系统看似矛盾的感受、信念和本能反应中建构出意义。需要更高阶的认知加工，才能理解“自己是在装腔作势”这一信念在创伤环境中可能具有适应性，而在日后的生活中继续持有这一信念则将是适应不良的。当信念被持续地与感受区分开来，内脏反应被与知觉区分开来，紧张被与放松区分开来（Ogden和Fisher，2015），并且当所有这些输入都与贡献它们的各部分联系起来时，来访者便开始对自己作为一个整体是谁，以及他们行动与反应背后的创伤逻辑，有了更清晰的认识。

Blending, Shifting, and Switching of Parts

各部分的融合、转移与切换

Parts do not wear name tags, nor do personality systems come with road maps or instruction manuals. Every part of the client shares the same body, the same brain, the same environment. When we have a feeling or thought, it could be the expression of any part. To know “whose” feeling or thought requires familiarity: a personal relationship with the part that allows immediate recognition when we hear its voice. Or to know requires that we pause, listen carefully, and piece together the data or clues: which part would have reacted to that trigger? What kind of part would feel ashamed right now? But any of these acts of curiosity and interest are impossible when we identify with the part, when we “blend” with its feelings and reactions, interpreting them as our own. The term “blending,” created by Richard Schwartz (2001) and used in Internal Family Systems, refers to two confusing phenomena described by trauma clients: the tendency to identify with parts (“I am depressed,” “I want to die.”) and the tendency to become so flooded with their intense feelings and body responses that who “they” are and who “I” am become indistinguishable.

各部分不会佩戴名牌，人格系统也不会附带路线图或使用说明书。来访者的每一个部分都共享同一个身体、同一个大脑、同一个环境。当我们有某种感受或想法时，它可能是任何一个部分的表达。要知道这是“谁的”感受或想法，需要熟悉：一种与该部分的个人关系，使我们一听到它的声音便能立即辨认。或者，要知道这一点，需要我们停下来，仔细倾听，并把数据或线索拼凑起来：哪个部分会对那个触发因素作出反应？什么样的部分此刻会感到羞耻？但是，当我们认同某个部分、当我们与它的感受和反应“融合”、将它们诠释为我们自己的时候，所有这些好奇与兴趣之举都不可能发生。“融合”一词由Richard Schwartz（2001）提出并用于内部家庭系统，它指的是创伤来访者所描述的两种令人困惑的现象：认同各部分的倾向（“我很抑郁”，“我想死”），以及被它们强烈的感受和身体反应淹没到如此程度，以至于“它们”是谁和“我”是谁变得无法区分的倾向。

Catherine was on vacation with her husband in the Caribbean, a place they’d visited many times before and to which both felt a deep connection. The second morning of their trip, she woke up to inexplicable feelings of loneliness. She felt sad and empty, far away from her husband, even though he was only inches from her. “Believing” the feelings were hers, she found herself interpreting them: “He really doesn’t understand me—he means well, but he’s not really there for me.” By the time her husband woke up, she was in tears, accusing him of not truly caring. Only later in the day, when she was better connected to her normal life part, did she realize that the lonely feeling came from a young part who, disconnected and split off from Catherine’s present day life, didn’t experience the safety, support, and companionship she’d found in her marriage. The little part just needed reassurance that she wasn’t all alone.

Catherine正与丈夫在加勒比海度假，那是一个他们之前去过许多次、两人都对其怀有深厚情感连接的地方。旅程的第二个早晨，她醒来时被一种莫名的孤独感笼罩。她感到悲伤、空虚，与丈夫相距遥远，尽管他离她不过几英寸。“相信”这些感受是她自己的，她发现自己在诠释它们：“他真的不理解我——他出发点是好的，但他并没有真正在我身边。”等到丈夫醒来时，她已泪流满面，指责他并不是真的在乎。直到那天晚些时候，当她更好地连接到自己的正常生活部分时，她才意识到，那种孤独的感觉来自一个年幼的部分——这个部分与Catherine当下的生活断开并分裂开来，没有体验到她在婚姻中所找到的安全、支持与陪伴。那个小部分只是需要被安慰，确认她并非孤身一人。

Catherine had not only become blended with the young part but had shifted into an altered state during her dream sleep so that, on awakening, she was in another time and place. Now in the implicit memory state of a little girl in a very lonely, frightening family environment, she lost any connection to her present day perspective altogether. Gone was her happy marriage, gone the successful creation of a new life, a new safety, and a new family in which she was welcome and valued. She was back in Michigan, and it didn’t feel safe.

Catherine不仅与那个年幼的部分融合了，而且在她做梦的睡眠中转移进了一种改变了的状态，以致醒来时，她身处另一个时间和地点。此刻处于一个身处极度孤独、令人恐惧的家庭环境中的小女孩的内隐记忆状态里，她彻底失去了与自己当下视角的任何连接。她幸福的婚姻消失了，她成功创造的新生活、新的安全感以及一个接纳并珍视她的新家庭也都消失了。她回到了密歇根，而那里感觉并不安全。

Rachel alternated between feeling depressed and feeling irritated—sometimes irritated more with herself, sometimes annoyed by others. The depression took hold most powerfully when her partner, Susan, was busy with work and friends, leaving little time or energy for Rachel. At such times, the depression often convinced her that it would be better to die than to live, but, knowing how much it would hurt her partner to lose her, she would fight the impulse to act on her suicidal feelings. When she felt the irritability, on the other hand, she lost the empathic perspective toward her partner: feeling annoyed and “morally correct” in her judgment, she had no qualms about hurting Susan’s feelings. The depression and abandonment feelings were triggered by the loss of Susan’s attention, whereas the critical feelings were usually triggered by Susan’s tendency to “rescue” friends and family who “needed help,” even if it meant being disconnected from Rachel and absorbed in the crises of others.
     When Rachel was asked to notice the depression as a depressed part and to be curious about how old that part was, the number “12” immediately came to mind. “That was a tough age,” she recalled. Asked to focus on the depressed 12-year-old and to notice what other feelings went with the depression, Rachel could feel a sense of not belonging, of not being wanted or worthy of notice, as well as fear that being noticed might bring something bad instead of good. During her childhood, Rachel’s mother could barely manage the stress of having six children she hadn’t truly wanted; being noticed was a mixed blessing because more often than not, it led to anger or demands to perform rather than reassurance and closeness. It made sense that this part would be triggered by a partner who had too many people depending on her. Despite how loving and validating Susan tended to be, Rachel’s 12-year-old part held a different reality. When Susan was busy, she re-experienced the implicit memories of a cold, unaffectionate mother whose attention was divided. When asked to notice the irritability as another part, Rachel immediately thought of her mother: “Oh, my God! The irritable part sounds just like my mother—frugality and avoidance of “excess” emotion were moral issues for her.” When she was asked to “unblend” or mindfully separate from the irritable part and notice it as a part, she became aware that “she” (i.e., her normal life self) prized the unconditionally accepting relationship she and Susan had developed much more than the judgmental part did. The judgmental part was still trying to enforce her mother’s rules decades later, Rachel realized with a laugh—as if her mother’s approval were still necessary for survival!

Rachel在感到抑郁与感到恼怒之间交替——有时更多是对自己恼怒，有时是被他人惹恼。当她的伴侣Susan忙于工作和朋友、几乎没有时间或精力留给Rachel时，抑郁的掌控最为强烈。在这种时候，抑郁常常说服她，死了比活着更好，但她知道失去她会让伴侣多么痛苦，于是会与付诸自杀感受的冲动作斗争。另一方面，当她感到易怒时，她便失去了对伴侣的共情视角：感到恼怒，并自认为在评判上“道德正确”，她毫不顾忌地伤害Susan的感受。抑郁和被抛弃的感受是由失去Susan的关注所触发的，而批评性的感受通常是由Susan倾向于“拯救”那些“需要帮助”的朋友和家人所触发的，即便这意味着与Rachel断开连接、沉浸于他人的危机之中。
当Rachel被要求把抑郁当作一个抑郁部分来注意，并对那个部分有多大年纪产生好奇时，“12”这个数字立刻浮现在她脑海。“那是个艰难的年龄，”她回忆道。当被要求专注于那个抑郁的12岁孩子，并注意还有哪些感受伴随着抑郁时，Rachel能够感受到一种不属于这里、不被需要、不值得被注意的感觉，以及一种害怕——害怕被注意可能带来的是坏事而非好事。在Rachel的童年时期，她的母亲几乎无力应付养育六个她并非真正想要的孩子所带来的压力；被注意是一种喜忧参半的事，因为它往往带来的是愤怒或要求她有所表现，而非安慰与亲近。这个部分会被一个有太多人依赖她的伴侣所触发，是合情合理的。尽管Susan往往充满爱意、给予肯定，Rachel那个12岁的部分却持有一种不同的现实。当Susan忙碌时，她重新体验到一个冷漠、缺乏温情、注意力被分散的母亲的内隐记忆。当被要求把这种易怒当作另一个部分来注意时，Rachel立刻想到了她的母亲：“天哪！这个易怒部分听起来就和我母亲一模一样——节俭和回避‘过度’情绪对她来说是道德问题。”当她被要求从这个易怒部分“解融合”、以正念与之分离并把它当作一个部分来注意时，她意识到“她”（即她的正常生活自我）远比那个评判部分更珍视她与Susan所建立起来的无条件接纳的关系。Rachel笑着意识到，那个评判部分在几十年后仍试图执行她母亲的规矩——仿佛母亲的认可对生存仍然不可或缺！

Rachel exemplifies the phenomenon of “blending,” while Catherine exemplifies “shifting” of mental states. Rachel could easily reality-test her perceptions; she could step back from them; and she could even be curious about why she was having such strong reactions. Catherine, on the other hand, experienced palpable changes in both emotional state and perspective during which she had no memory or connection to other feelings and states. On the first day of their vacation, she had felt a sense of appreciation for being in such a beautiful place with a loving, supportive husband. But after “time-traveling” in her sleep, she woke up to profound feelings of loneliness. There was no curious “Why am I feeling this way?” in her mind, only a frantic sense of urgency to end the painful feeling.

Rachel是“融合”现象的范例，而Catherine则是心理状态“转移”的范例。Rachel可以轻松地对自己的知觉进行现实检验；她可以从中退后一步；她甚至能够对自己为何会有如此强烈的反应产生好奇。而Catherine则经历了情绪状态和视角上清晰可感的改变，在此期间她对其他感受和状态没有任何记忆或连接。度假的第一天，她曾因与一位充满爱意、给予支持的丈夫共处于如此美丽的地方而心怀感激。但在睡梦中“穿越时空”之后，她醒来时被深切的孤独感笼罩。她脑海中没有好奇的“我为什么会有这种感觉？”，只有一种要结束这痛苦感受的疯狂紧迫感。

Nelly, on the other hand, had dissociative identity disorder (DID) and often “switched” from state to state, a cardinal symptom of DID in which changes in state are sudden, frequent, and often accompanied by losses of consciousness. (For example, had Catherine “switched,” she might not have recognized her husband at all; she might not have known where she was or even how old or what her name was.)

而Nelly则患有解离性身份障碍（DID），常常在状态之间“切换”，这是DID的一个主要症状，其状态的变化突然、频繁，并且常常伴随意识的丧失。（例如，假如Catherine曾发生“切换”，她可能根本认不出她的丈夫；她可能不知道自己身在何处，甚至不知道自己多大年纪或叫什么名字。）

When Nelly was “in” her depressed “default setting” part, there was no other reality, no other perspective. During the day, she found she could switch states by making lunch dates with friends. Their affection and interest acted as a positive trigger, facilitating a switch into her going on with normal life self. One moment, she would feel shame and self-loathing, questioning why she had made a lunch date at all, then, as soon as her friend arrived, the normal life part that had made the appointment would be present. At night, feeling better after seeing her friends, “she” would make a commitment to herself to get up in the morning and start the day, no matter how badly she felt. But when she woke up “hijacked” by the depressed part, she would have no memory of the commitment “she” had made the night before. Then “she” would go back to sleep, not wanting to face the day, and when “she” woke up again in the early afternoon, the ashamed part would feel “pathetic” and inadequate. It was not important that Nelly’s dissociative disorder be diagnosed, but identifying the switching was important. Without realizing that she switched, she could only interpret her behavior through the lens of failure.

当Nelly“处于”她那个抑郁的“默认设置”部分中时，便没有别的现实、别的视角。白天，她发现自己可以通过与朋友约午餐来切换状态。朋友们的关爱和兴趣起到了积极触发的作用，促成她切换进自己的正常生活自我。前一刻，她还感到羞耻和自我厌恶，质问自己究竟为什么要约午餐，然后，朋友一到，那个约定见面的正常生活部分便会出现。到了晚上，见过朋友后感觉好些了，“她”会向自己许下承诺，无论感觉多么糟糕，第二天早上都要起床开始新的一天。但当她醒来时被那个抑郁部分“劫持”，她对“她”前一晚许下的承诺便毫无记忆。然后“她”会重新睡去，不愿面对这一天，而当“她”在午后再次醒来时，那个羞耻部分会感到“可悲”和不胜任。Nelly的解离障碍是否被诊断出来并不重要，但识别出这种切换却很重要。若没有意识到自己发生了切换，她只能透过失败的镜片来诠释自己的行为。

All of these clients, regardless of diagnosis, were learning to separate from the feelings, beliefs, activation, and body responses of their parts, rather than automatically assuming that all of their mental and emotional life belonged to one self. They were practicing mindful noticing and the ability to identify parts as they appeared moment by moment rather than identify with those parts as “who I am.” Over and over again, they became aware that each time they noticed a part as “she” or “he,” rather than automatically identifying with its feelings, they felt some relief—a little or even a lot. When, in addition, they became more deeply curious about a part, they began naturally to feel compassion toward him or her—in spite of themselves or in spite of the parts that were hostile toward the other parts. There was a gain in perspective, a feeling of greater calm, and that calming would often “turn on” the prefrontal cortex, facilitating more creative solutions to problems that represented internal conflict between parts.

所有这些来访者，无论其诊断如何，都在学习从自己各部分的感受、信念、激活和身体反应中分离出来，而不是自动地假设他们全部的心理和情感生活都属于同一个自我。他们在练习正念觉察，以及在各部分逐时出现时识别它们、而非把那些部分认同为“我是谁”的能力。一次又一次地，他们意识到，每当他们把一个部分注意为“她”或“他”，而不是自动认同它的感受时，他们就会感到某种宽慰——或多或少，甚至非常之多。此外，当他们对一个部分产生更深的好奇时，他们便开始自然而然地对他或她生出慈悲——尽管他们自己未必情愿，或尽管那些对其他部分怀有敌意的部分并不情愿。视角得到了拓展，产生了一种更加平静的感觉，而这种平静往往会“开启”前额叶皮层，促成对那些代表各部分之间内部冲突的问题更具创造性的解决方案。

Facilitating Empathy

促进共情

Not only does the therapist model bearing witness to each part’s qualities, emotions, and trauma-related perspective, she or he must also provide the missing link of empathy for each part. Knowing that simply observing and naming what they observe as a “part” is challenging enough for clients, I try to model mindful use of the language of parts. I notice their “voices,” feelings, and points of view, often before the client does, name their appearance, and then I deliberately add a tone of warmth or communicate pleasure and appreciation for each part. I describe their plight (“What was a little boy supposed to do?”) when clients are struggling with compassion. I try to verbalize appreciation for their contributions to the client’s survival: “Had he not given up and given in, what would have happened to all of you? How would your stepfather have reacted?” Most importantly, I share my own personal experience of the client’s parts to bring them alive and make them “real.” Using language that evokes positive feelings and associations, I try to communicate that they are much more than disembodied implicit memories without a context. I might admire the ingenuity of a very young part: “That little girl was one smart cookie, wasn’t she? Oh my!” Or the courage of an adolescent part: “That 15-year-old was one determined young lady, huh? But, you know, she was always very creative, too—who would have thought to ‘hide out’ in a hospital where her parents couldn’t get to her? It was pretty amazing how she pulled that off. It’s not so easy to keep being admitted to hospitals to get away from your parents!” If we are speaking about an adolescent male part, I might say, “Wow, he took some big risks. Nelly responded to my helping her notice the depressed part by judging its behavior: “Well, she’s a loser—she doesn’t let me even get out of bed!”

[I raised my hand as if to volunteer, and we both laughed.] No baby in the crib volunteers to be depressed or to hate herself … let’s be curious about how she lost her hope …”

对于我帮助她注意那个抑郁部分，Nelly的回应是评判它的行为：“哼，她是个废物——她连让我下床都不肯！”
我立刻向她发起挑战：“你是在暗示一个抑郁的11岁部分选择了成为这样吗？她在出生时就自愿要做一个‘废物’？〔我举起手仿佛在自愿报名，我们俩都笑了。〕没有哪个躺在婴儿床里的婴儿会自愿变得抑郁或憎恨自己……让我们对她是怎样失去希望的产生好奇吧……”

“Seeing” the Parts: Externalized Mindfulness

“看见”各部分：外化的正念

When individuals are too identified or too blended with the their parts’ feelings and beliefs to access mindful observation, as is common with very dysregulated clients, therapists need other ways of facilitating dual awareness—enough dual awareness that the normal life self develops a perspective on and a relationship with the trauma-related part rather than blend with it.

当个体过度认同或过度融合于其各部分的感受和信念，以至于无法进入正念观察时——这在极度失调的来访者身上很常见——治疗师就需要其他方式来促进双重觉察，使其足以让正常生活自我对创伤相关部分形成一种视角并与之建立关系，而不是与它融合。

There are a number of ways to achieve dual awareness, even in very decompensated individuals, but all depend upon multi-modal interventions. Because visual focusing seems to increase curiosity and activate the medial prefrontal cortex, trauma therapists can benefit from including an easel or large clipboard as part of their office equipment (as I do). For example, I might ask a client to draw a picture of some part with which he or she has been struggling and then invite the client to look at the picture with curiosity: what does the drawing tell him about this part? What is he learning from the drawing that challenges what he’s previously believed about the part? How does he feel toward this part now?

即便在极度失代偿的个体身上，也有若干种方式可以实现双重觉察，但所有方式都依赖于多模态的干预。由于视觉聚焦似乎能增加好奇心并激活前额叶内侧皮层，创伤治疗师可以从把一块画架或大型写字夹板纳入办公设备中受益（我便是这么做的）。例如，我可能会请来访者画出某个他或她一直在与之挣扎的部分，然后邀请来访者带着好奇去看这幅画：这幅画告诉了他关于这个部分的什么？他从这幅画中学到了什么，足以挑战他先前对这个部分的看法？他现在对这个部分有何感受？

Or we can help clients decode struggles between parts by creating a “flow chart” that tracks the internal relationships between the parts in conflict, starting with an initial trigger and then noting step by step which part or parts were activated. Drawing a rectangle at the top of the page to depict the going on with normal life self, clients are asked to observe retrospectively, frame by frame, the sequence of triggers and parts that set the internal conflict in motion. The trigger is usually represented by a large arrow shape that I color in red. Next, the client is asked to recall which part reacted first to the triggering stimulus, and that part is depicted by a circle within which the therapist can write the approximate age or some description of the part (i.e., “depressed part,” “anxious part”) by which to recognize it in the future. The therapist next asks: “How did this part react to [the trigger]? How did he feel?” Then the therapist writes down the words connected to that part below its circle, making sure to identify its feelings and beliefs: “Believes she is disgusting and worthless—‘just wants to crawl into a hole’.”

或者，我们可以通过制作一张“流程图”来帮助来访者解码各部分之间的挣扎，这张图追踪冲突中各部分之间的内部关系，从最初的触发因素开始，然后一步步记录哪个或哪些部分被激活了。在页面顶端画一个矩形来表示正常生活自我，来访者被要求逐帧地回溯观察那一连串触发因素和部分，正是它们启动了这场内部冲突。触发因素通常用一个我涂成红色的大箭头形状来表示。接下来，来访者被要求回忆哪个部分最先对触发刺激作出反应，那个部分用一个圆圈来表示，治疗师可以在圆圈内写下该部分的大致年龄或某些描述（即“抑郁部分”、“焦虑部分”），以便日后辨认它。治疗师接着问：“这个部分对〔触发因素〕作出了怎样的反应？他有何感受？”然后治疗师把与那个部分相关的话语写在它的圆圈下方，确保识别出它的感受和信念：“相信她是令人厌恶、毫无价值的——‘只想钻进一个洞里去’。”

Following that, the client is asked to observe, “What part got triggered by the ashamed part?” For example, Nelly would have said, “Then the hopeless part gets triggered: she just keeps saying, ‘It’s all bad and it will never get better.’” That part in turn gets represented by a circle within which is written its age and “name” or descriptor and below which are the words that describe its perspective and emotions. Typically, internal struggles occur between 3 to 6 different parts, and the flow-charting continues until a full picture of the conflict or problem emerges and can be appreciated. In the following example, the client came in feeling distressed and suicidal, so dysregulated that she could not unblend from the parts, so I asked her if we could diagram what was happening so “we can both really ‘get’ it—we know there are some pretty distressed parts, but we don’t understand what’s triggering them.”

在那之后，来访者被要求观察：“是哪个部分被那个羞耻部分触发了？”例如，Nelly会说：“然后那个绝望部分被触发了：她只是不停地说，‘一切都很糟糕，永远不会好起来。’”那个部分也同样用一个圆圈来表示，圈内写上它的年龄和“名字”或描述语，圈下写上描述其视角和情绪的话语。通常，内部斗争发生在3到6个不同的部分之间，而流程图绘制会持续进行，直到冲突或问题的全貌浮现并能够被领会为止。在下面这个例子中，来访者前来时感到痛苦并有自杀念头，失调到无法从各部分中解融合，于是我问她，我们能否把正在发生的事情画成图，这样“我们俩都能真正‘弄明白’它——我们知道有一些相当痛苦的部分，但我们不理解是什么在触发它们。”

Clients rarely refuse to diagram because it tends to feel less threatening than talking about their emotions, but I can add, “And if we start diagramming, and you find it too overwhelming or not helpful, just tell me.” “Let’s start with what happened first—there was some trigger, and then the first feelings you felt were … what?”

来访者很少拒绝画图，因为这往往比谈论自己的情绪感觉更不具威胁性，但我可以补充道：“而且如果我们开始画图，你发现它太让人不堪重负或没有帮助，尽管告诉我。”“我们先从最先发生的事情开始——有某个触发因素，然后你最先感受到的感觉是……什么？”

CLIENT: “I felt so lonely and unwanted—like no one was there—I’ve just been abandoned.”

来访者：“我感到如此孤独、不被需要——好像没有人在那里——我就这么被抛弃了。”

ME: “A little part was triggered, huh? [While she talks, I draw a circle for the young part and describe her distress in the same words used by the client.] Back to that place of painful loneliness where no one wanted her. How sad! And then what happened next? What part came up next?”

我：“一个小部分被触发了，是吧？〔在她说话的同时，我为这个年幼的部分画了一个圆圈，并用来访者所使用的同样的话语描述她的痛苦。〕回到了那个无人想要她的、痛苦孤独的地方。多么悲伤啊！那接下来发生了什么？接下来又冒出了哪个部分？”

CLIENT: “Then I felt such intense shame—it was overwhelming—I felt so disgusting and dirty that no wonder no one wanted me.”

来访者：“然后我感到如此强烈的羞耻——它压倒了我——我感到自己如此令人厌恶、肮脏，难怪没有人想要我。”

ME: “So the little part triggered the shame part and the shame part blamed herself! And not just for the little part’s being alone but for everything—she just took it all on her shoulders. It was all her. That’s what she does, doesn’t she? She always assumes it’s her.”

我：“所以那个小部分触发了羞耻部分，而羞耻部分责怪了她自己！而且不只是为那个小部分的孤独，而是为一切——她就这样把所有的一切都扛在了自己肩上。全都是她的错。这就是她惯常做的，不是吗？她总是认定那是她的错。”

Much like the diagram in Figure 5.1, what feels like an internal struggle to the client usually appears in the diagram as a series of parts, each of which triggers each other in succession, leading to some impulse to give up, hurt the body, die, or run away—some desperate measure for what feels like desperate times.

正如图5.1中的图示一样，对来访者而言感觉像是一场内部斗争的东西，在图中通常呈现为一系列部分，它们彼此相继触发，最终导向某种放弃、伤害身体、死去或逃跑的冲动——一种针对看似绝望境况的孤注一掷之举。

The visual images symbolizing each different part and his or her feelings tend to spontaneously invite unblending. As they study the diagram, I can often see a shift in clients’ body language or tone of voice indicating to me that the normal life self is noticing the parts instead of blending with them. And if unblending does not occur spontaneously, I can ask the client to focus specifically on each element of the drawing separately and increase curiosity about each part, observing each set of feelings and thoughts and noticing how each has made meaning of the implicit feelings triggered by other parts.

那些象征着每个不同部分及其感受的视觉图像，往往会自发地邀请解融合发生。当他们端详这幅图时，我常常能看到来访者肢体语言或语气上的转变，这向我表明正常生活自我正在注意各部分，而不是与它们融合。而如果解融合没有自发发生，我可以请来访者分别专门聚焦于图画中的每一个元素，增加对每个部分的好奇，观察每一组感受和想法，并注意每个部分是如何对其他部分所触发的内隐感受进行意义建构的。

“Notice how it all began when your boyfriend was late, and he triggered the little part—she was so hurt! She felt so disappointed and unimportant, and then the hopeless part triggered her more and triggered your fight part—and she was beside herself! Can you see how it worked?” Observing on the diagram how suicidal, addictive, acting out, or self-destructive parts get triggered by the emotions of the vulnerable parts and then emerge to offer the young parts a “way out” of their distress further clarifies that the underlying purpose of trauma-related self-destructive behavior is to bring relief and regulation to the body: the very opposite of trying to die. Once curiosity and even compassion are elicited, the next step is to diagram a solution—in this case, a solution for the lonely part and a solution for the suicidal part. A solution or intervention is always best if it emerges organically out of the concern or protectiveness of the going on with normal life self (Figure 5.2).

“注意这一切是如何开始的：你的男友迟到了，他触发了那个小部分——她受到了多大的伤害！她感到如此失望、如此无足轻重，然后那个绝望部分进一步触发了她，并触发了你的战斗部分——而她则方寸大乱！你能看出它是怎样运作的吗？”在图上观察自杀、成瘾、付诸行动或自我毁灭的各部分如何被脆弱部分的情绪所触发，继而现身为年幼的部分提供一条摆脱其痛苦的“出路”，这进一步阐明了创伤相关的自我毁灭行为其根本目的是为身体带来缓解和调节：这与试图死去恰恰相反。一旦好奇乃至慈悲被唤起，下一步就是把一个解决方案画成图——在这个例子中，是为孤独部分提供一个解决方案，为自杀部分提供一个解决方案。如果一个解决方案或干预是从正常生活自我的关切或保护之心中有机地生长出来的，那总是最好的（图5.2）。

The first diagram(s) depicts for the client how the system of parts became activated and polarized as a result of the triggering. The second diagram is used to depict how the normal life self can now provide healing and care for the child parts, making the suicidal part’s “attempt to help” unnecessary. Had I treated the suicidal part’s “offer to help” as life-threatening suicidal ideation and tried to hospitalize the client, both the fight part and the attach part would have been further triggered. The attach part would have felt more alone, banished to the hospital, and the fight part would have felt hostility and entrapment by a controlling authority figure.

第一幅（或几幅）图为来访者描绘出各部分组成的系统是如何因触发而被激活并两极化的。第二幅图则用来描绘正常生活自我如今可以如何为孩子般的各部分提供疗愈和关怀，从而使自杀部分的“试图帮忙”变得不再必要。假如我把自杀部分的“提议帮忙”当作危及生命的自杀意念，并试图让来访者住院，那么战斗部分和依恋部分都会被进一步触发。依恋部分会感到更加孤独，被放逐到医院；而战斗部分则会感到敌意，以及被一个控制欲强的权威人物所困。

Figure 5.2 Diagramming a Solution for the System.

图5.2 为系统绘制解决方案图。

In this example and many others, the solution to what could have been a life-or-death crisis results from a comforting reparative experience being offered to the vulnerable little part. A compassionate normal life self is asked to take the lonely, ashamed, hopeless child parts “under her wing,” communicating a sense of care and protection. Even if the client is struggling with compassion, the visual image of the arms encircling the young parts evokes positive sensations in the client’s body: warmth, protectiveness, a smile, the impulse to move toward the drawing and thus the parts. The advantage of the diagrams is the ability to introduce something foreign and potentially challenging through non-verbal communication. Had I asked this client to say, “I will take care of you,” to the younger parts, I might have received the answer: “No, I don’t want to take care of them!” But when I draw the arms and describe the intervention as a gesture, “See what happens when you take these young parts under your wing so they aren’t so overwhelmed and scared,” no client protests. As I speak, I make a gesture with my right arm as if taking someone under my wing, and I repeat the gesture again each time I say the words “under your wing.” The somatic communication speaks directly to the parts themselves. The left brain, specializing in words, may react negatively to the verbal communication but cannot block the somatic message intended for the right brain (Gazzaniga, 1985). The younger parts can feel the “wing” in the drawing and in my gesture.

在这个例子以及许多其他例子中，原本可能演变为生死危机的局面，其解决之道来自于向脆弱的小部分提供一种令人安慰的修复性体验。一个充满慈悲的正常生活自我被请求将那些孤独、羞耻、绝望的孩子般的各部分“护在羽翼之下”，传达出一种关怀与保护之感。即便来访者在慈悲方面挣扎，双臂环抱着年幼部分的视觉图像也会在来访者的身体里唤起积极的感觉：温暖、保护欲、微笑，以及朝着图画、从而朝着各部分靠近的冲动。这些图示的优势在于，它们能够通过非言语的沟通引入某种陌生且可能具有挑战性的东西。假如我请这位来访者对那些年幼的部分说“我会照顾你”，我可能会得到这样的回答：“不，我不想照顾它们！”但当我画出那双臂膀，并把这一干预描述为一个姿态——“看看当你把这些年幼的部分护在羽翼之下、让它们不再那么不堪重负和害怕时，会发生什么”——没有哪位来访者会抗议。在我说话的同时，我用右臂做出一个仿佛把某人护在羽翼之下的姿态，并且每当我说出“护在你的羽翼之下”这几个字时，便再次重复这一姿态。这种身体上的沟通直接对各部分本身说话。专司言语的左脑可能会对言语沟通作出负面反应，但它无法阻断那条意在传达给右脑的身体讯息（Gazzaniga，1985）。年幼的部分能够在图画中、在我的姿态中感受到那只“羽翼”。

Another means of externalizing the parts’ struggles and conflicts so they can be witnessed is to use objects to represent them: sand tray figurines, animal figures, stones and crystals, even rubber ducks. Notice that all of these figures are appealing to a child’s mind, not just an adult mind and body. The therapist must never forget that the client is not a “he” or “she” but is a system composed of parts of all ages from infants to wise elders.

另一种将各部分的挣扎与冲突外化、以便它们能够被见证的方式，是用物件来代表它们：沙盘小人偶、动物模型、石头和水晶，甚至橡皮鸭。请注意，所有这些小物件都吸引着孩子的心，而不仅仅是成人的身心。治疗师永远不可忘记，来访者并不是一个“他”或“她”，而是一个由从婴儿到智慧长者、各种年龄的部分所组成的系统。

Cath came for a consultation to assess whether a DID diagnosis would be more appropriate for her than psychotic disorder, the label given to her each time she reported hearing voices. She was challenging to interview, though, because she was constantly distracted by what seemed to be a very heated internal conversation among her voices. Her mouth moved as if she were talking, though she emitted no sound; she would gesture as though angry, a scowl on her face. Occasionally, I could read her lips as she shook her head vigorously and mouthed “No, no!” Each time I used the word “part” or asked her about the voices that were talking to her, the dialogue might pause for a moment, but there was no clear sign I had been heard—until the third appointment to which Cath came carrying a little plastic bag. “Here, these are the parts,” she said, and she dumped the contents of her little bag on the table and promptly returned to her internal conversation. A small pile of miniature rubber ducks sat on my coffee table, a gift from Cath representing the parts she couldn’t yet tell me about because she was too overwhelmed by their sheer number and the intensity of their feelings and conflicts.

Cath前来咨询，以评估对她而言，DID诊断是否比精神病性障碍更为合适——后者是她每次报告听到声音时被贴上的标签。然而，对她进行访谈颇具挑战，因为她不断地被一场似乎在她那些声音之间进行的、非常激烈的内部对话所分心。她的嘴在动，仿佛在说话，却没有发出任何声音；她会做出愤怒的姿态，脸上带着怒容。偶尔，当她用力摇头、口型说着“不，不！”时，我能读出她的唇语。每当我使用“部分”这个词，或问及那些与她交谈的声音时，对话或许会暂停片刻，但没有任何明确的迹象表明我被听到了——直到第三次会面，Cath带着一个小塑料袋前来。“给，这些就是那些部分，”她说着，把小袋里的东西倒在桌上，旋即又回到她的内部对话中去。一小堆微型橡皮鸭就摆在我的咖啡桌上，这是Cath送来的礼物，代表着那些她还无法向我讲述的部分，因为它们的数量之多以及它们感受和冲突的强烈程度让她太不堪重负了。

Following that session, Cath gradually learned to describe her week-to-week internal problems and conflicts using the ducks, choosing different sizes and colors of little rubber ducks to represent different parts. Weeks later, she even brought in a rubber stress ball in the shape of a human brain to represent, “that big brain you keep talking about,” proof that she had been listening to me after all when I talked about the “wise mind” part she had in her grownup brain. Each week, we would address whatever issue she brought in by using the ducks to create a psychodrama-like sculpture that depicted how inner conflicts between parts had resulted in a crisis or problem. Then I would describe duck by duck what she had shown me:

那次治疗之后，Cath逐渐学会用这些鸭子来描述她每周的内部问题和冲突，选用不同大小、不同颜色的小橡皮鸭来代表不同的部分。几周后，她甚至带来了一个人脑形状的橡胶减压球，用来代表“你一直在说的那个大大的脑子”，这证明当我谈到她成人大脑中那个“智慧心智”部分时，她终归是一直在听我说的。每一周，我们都会通过用这些鸭子创作一个类似心理剧的雕塑来处理她带来的任何议题，这个雕塑描绘出各部分之间的内部冲突是如何导致某个危机或问题的。然后我会一只鸭子一只鸭子地描述她向我展示的内容：

“When the little orange duck [I pointed to it to focus her attention] got triggered by the angry man at the post office, the green duck started to run out of the building, which freaked out Jeremy [the medium-sized red teenage duck]. He thought the man was after him, so he jumped in the car and started driving away too fast, and that scared all the young parts! He drove so fast, and he was so mad and scared that it frightened the other ducks even more. What they need is to have the big brain help them at the post office so the angry man doesn’t yell at them. What does the part with the big brain think should be done about this? The child parts like to go to the post office, but they always get yelled at because people get confused when child parts are talking in a grownup’s body. They think the grownup is being weird. Maybe they shouldn’t be going into stores alone without a grownup—what do you think?”

“当那只橙色的小鸭子〔我指着它以集中她的注意力〕被邮局里那个愤怒的男人触发时，绿鸭子开始往大楼外跑，这把Jeremy〔那只中等大小的红色青少年鸭子〕吓坏了。他以为那个男人在追他，于是跳进车里，开得太快地逃走了，这把所有年幼的部分都吓到了！他开得那么快，又那么愤怒和害怕，以至于把其他鸭子吓得更厉害了。它们所需要的，是让那个大脑子在邮局帮助它们，好让那个愤怒的男人不冲它们吼叫。那个有着大脑子的部分认为应该怎么处理这件事呢？孩子般的部分喜欢去邮局，但它们总是挨吼，因为当孩子般的部分用一个成年人的身体说话时，人们会感到困惑。他们觉得这个成年人在犯怪。也许它们不该在没有大人陪同的情况下独自走进商店——你怎么看？”

Invariably, with some coaching from me, the rubber brain would come up with a creative solution that reflected wider perspective-taking made possible by Cath’s being able to observe the duck tableau’s depiction of the whole system and the whole of an experience. The internal intensity and the “noise” of the voices made it difficult for her to be mindful, but she could focus on the ducks and access more curiosity. Cath began to see the patterns of chaos and crisis that resulted when she switched into younger parts and then they were triggered by an external stimulus. Their hyperaroused emotional reactions would then trigger the defensive responses of fight and flight parts like Jeremy, retriggering the young parts all over again. Whether achieved through mindful observation, drawing, diagramming, or “duck therapy,” externalization or visual depiction of the parts in interaction facilitates a wider field of awareness, greater curiosity and interest, a sense of perspective (“I’m safe now even though my parts don’t believe it”), and increasing capacity to access the better judgment of the wise mind.

每一次，在我的一些引导下，那个橡胶脑子都会想出一个富有创造性的解决方案，它反映出一种更宽广的视角采择，而这正是因为Cath能够观察鸭子组成的画面对整个系统、对一段完整经历的描绘才得以可能。内部的强烈程度和那些声音的“噪音”使她难以保持正念，但她可以专注于鸭子，从而接触到更多的好奇。Cath开始看出，当她切换进更年幼的部分、而后它们又被某个外部刺激触发时所导致的混乱与危机的模式。它们高度唤起的情绪反应继而会触发像Jeremy这样的战斗和逃跑部分的防御性反应，进而又再次触发那些年幼的部分。无论是通过正念观察、绘画、画流程图，还是“鸭子疗法”来实现，将各部分在互动中的状态加以外化或视觉化呈现，都能促成一个更宽广的觉察场域、更大的好奇与兴趣、一种视角感（“即便我的各部分不相信，我现在是安全的”），以及不断增长的、接触智慧心智更佳判断力的能力。

Blending and Reality-Testing

融合与现实检验

Because her survival strategy had historically been to automatically blend with whatever part was activated in a given moment, Annie had never questioned the information she regularly received through her body (tension, held breath, elevated heart rate, shaking and trembling), her thoughts (disparaging, hopeless, scathing), or her emotions (shame, panic, dread). Small, unremarkable daily events, including positive experiences like being asked by a potential friend to go to lunch, repeatedly triggered the parts and their implicit memories, which triggered other parts. “That’s why it’s easier to have a beer at 10 in the morning and go back to bed.” For example, being invited on a lunch date triggered an insecure child part afraid she wouldn’t know how to act, which then triggered hypervigilant parts guarding childhood secrets by discouraging close friendships, which then triggered judgmental, humiliating parts: “How stupid! What a ridiculous idea! Why would anyone want to be friends with you? She’ll see how dumb you are in a minute.” Between the sense of danger evoked by normal life triggers and the shame triggered by the judgmental parts, it was natural that, in her blended state, Annie assumed her world must be humiliating and dangerous and she was defective and unwanted.

由于Annie历来的生存策略就是自动地与某一时刻被激活的任何部分相融合，她从未质疑过自己经常通过身体（紧张、屏住呼吸、心率升高、颤抖发抖）、想法（贬低、绝望、尖刻）或情绪（羞耻、惊恐、畏惧）所接收到的信息。微小、不起眼的日常事件，包括像被一位潜在朋友邀请去吃午餐这样的积极经历，都反复触发着各部分及其内隐记忆，而这些又触发了其他部分。“这就是为什么早上十点喝杯啤酒再回床上睡觉更省事。”例如，被邀请共进午餐触发了一个不安的孩子部分，她害怕自己不知道该如何举止，这又触发了那些守护童年秘密、通过劝阻亲密友谊来加以保护的高度警惕的部分，而这些部分继而又触发了评判性的、羞辱性的部分：“多蠢啊！多荒唐的主意！谁会想跟你做朋友？她过一会儿就会看出你有多笨。”在正常生活触发因素所唤起的危险感与评判部分所触发的羞耻之间，在融合状态下，Annie自然而然地认定她的世界一定是羞辱性且危险的，而她自己则是有缺陷、不被需要的。

Blending Keeps the Trauma “Alive”

融合让创伤“活着”

How could any individual resolve traumatic experience in what subjectively feels like an unsafe, hostile environment? Clients and therapists alike often collude in believing that trauma can be resolved by processing the events even when critical voices are still attacking the individual at will, using the same words or scathing tone as the perpetrator. Similarly, both may equate “safety” with freedom from self-harm or a non-abusive home environment, as I did when I first met Annie. It might not occur to us that a “felt sense” of safety is likely to be unattainable if the client is habitually blended with child parts who still feel unwelcome, frightened, or ashamed or suicidal. Although the external environment may now be safe from an objective perspective, clients who are blended with their implicit memories and parts may have no bodily or emotional sense of safety that could reassure the parts that “it” is over. Before trauma can be resolved, the client must learn how to unblend from parts so that the realities of both brains can be appreciated. From an unblended, dual awareness perspective, the normal life self can learn to orient to the immediate environment by focusing visual attention, can correctly evaluate the level of safety, but also feel the parts’ fear and bracing for danger as “their” evaluation. From the perspective of “present reality,” as I call it, the normal life part can bear witness to the parts’ past reality and often feel empathy for their still being “there.”

在主观上感觉像是不安全、充满敌意的环境中，任何个体又怎能化解创伤经历呢？来访者和治疗师双方常常达成一种共谋，相信创伤可以通过处理那些事件来化解，即便此时那些批评性的声音仍在随意攻击着这个个体，使用着与施害者相同的话语或尖刻的语气。同样，双方都可能把“安全”等同于免于自伤或一个非施虐的家庭环境，正如我初次见到Annie时所做的那样。我们也许没有想到，如果来访者习惯性地与那些仍感到不受欢迎、害怕、羞耻或有自杀念头的孩子部分相融合，那么安全的“感受体验”很可能是无法企及的。尽管从客观视角看，外部环境如今或许是安全的，但与自身内隐记忆和各部分相融合的来访者，可能没有任何身体上或情感上的安全感来安抚各部分、让它们确信“它”已经结束了。在创伤能够被化解之前，来访者必须学会如何从各部分中解融合，以便两个大脑的现实都能被领会。从一种解融合的、双重觉察的视角出发，正常生活自我可以学会通过聚焦视觉注意来定向于当前的环境，可以正确地评估安全的程度，但同时又把各部分的恐惧和对危险的戒备感受为“它们的”评估。从我所称的“当下现实”的视角出发，正常生活部分可以见证各部分过去的现实，并常常对它们仍“滞留在那里”而生出共情。

Learning to Unblend

学习解融合

Because it takes practice to detect when one is blended, often the therapist becomes the observer who notices blending: “Hmmm, I can see you’re really blended with the ashamed part today,” “It’s hard not to blend with that anxious part—you’re so used to ‘insta-blending’ with her.” “Insta-blending” is the term I use to describe procedurally learned habits of automatically blending with whichever parts have the strongest feelings, often so quickly that mindful noticing doesn’t catch it. To be able to identify these patterns as they happen, it is important for clients to have a language for what is happening to them that is non-interpretive and non-pathologizing and helps them to notice potentially problematic conditioned learning. Clients also benefit from “unblending protocols,” skills and steps for what to do after they’ve noticed that they are blended. (See Appendix A for a model of unblending protocol.)

由于要察觉自己何时处于融合状态需要练习，治疗师往往成为那个注意到融合发生的观察者：“嗯，我能看出你今天真的和那个羞耻部分融合在一起了，”“很难不与那个焦虑部分融合——你太习惯于与她‘即时融合’了。”“即时融合”是我用来描述这样一种程序性习得的习惯：自动地与拥有最强烈感受的那个部分相融合，往往快到正念觉察都来不及捕捉。为了能够在这些模式发生时识别出它们，来访者拥有一种语言来描述正在发生在他们身上的事情就显得很重要，这种语言是非诠释性、非病理化的，并能帮助他们注意到那些可能存在问题的条件化学习。来访者也能从“解融合方案”中获益，那是关于他们在注意到自己处于融合状态后该做什么的技能与步骤。（解融合方案的一个范例参见附录A。）

Suzanne came into her session with the guarded facial expression I had learned to expect as the expression connected to her hypervigilant part. This “bodyguard” part was always primed for disappointment or betrayal, both of which had been daily experiences in Suzanne’s childhood. “I can’t do it,” she said. “I can’t do it. I can’t do what you and Dr. G. want from me.” (Both her primary therapist and I had been trying to help her learn to unblend, but she was more often blended than not.) “Why do you keep trying to make me do something I can’t do?!” As she spoke, her voice got more emotional and higher-pitched, a sign to me that this was a part speaking.

Suzanne走进治疗室时，脸上带着那种戒备的表情，我已学会预料到这是与她那个高度警惕部分相连的表情。这个“保镖”部分总是为失望或背叛而严阵以待，而这两者都曾是Suzanne童年时期日复一日的经历。“我做不到，”她说。“我做不到。我做不到你和G医生想要我做的事。”（她的主要治疗师和我都一直在试图帮助她学会解融合，但她处于融合状态的时候多过没有融合的时候。）“你们为什么一直想逼我去做一件我做不到的事？！”随着她说话，她的声音变得更加情绪化、音调更高，这对我而言是一个标志，表明此刻说话的是一个部分。

ME: “Notice the part that keeps saying, ‘I can’t—I can’t!’ Can you separate from that part a little bit? See if you can feel her without ‘becoming’ her … so she knows you’re hearing her.”

我：“注意那个一直在说‘我做不到——我做不到！’的部分。你能稍微和那个部分分离一点吗？看看你能不能在不‘变成’她的情况下感受到她……好让她知道你正在倾听她。”

SUZANNE: “No, I told you, I can’t do that stuff you guys want me to do!”

SUZANNE：“不，我告诉过你了，我做不到你们想要我做的那些事！”

ME: “Suzanne, I know it’s hard to learn to unblend when you’re so used to thinking of all these parts as ‘just you.’ But would you be willing to try something? [She nods.] See what happens if you say, ‘She is afraid I can’t do it—she’s afraid.’ What happens?”

我：“Suzanne，我知道，当你如此习惯于把所有这些部分都当成‘就是你’时，学习解融合是很难的。但你愿意尝试一下吗？〔她点头。〕看看如果你说‘她害怕我做不到——她害怕’会发生什么。会发生什么？”

SUZANNE: “It’s not quite as intense.”

SUZANNE：“没那么强烈了。”

ME: “Yes, it’s not as intense when she hears you say, ‘She’s afraid.’ Can you keep saying ‘she’?”

我：“是的，当她听到你说‘她害怕’时，就没那么强烈了。你能继续说‘她’吗？”

SUZANNE: “OK.”

SUZANNE：“好的。”

ME: “Ask her: what is she worried about if she can’t ‘do it’?”

我：“问问她：如果她‘做不到’，她在担心什么？”

SUZANNE: [pauses, appearing to be listening inside.] “She’s afraid you’ll give up on her and then you won’t help her.”

SUZANNE：〔停顿，似乎在向内倾听。〕“她害怕你会放弃她，然后就不再帮助她了。”

ME: “Of course, she’s worried about that! Because she had a mother whose motto was ‘my way or the highway,’ that’s always a worry for her, isn’t it?”

我：“她当然会担心那个！因为她有一个座右铭是‘要么听我的，要么滚蛋’的母亲，这对她来说一直是个担忧，不是吗？”

SUZANNE: “But it feels like my worry, too. Can I tell you the problem I have with this unblending thing? When I try to do it, when I step back from the parts’ feelings, then I can’t feel them at all. I just feel numb. Either I feel all of it, or I can’t feel it at all.”

SUZANNE：“但这也感觉像是我的担忧。我能告诉你我对这个解融合的事有什么困扰吗？当我试着去做的时候，当我从各部分的感受中退后一步时，我就完全感受不到它们了。我只感到麻木。我要么全部都感受到，要么就一点也感受不到。”

ME: “Well, that is a problem, isn’t it?” [Note that I validate her issues with unblending as normal and natural, trying to imply that my standards are different from her mother’s.] “If the little part hadn’t been so worried that we would reject and abandon her, maybe you’d have been able to tell us about this problem. And it’s such a common one.” [I now shift to some psychoeducation for her normal life self as a context for understanding her difficulties in unblending.] “Many trauma survivors find it hard to have what we call ‘dual awareness’: awareness of your being and awareness of the parts’ feelings at the same time. But let’s see if I can teach you some ways of unblending that might help with that. Would you be willing to try five steps to unblending?” [I deliberately chose a very structured approach, broken down into concrete small steps, to make the learning easier than she was primed to expect.]

我：“嗯，那确实是个问题，不是吗？”〔请注意，我把她在解融合上遇到的问题确认为正常且自然的，试图暗示我的标准与她母亲的不同。〕“如果那个小部分没有那么担心我们会拒绝和抛弃她，也许你早就能把这个问题告诉我们了。而这是一个如此常见的问题。”〔现在我转向为她的正常生活自我提供一些心理教育，作为理解她在解融合上的困难的背景。〕“许多创伤幸存者发现，要拥有我们所说的‘双重觉察’是很难的：同时觉察到你自己的存在和各部分的感受。不过，让我们看看我能不能教你一些可能对此有帮助的解融合方法。你愿意尝试解融合的五个步骤吗？”〔我刻意选择了一种非常结构化、被拆解为具体小步骤的方法，好让这种学习比她预设会遇到的更容易。〕

SUZANNE: “OK.”

SUZANNE：“好的。”

ME: “First, notice the feeling of ‘I can’t’—can you still feel it?”

我：“首先，注意那种‘我做不到’的感受——你还能感受到它吗？”

SUZANNE: “Yes, I can—it’s not as intense, but it’s still there.”

SUZANNE：“是的，我能——没那么强烈了，但它还在那里。”

ME: “Start by noticing the feeling and repeating again, ‘She’s afraid I can’t do it.’ That’s Step One.”

我：“先从注意那种感受开始，再重复一遍，‘她害怕我做不到。’这就是第一步。”

SUZANNE: “Say it out loud or to myself?”

SUZANNE：“大声说出来，还是对自己默念？”

ME: “Whichever feels more comfortable for you. If you are feeling distress, just assume that it belongs to a part and say to yourself or out loud, ‘She’s afraid … or ‘She’s upset.’” [I give her a minute or two to try out this new language with herself.] “Is that better or worse?”

我：“哪一种让你感觉更舒服都行。如果你感到痛苦，就假定它属于某个部分，对自己或大声说，‘她害怕……’或‘她心烦。’”〔我给她一两分钟，让她对自己尝试这种新的语言。〕“这样是好些了还是更糟了？”

SUZANNE: “Better.”

SUZANNE：“好些了。”

ME: “Ready for Step Two?”

我：“准备好进行第二步了吗？”

SUZANNE: “OK.”

SUZANNE：“好的。”

ME: “Here’s Step Two: engage your core—slightly tense the muscles in your core so she can feel your presence. … Can you still feel her?” [Remembering that Suzanne had been an athlete as a child, I chose to try out using the body as a resource here.]

我：“这是第二步：调动你的核心——稍微绷紧你核心部位的肌肉，好让她能感受到你的存在。……你还能感受到她吗？”〔记起Suzanne小时候曾是一名运动员，我在这里选择尝试把身体当作一种资源来使用。〕

SUZANNE: “I can!”

SUZANNE：“我能！”

ME: “Great! Now ask her if she can feel you.”

我：“太好了！现在问问她，她能不能感受到你。”

SUZANNE: “She can.”

SUZANNE：“她能。”

ME: “Wonderful! You can both feel each other! Great work! Hooray!! Does she like that?”

我：“太棒了！你们俩都能感受到彼此！干得好！万岁！！她喜欢这样吗？”

SUZANNE: “She does. She’s telling me that’s why the parts are so freaked out all the time—because they don’t think anyone hear them.”

SUZANNE：“她喜欢。她在告诉我，这就是为什么那些部分总是这么惊慌失措——因为它们觉得没有人听见它们。”

ME: “A good reason for us to work on this, huh? Now you are both ready for Step Three! OK, now lengthen your spine a little bit from your lower back up—like you’re putting space between the vertebrae … Ask her if she can feel how tall you are now …”

我：“这正是我们着手处理这件事的一个好理由，是吧？现在你们俩都准备好进行第三步了！好，现在把你的脊柱从下背部往上稍微拉长一点——就像你在椎骨之间腾出空间……问问她，她现在能不能感受到你有多高……”

SUZANNE: “She’s surprised—she didn’t know I was that tall.”

SUZANNE：“她很惊讶——她不知道我这么高。”

ME: “Good work! Now you can feel her, and she can feel you, and you’re talking! Does she like that?”

我：“干得好！现在你能感受到她，她也能感受到你，而且你们在交谈！她喜欢这样吗？”

SUZANNE: “Yes, she likes it a lot. And I like it because I hate it when I try to unblend and then I can’t feel the part at all.”

SUZANNE：“是的，她非常喜欢。我也喜欢，因为我讨厌每当我试图解融合时却完全感受不到那个部分。”

ME: “Ask her if it feels good to hear you say that you want to be able to feel her with you.”

我：“问问她，听到你说你希望能够感受到她和你在一起，感觉好不好。”

SUZANNE: “She likes that, but she says she’s still worried that I won’t get it, and you and Dr. G. won’t like her …”

SUZANNE：“她喜欢，但她说她还是担心我学不会，还有你和G医生会不喜欢她……”

ME: “Good, I’m glad she can tell you her worries. That’s a good thing. Just be careful not to blend with her worries. Let’s practice Step 4: connect to your department supervisor role for a moment. Imagine that this part is coming to you as her supervisor, and she’s worried that she’s not getting things fast enough and that she’ll be fired. What would you tell her?”

我：“很好，我很高兴她能把她的担忧告诉你。这是件好事。只是要小心不要与她的担忧融合。让我们来练习第四步：暂时连接到你作为部门主管的角色。想象这个部分作为她的主管前来找你，她担心自己学东西不够快、会被解雇。你会对她说什么？”

SUZANNE: [Thinks for a moment.] “I’d tell her not to worry—to just keep trying to learn the job and trust that if she keeps trying, she will get it.”

SUZANNE：〔想了一会儿。〕“我会告诉她别担心——只要继续努力学习这份工作，并相信只要她不断尝试，就一定能学会。”

ME: “Wonderful advice, Suzanne—your staff are lucky to have such a wise, compassionate supervisor—now to Step 5: ask her if it helps her to hear that or if she still needs something else.”

我：“多好的建议，Suzanne——你的员工有这样一位睿智、富有慈悲心的主管真是幸运——现在到第五步：问问她，听到这些对她有没有帮助，或者她是否还需要别的什么。”

SUZANNE: “She says it does help, she wants me to keep saying it because she needs to hear it over and over …”

SUZANNE：“她说这确实有帮助，她想要我一直说下去，因为她需要一遍又一遍地听到它……”

ME: “Good point! She hasn’t heard it often enough in her life to believe it and take it in. Do you think you could remember to keep telling her that she’ll get it if she keeps trying? Would it help if you put it on your calendar and asked your phone to remind you? I know you keep track of your kids’ schedules that way, and this part is a kid, too.”

我：“说得好！她这辈子听到这话的次数还不够多，不足以相信它、把它吸收进去。你觉得你能记得一直告诉她，只要她不断尝试就会学会吗？如果你把它写进日历、让你的手机提醒你，会有帮助吗？我知道你就是这样记录你孩子们的日程的，而这个部分也是个孩子。”

SUZANNE: [now clearly in her going on with normal life part] “I’ll put her in my schedule, but it would also help if you wrote down those five steps for me so I can remember and practice.” [This request told me that her normal life part was more present than I had seen before: she could think about how to achieve a goal that involved the parts.]

SUZANNE：〔此刻显然处在她的正常生活部分中〕“我会把她安排进我的日程，但如果你能把那五个步骤写下来给我，让我可以记住并练习，也会有帮助。”〔这一请求告诉我，她的正常生活部分比我以前所见到的更为在场：她能够思考如何实现一个涉及各部分的目标。〕

ME: [Talking as I write down the five steps we just practiced.]”That would definitely reassure her, Suzanne! And now, before we end today, let’s thank her for telling you she was so worried! That was important. How else would you know? Maybe we can also schedule a time for her or other parts to tell you about their worries … Let’s be thinking about that.”

我：〔我一边把我们刚刚练习过的五个步骤写下来，一边说。〕“那一定会让她安心，Suzanne！现在，在我们今天结束之前，让我们感谢她告诉你她是多么担心！那很重要。否则你怎么会知道呢？也许我们还可以安排一个时间，让她或其他部分向你讲述它们的担忧……让我们好好想想这件事。”

Less afraid to connect to her young self because she was provided with a set of structured steps and not expected to connect to “too much” vulnerability too soon, Suzanne was able to not only unblend but also begin a dialogue with the little part who had been so upset. Like Suzanne, clients often spontaneously experience compassion for their parts once they are no longer blended with them: “I feel really sad for her—I want to just pick her up and hold her.” Here, the therapist might feel tempted to stay with the emotion of sadness rather than continue with the parts focus. But the therapist’s role at this point is keep the client in a mindful state and centered on the child: “What is it like for her to feel your sadness for her? To feel that someone actually cares how she feels?” “See what happens if you reach out to her and just offer your hand.” As clients imagine reaching out to their younger selves or even make a reaching out gesture, a technique drawn from Sensorimotor Psychotherapy (Ogden & Fisher, 2015), their internal state usually transforms: they feel a sense of warmth; their bodies relax; they feel calmer. To ensure that these positive internal states are more than momentary, the therapist must continue focusing on “what happens inside” to deepen the felt connection to the child part and increase closeness and compassion.

由于得到了一套结构化的步骤，又没有被期望过早地连接到“太多”的脆弱，Suzanne不再那么害怕连接她那个年幼的自我，于是她不仅能够解融合，还能够与那个一直如此心烦的小部分开始一场对话。和Suzanne一样，来访者一旦不再与他们的各部分相融合，往往会自发地体验到对这些部分的慈悲：“我真的为她感到难过——我只想把她抱起来搂在怀里。”在这里，治疗师可能会忍不住想停留在悲伤这一情绪上，而不是继续聚焦于各部分。但此刻治疗师的角色是让来访者保持在一种正念状态中，并以那个孩子为中心：“当她感受到你为她而生的悲伤时，那是什么感觉？感受到真的有人在乎她的感受，是什么感觉？”“看看如果你向她伸出手、只是把手递给她，会发生什么。”当来访者想象着向他们更年幼的自我伸出手，甚至做出一个伸手的姿态时——这是一种取自感觉运动心理治疗（Ogden和Fisher，2015）的技术——他们的内部状态通常会发生转变：他们感到一种温暖；他们的身体放松下来；他们感到更加平静。为了确保这些积极的内部状态不只是转瞬即逝的，治疗师必须继续聚焦于“内在发生了什么”，以加深与孩子部分的感受连接，并增进亲近感与慈悲。

Sometimes clients become expert at saying all the right words to their parts but without an embodied connection to the words or the part. It is very important that the therapist ask clients to linger on the changes in emotion or sensation that occur when the normal life self says or does something nourishing or comforting for the parts. He or she can ask, “What changes in her feelings when she senses you are really ‘there’?” Or ask her, “How can she feel that you are sincere? What tells her that?” Sometimes, the therapist has to interweave psychoeducation with the parts work, as I did with Suzanne above, to make the point that change will come only through repetition of new patterns: “The more you hold her, the safer she will feel, and then the calmer you will feel. She can’t let you feel calm and centered if she’s terrified.”

有时来访者会变得很擅长对他们的各部分说出所有正确的话语，却没有与那些话语或那个部分建立起具身的连接。极为重要的是，治疗师要请来访者停留在当正常生活自我为各部分说出或做出某种滋养性或安慰性的事情时所发生的情绪或感觉的变化上。他或她可以问：“当她感觉到你真的‘在那里’时，她的感受发生了什么变化？”或者问她：“她怎样才能感觉到你是真诚的？是什么让她有这种感觉？”有时，治疗师必须把心理教育与各部分工作交织在一起，正如我上面对Suzanne所做的那样，以阐明这一点：只有通过新模式的反复重复，改变才会到来：“你越是搂着她，她就会感到越安全，然后你也会感到越平静。如果她惊恐万状，她就无法让你感到平静和安定。”

Sometimes, the child’s predicament has to be “translated” by the therapist to help the normal life self “get it”:

有时，孩子的困境必须由治疗师加以“翻译”，以帮助正常生活自我“领会”它：

“She’s saying that she likes you here with her, but she doesn’t trust it yet. … That makes sense, doesn’t it? Can you feel her holding back a little? It just means that she’s been through a lot—as she certainly has, right? And that makes it hard for her to trust. For her to believe that someone will really be there for her, you’ll need to keep on showing up, day after day after day, communicating that you care how she feels. That’s what she would need to truly believe you are there for good, and she can finally relax and feel safe.”

“她在说，她喜欢你在这里陪着她，但她还不信任它。……这是说得通的，不是吗？你能感觉到她有些退缩吗？这只是意味着她经历了很多——她确实如此，对吧？而这让她难以信任。要让她相信真的有人会陪在她身边，你需要持续地现身，日复一日，传达出你在乎她的感受。这正是她真正相信你会长久地在她身边、从而终于能够放松下来、感到安全所需要的。”

Providing Hospitality

提供款待

In the late 1980s and early 1990s, when early experts in dissociation were trying to put words to what they observed in patients with “multiple personality disorder,” the term chosen for what we now know as the going on with normal life self was “host.” Although that label was meant to convey the sense of an empty vessel holding the traumatized parts, another meaning we could assign to the term “host” is that of homeowner and provider of hospitality. In fact, if the going on with normal life self is in charge of the body’s health and well-being, must provide food, shelter, and other necessities, and is focused on present moment priorities, it is quite literally the “host” or home base for all parts of the self. In addition, given its access to the medial prefrontal cortex, the going on with normal life self has the unique ability to see a wider perspective, to conceptualize, to reconcile opposites or at least keep them simultaneously in mind. The normal life self has the capacity to hold in dual awareness both past and present, part and whole, animal brain and thinking brain. However, when clients finally come for treatment, the going on with normal life self is often demoralized or depleted, identified with certain parts and intimidated by or ashamed of others. Although the normal life part has the innate ability to learn to observe them all, to decrease autonomic dysregulation, and to become interested in rather than afraid of the parts, he or she may need education to recognize them as young child selves trying to communicate their trauma-related fears and phobias.

在20世纪80年代末和90年代初，当解离领域的早期专家试图为他们在“多重人格障碍”患者身上所观察到的现象找到措辞时，他们为我们今天所知的正常生活自我所选用的术语是“宿主（host）”。尽管这个标签意在传达一个容纳着被创伤各部分的空容器之感，但我们也可以赋予“host”一词另一层含义：房屋的主人和款待的提供者。事实上，如果正常生活自我负责身体的健康与福祉，必须提供食物、住所及其他必需品，并专注于当下时刻的优先事务，那么它就名副其实地是自我所有部分的“主人”或大本营。此外，由于它能够接触到前额叶内侧皮层，正常生活自我具备一种独特的能力，能够看到更宽广的视角，进行概念化，调和对立面，或至少把它们同时放在心中。正常生活自我有能力在双重觉察中同时持有过去与现在、部分与整体、动物脑与思考脑。然而，当来访者最终前来治疗时，正常生活自我往往士气低落或精疲力竭，认同某些部分，又被另一些部分所恐吓或为之感到羞耻。尽管正常生活部分天生就有能力去学习观察所有这些部分、减轻自主神经的失调，并对各部分产生兴趣而非恐惧，但他或她可能需要受到一些教育，才能将它们辨认为试图传达其创伤相关恐惧和恐惧症的年幼孩子自我。

Welcoming the Lost Souls and Traumatized Children

欢迎失落的灵魂和受创的孩子

But why should the normal life self become a warm and welcoming host to trauma-related parts when they are turning his or her daily life upside down? Just as therapists have to make a convincing case that connection to emotion or remembering the past or practicing skills is of benefit to clients, it is our job to make the link between the hopes and dreams that brought the client to therapy and the ability to recognize and befriend the parts. Think about what the client is seeking from therapy: what is the wish that drove her to your door? What is he hoping for as a result of treatment? Why is he or she here? Is this client seeking relief or self-actualization? Trying to stay alive or trying to make meaning of his or her experience?

但是，当那些创伤相关的部分正把他或她的日常生活搅得天翻地覆时，正常生活自我又为何要成为它们一位温暖而热情的主人呢？正如治疗师必须令人信服地论证连接情绪、回忆过去或练习技能对来访者是有益的一样，把那些促使来访者前来治疗的希望与梦想，同辨认并与各部分为友的能力联系起来，正是我们的职责所在。想一想来访者从治疗中寻求的是什么：是什么样的愿望把她带到了你的门前？他希望治疗带来什么结果？他或她为什么会在这里？这位来访者寻求的是缓解，还是自我实现？是在努力活下去，还是在努力为他或她的经历建构意义？

Notice that each explanation I give to a client is generally positive, normalizing, and speaks to the “best self” of the client:

请注意，我给来访者的每一个解释通常都是积极的、正常化的，并且诉诸来访者的“最佳自我”：

“I know you wish the parts would just go away, but would that be fair? To neglect them the way you were neglected? I don’t believe you’re that kind of person. The person I know you to be would never reject wounded children because they were upset or inconvenient.”
     “Think of the parts as your roommates—you all share the same body, the same home. You have a choice: you can learn to accept each other and get along, or you can struggle to win every battle!”
     “We wouldn’t be sitting here today if it weren’t for your parts. By taking on the survive-at-all-costs role, they allowed you to leave home, go to college, and start a life far away from the world of your childhood. It’s only fair to take them with you to this better, safer world—it could be a way to thank them. It’s not much of a thanks to leave them ‘there’ while you go forward.”
     “Rightly or wrongly, you and the parts are inseparable: as long as their distress becomes your distress. For you to live a life freer of fear, anger, and shame, the parts need to welcomed—they have to feel safe.”

“我知道你希望那些部分干脆消失，但那样公平吗？像你曾经被忽视那样去忽视它们？我不相信你是那种人。我所认识的那个你，绝不会因为受伤的孩子心烦或带来不便就拒绝他们。”
“把那些部分想象成你的室友——你们大家共享同一个身体、同一个家。你有一个选择：你可以学着彼此接纳、好好相处，也可以挣扎着去赢得每一场争斗！”
“要不是因为你的那些部分，我们今天就不会坐在这里。它们承担起了不惜一切代价求生存的角色，让你得以离开家、上大学，并在远离你童年世界的地方开始新的生活。把它们带上，一同前往这个更美好、更安全的世界，才是公平的——这可以是感谢它们的一种方式。在你向前走的时候把它们留在‘那里’，可算不上什么感谢。”
“无论对错，你和这些部分都密不可分：只要它们的痛苦会变成你的痛苦。为了让你过上一种更少恐惧、愤怒和羞耻的生活，这些部分需要被欢迎——它们必须感到安全。”

Notice that the meaning-making of the therapist challenges the going on with normal life self while at the same time expressing support for the parts. Each statement or question communicates that the therapist will be an advocate for the young, vulnerable, and traumatized. There is no emphasis on discovering what happened in the past: the focus is on the relationship between parts and normal life self now, what is happening between them in the present moment. There is an implicit assumption that they are driven by the experiences of the past, by painful body and emotional memories. That can be acknowledged, but no effort is made to connect the parts’ reactions now to specific events then. When clients spontaneously associate a part with particular image or event, the therapist reframes the intrusion of memory as the part’s way of communicating why it is afraid or ashamed or angry.

请注意，治疗师的意义建构既挑战着正常生活自我，同时又表达着对各部分的支持。每一句陈述或每一个问题都传达出，治疗师将会成为那些年幼、脆弱、受创者的倡导者。这里并不强调去发掘过去发生了什么：焦点在于此刻各部分与正常生活自我之间的关系，在于此时此刻它们之间正在发生着什么。这里有一个隐含的假设：它们是被过去的经历、被痛苦的身体记忆和情感记忆所驱动的。这一点可以被承认，但并不刻意把各部分此刻的反应与彼时的具体事件联系起来。当来访者自发地把某个部分与特定的画面或事件联系起来时，治疗师便把记忆的侵入重构为那个部分用以传达它为何害怕、羞耻或愤怒的方式。

“When we talk about the part that fears abandonment, that same image always come up, doesn’t it? That image of your mother driving away angrily, and this little part of you running down the street after her … I wonder if that little girl is trying to ask you, ‘Will you get overwhelmed and run away from me, too?’”
     “If that image were a message from some young part of you, what would he be trying to tell you? Would he be saying, ‘Yes, that’s why I’m so scared all the time’? Or ‘Help me!’ or “Don’t let anyone hurt me.’ It’s important to know, isn’t it? Otherwise he’s going to have to keep giving you disturbing pictures to make his point.”

“当我们谈到那个害怕被抛弃的部分时，总会浮现出同一个画面，不是吗？那个你母亲愤怒地开车离去、而你这个小小的部分沿着街道追在她身后奔跑的画面……我在想，那个小女孩是不是在试图问你，‘你也会不堪重负、从我身边逃走吗？’”
“如果那个画面是来自你某个年幼部分的一则讯息，他会试图告诉你什么呢？他会不会在说，‘是的，那就是我为什么一直这么害怕’？或者‘救救我！’或者‘别让任何人伤害我。’知道这一点很重要，不是吗？否则他就只能不断地给你那些令人不安的画面来表明他的意思。”

Initially, therapy sessions must be used as the opportunity to practice these new habits of noticing the parts, naming what is noticed, and unblending. But practice requires a therapist who will consistently ask: “‘Who’ is speaking right now? What parts are reacting to the conversation we’re having? What part is having this strong emotional reaction?” As noted in Chapter 4, most human beings are in the habit of assuming that all thoughts, feelings, and physical reactions are “mine,” that what “I” am feeling represents “my” emotions. It takes week-after-week practice to help clients relinquish that automatic assumption and learn instead to assume that anything they might be feeling or thinking could be an expression of any one of many parts.

起初，治疗会谈必须被用作练习这些新习惯的机会：注意各部分、为所注意到的命名，以及解融合。但这种练习需要一位会持续发问的治疗师：“此刻是‘谁’在说话？哪些部分正在对我们正进行的这场对话作出反应？是哪个部分有着这种强烈的情绪反应？”正如第4章所指出的，大多数人都习惯于假设所有的想法、感受和身体反应都是“我的”，假设“我”所感受到的代表着“我的”情绪。需要一周又一周的练习，才能帮助来访者放弃那种自动的假设，转而学会去假设：他们可能正在感受或思考的任何东西，都可能是众多部分中某一个的表达。

To help individuals reclaim their fragmented, disowned, alienated parts, the therapist must be relentless and persistent in using the language of parts in therapy and asking the client to use it, too: “What happens when you say, ‘She is feeling ashamed’? Do the feelings get more intense or less?” Each time I invite clients to name what they feel as “his” or “her” feelings, they notice a slight relaxing or relief—as if by naming the emotion as “his” emotion conveys to the part a sense of being heard or being understood.

为了帮助个体重新认领他们碎裂、被否认、被疏离的各部分，治疗师必须坚持不懈、锲而不舍地在治疗中使用各部分的语言，并要求来访者也使用它：“当你说‘她正感到羞耻’时，会发生什么？那些感受是变得更强烈了，还是减弱了？”每当我邀请来访者把他们的感受命名为“他的”或“她的”感受时，他们都会注意到一种轻微的放松或宽慰——仿佛把那种情绪命名为“他的”情绪，便向那个部分传达了一种被听见或被理解之感。

Most clients have evolved a procedurally learned habitual strategy for dealing with the intrusive or underlying communications from parts. Some try to control the intrusive feelings and impulses, ignore the tears or self-denigrating thoughts or voices. Others interpret each feeling, impulse, or belief as “my feeling” or “how I feel,” forgetting that they may have felt differently even seconds before. The former strategy yields a more emotionally cut-off, controlled way of being that interferes with the enjoyment of life. The latter leads to chaos or a feeling of being overwhelmed, out of control, crazy, on the verge of implosion or explosion. Not only do these patterns need to be noticed and translated into parts language, but it is also important that the therapy emphasize strengthening the normal life self and enhancing the qualities associated with “self” or “self energy” in the Internal Family Systems approach (Schwartz, 2001). The normal life self must develop the capacities of “wise mind”: staying connected to present time, capable of meta-awareness or the capacity to hover above, seeing all of the parts, and the ability to make decisions for the sake of the whole. The concept of ‘self’ in Internal Family Systems helps clients connect to states of compassion, creativity, curiosity, and perspective, while the going on with normal life self of the Structural Dissociation model emphasizes the importance of developing the functional ability to take action to implement decisions for the sake of the system. If we put the two models together and encourage the development of wise mind or “self energy” in the going on with normal life self, then we have leadership informed by clarity of view, compassionate acceptance, and the capacity for behavior change. The challenge is how to access a going on with normal life part and convince that aspect of self to not only assume a leadership role but also cultivate the qualities of self: curiosity, compassion, clarity, calm, creativity, courage, commitment, and connection.

大多数来访者都已演化出一种程序性习得的惯常策略，用来应对来自各部分的侵入性或潜在的传达。有些人试图控制那些侵入性的感受和冲动，无视眼泪或自我贬低的想法或声音。另一些人则把每一种感受、冲动或信念都诠释为“我的感受”或“我的感觉”，忘记了哪怕就在几秒钟之前他们的感受可能还截然不同。前一种策略产生出一种情感上更为隔绝、受控的存在方式，妨碍了对生活的享受。后一种则导致混乱，或一种不堪重负、失控、发疯、濒临内爆或外爆的感觉。这些模式不仅需要被注意到并被翻译成各部分的语言，治疗强调强化正常生活自我、增进内部家庭系统方法中与“自我”或“自我能量”相关的品质（Schwartz，2001）也同样重要。正常生活自我必须发展出“智慧心智”的能力：与当下时刻保持连接，能够进行元觉察或拥有凌驾其上、看见所有部分的能力，以及为了整体的利益作出决定的能力。内部家庭系统中“自我”的概念帮助来访者连接到慈悲、创造力、好奇和视角的状态，而结构性解离模型的正常生活自我则强调发展出采取行动、为了系统的利益执行决定的功能性能力的重要性。如果我们把这两个模型结合起来，并鼓励在正常生活自我中发展智慧心智或“自我能量”，那么我们便拥有了一种由清明的视野、慈悲的接纳以及行为改变的能力所引导的领导力。挑战在于，如何接触到一个正常生活部分，并说服自我的这一方面不仅承担起领导角色，还要培养自我的那些品质：好奇、慈悲、清明、平静、创造力、勇气、承诺和连接。

Forming a Connection to a Wise Compassionate Adult

与一个睿智而充满慈悲的成年人建立连接

Because the actions and reactions of the parts are driven by autonomic activation and animal defense survival responses, it is common for survivors of trauma to “own” and become identified with some parts just as they disown other parts. Some identify with their normal life selves, as did Carla; some identify with their suicidal parts or angry parts; some identify with the attach part’s desperate proximity-seeking and end up “looking for love in all the wrong places.” Some identify with the submit part and become the caretakers even for family members who have abused them. But when clients identify with trauma-related parts or blend with them, they can quickly lose access to the prefrontal cortex and the normal life self. The intensity of the parts’ trauma responses tends to “drown out” a felt connection to the left brain self who can remember to grocery shop even though the freeze part is having panic attacks today. It is not surprising that the normal life self’s dogged persistence in making a normal life possible comes to be interpreted as “pretending” or “fraudulent.” It is counterintuitive to conceive of a part that thinks and acts rationally and functionally despite the overwhelming sensations and emotions as reflecting an authentic self, much less a self that embodies how the client survived the years of abuse, neglect, or captivity without losing the capacity or drive to “go on.”

由于各部分的行动与反应是被自主神经的激活和动物防御生存反应所驱动的，创伤幸存者“认领”并认同某些部分，同时又否认另一些部分，这是很常见的。有些人认同自己的正常生活自我，正如Carla那样；有些人认同自己的自杀部分或愤怒部分；有些人认同依恋部分那种绝望的寻求亲近，最终“在所有错误的地方寻找爱”。有些人认同顺从部分，甚至成为那些曾虐待过他们的家庭成员的照料者。但当来访者认同创伤相关部分或与它们融合时，他们会很快失去接触前额叶皮层和正常生活自我的途径。各部分创伤反应的强烈程度往往会“淹没”一种与左脑自我的感受连接——这个左脑自我即便在冻结部分今天惊恐发作时，仍能记得去采购杂货。正常生活自我为使正常生活成为可能而表现出的顽强坚持，到头来被诠释为“假装”或“欺骗”，这并不令人意外。把一个尽管面对压倒性的感觉和情绪却依然理性、有功能地思考和行动的部分构想为一个真实自我的反映，是有违直觉的，更不用说把它构想为一个体现了来访者如何在经年累月的虐待、忽视或囚禁中存活下来、却未曾丧失“继续下去”的能力或动力的自我了。

Many clients immediately reject the concept of having a normal life self: “There’s no adult here—I don’t even like adults,” said the law student who came to see me for a consultation, promoting the agenda of the little part that needed care because she was “home alone” with no one. “I used to function,” said an artist. “I used to have a life but no more. I couldn’t handle things—I couldn’t function in so much pain.” After a romantic breakup, her depressed part became intensely hopeless and encouraged by friends and therapist to see it as grief, the artist blended with the depressed part, making it harder and harder to function.

许多来访者立刻就拒绝“拥有一个正常生活自我”这一概念：“这里没有成年人——我甚至不喜欢成年人，”那位前来找我咨询的法学院学生说，她在推动那个需要被照顾的小部分的诉求，因为她“独自在家”、身边没有任何人。“我以前是有功能的，”一位艺术家说。“我以前有自己的生活，但现在没有了。我应付不了那些事情——在如此巨大的痛苦中我无法运作。”在一段恋情破裂之后，她的抑郁部分变得极度绝望，又被朋友和治疗师鼓励把它视为哀伤，于是这位艺术家与抑郁部分融合在一起，使得运作变得越来越困难。

I asked the artist, “Do you remember what it was like when you could function, when you had a life?”

我问那位艺术家：“你还记得当你能够正常运作、当你拥有自己生活的时候是什么样子吗？”

CLIENT: “I do—I had so many interests and things I loved to do.” [Her face lit up.]

来访者：“我记得——我有那么多兴趣，那么多我喜欢做的事情。”〔她的脸上焕发出光彩。〕

ME: “When you remember those days, what happens in your body?”

我：“当你回忆起那些日子时，你的身体里发生了什么？”

CLIENT: “I feel more energy—more hope—then I think, ‘Who am I kidding? There’s no hope for me.’”

来访者：“我感到更有能量——更有希望——然后我又想，‘我在骗谁呢？我没有希望了。’”

ME: “That’s what the depressed part keeps telling you! And you believe her which doesn’t help either of you. Would you have done that with your students in art class? If one of them told you the same thing, would you have agreed with her?”

我：“那正是那个抑郁部分一直在告诉你的！而你相信了她，这对你们俩都没有帮助。你会在美术课上对你的学生那样做吗？如果他们当中有一个对你说同样的话，你会同意她的说法吗？”

CLIENT: “No, of course not!” [Getting a bit irritated with me. But from a body perspective, irritation is an antidote to depression, so I am encouraged, not discouraged, by the irritation.]

来访者：“不，当然不会！”〔对我有点恼火。但从身体的角度看，恼火是抑郁的一剂解药，所以这种恼火让我受到鼓舞，而非气馁。〕

ME: “What would you tell your student?”

我：“你会对你的学生说什么？”

CLIENT: “I’d tell her, ‘Do what you love—that’s all you have to do. Hope will follow from that, not the other way around.’”

来访者：“我会告诉她，‘去做你热爱的事——你需要做的就这些。希望会随之而来，而不是反过来。’”

ME: “Right, she doesn’t need hope to follow her heart! Good point. And if she does, she will feel more hopeful. See what happens if you tell that to the depressed part …”

我：“没错，她并不需要先有希望才能追随自己的内心！说得好。而且如果她追随了，她就会感到更有希望。看看如果你把这话告诉那个抑郁部分，会发生什么……”

Here, I could challenge the over-identification with the depressed part by accessing her normal life part’s career experience and inherent compassion. The left-brain wisdom available to her right brain depressed part was still there in her normal life self. Sometimes, when clients are as adamant as the law student that they would never have a normal life or adult part, I challenge them with a simple biological fact: “Unless you have had a brain injury you forgot to mention, or brain surgery, the normal life self is still alive and well in your prefrontal cortex [I tap my forehead to demonstrate where to find that part of the personality]—it’s still there even if you haven’t been able to function for years.” Or as I said to the artist, “I’m happy to tell you that the brain is like the Library of Congress—it doesn’t lose information. If you have ever had even a day or hour of curiosity or clarity of mind or confidence, those abilities are still within you. You’ve just lost access to them because the depressed part hijacked you just trying to get you to see how upset she was.” By stressing that parts blend, hijack the body, intrude flashbacks and images in order to get the attention and help of the normal life self, I can often spontaneously evoke empathy for the part: “Really?! You mean she only brought me so low because she wanted me to see how much pain she was in? Because she wanted help?!”

在这里，我可以通过接触她正常生活部分的职业经验和内在的慈悲，来挑战她对抑郁部分的过度认同。她那个右脑抑郁部分所能获得的左脑智慧，仍然存在于她的正常生活自我之中。有时，当来访者像那位法学院学生一样固执地坚称自己绝不会有一个正常生活部分或成年部分时，我会用一个简单的生物学事实来挑战他们：“除非你有过某次你忘了提及的脑损伤，或者做过脑部手术，否则正常生活自我仍然好端端地活在你的前额叶皮层里〔我轻敲自己的前额，以示意到哪里去找人格的那个部分〕——即便你已经好几年无法正常运作，它依然在那里。”或者，正如我对那位艺术家所说的：“我很高兴地告诉你，大脑就像国会图书馆——它不会丢失信息。如果你哪怕曾经有过一天或一个小时的好奇、心智的清明或信心，那些能力仍然在你之内。你只是失去了接触它们的途径，因为那个抑郁部分劫持了你，只是为了让你看到她有多么难过。”通过强调各部分会融合、劫持身体、侵入闪回和画面，以便获得正常生活自我的关注和帮助，我常常能够自发地唤起对那个部分的共情：“真的吗？！你是说她把我拖到如此低谷，只是因为她想让我看到她身处多么巨大的痛苦之中？因为她想要帮助？！”

Connecting to the Resources of a Competent Adult

连接到一个胜任的成年人的资源

The easiest, most direct route to the normal life self is through those activities and life tasks or experiences with which this part is or was once identified. Is the client a parent? An administrator? Teacher? Attorney? Health care professional? Is there a hobby or cause that has meaning? If functioning has been problematic for an individual, we can ask: What “normal life” roles did he or she play earlier? Is there a wish or dream for a normal life that is important to the client? Does he or she play any role that requires prefrontal cortical activity? In using this model with young adults from inpatient and residential settings in a regional mental health system, we repeatedly described the normal life part to patients who, because of their abuse and neglect, had never functioned in normal life, even as children. Nonetheless, when our team from the Connecticut Department of Mental Health Young Adult Services described the Structural Dissociation model to these clients, almost all identified with having anormal life self: “That’s the part of me that wants out of this hospital!” “Yes, that’s the part that wants to be normal, not a mental patient.” “That’s the ‘me’ who wants to go to college and get a job.” “I recognize my normal life part: she’s the one who wants to get married, live in a real home, and have kids.” Others could begin to feel a more palpable connection to their normal life selves once they began to associate their symptoms with the different parts and could disidentify from them, that is, differentiate what “I” want from what the parts seemed to be seeking. Whatever past “normal life” experience or vision of a future a client may have, it can become the vehicle for developing a stronger felt sense of having an adult body and mind (Ogden et al., 2006). I have helped clients find a normal life part on inpatient units (the part that organized a ping-pong tournament for her peers), in activities such as jewelry-making, playing tennis, horseback riding, volunteer work with animals or children, even in serving as the voice of wisdom and support for others in their lives.

通往正常生活自我最容易、最直接的途径，是通过那些这个部分如今认同、或曾经认同的活动、生活任务或经历。来访者是父母吗？是管理者？教师？律师？医疗专业人员？有没有某种具有意义的爱好或事业？如果一个个体的功能运作一直存在问题，我们可以问：他或她早先扮演过哪些“正常生活”的角色？是否有某个对来访者很重要的、关于正常生活的愿望或梦想？他或她是否扮演任何需要前额叶皮层活动的角色？在一个地区性心理健康系统中，对来自住院和居住式机构的年轻成年人使用这一模型时，我们反复向那些由于受到虐待和忽视、即便在儿童时期也从未在正常生活中运作过的患者描述正常生活部分。尽管如此，当我们来自康涅狄格州心理健康部青年成人服务处的团队向这些来访者描述结构性解离模型时，几乎所有人都认同自己拥有一个正常生活自我：“那就是我那个想离开这家医院的部分！”“对，那就是那个想做一个正常人、而不是一个精神病人的部分。”“那就是那个想上大学、找份工作的‘我’。”“我认得我的正常生活部分：她就是那个想要结婚、住在一个真正的家里、生养孩子的人。”另一些人，一旦开始把自己的症状与不同的部分联系起来，并能够与它们去认同——也就是把“我”想要的与各部分似乎在寻求的区分开来——便能够开始对自己的正常生活自我感受到一种更为清晰可感的连接。无论来访者可能拥有怎样过去的“正常生活”经历或对未来的憧憬，它都可以成为发展出一种更强的、拥有成年身体与心智的感受体验的载体（Ogden等，2006）。我曾帮助来访者在住院病区找到一个正常生活部分（那个为同伴们组织乒乓球比赛的部分），在诸如制作首饰、打网球、骑马、为动物或儿童做志愿工作等活动中找到它，甚至在为他们生命中的他人充当智慧与支持之声的过程中找到它。

Each time I point out, “That’s your going on with normal life self—sticking with what’s important—putting one foot in front of the other no matter what,” I bring the client’s attention to the influence of the normal life self in their lives. When the client protests, “But that’s just a false self—I just fake it,” I challenge their curiosity: “So the belief is that your normal life self is just a fake—how interesting … But how could that be? Even if you are ‘faking it,’ it’s still you. If I faked it, I would do it differently.”

每当我指出“那就是你的正常生活自我——坚持那些重要的事情——无论如何都一步接一步地往前走”时，我都把来访者的注意力引向正常生活自我在他们生命中的影响。当来访者抗议说“可那只是一个假自我——我不过是在装样子”时，我会挑战他们的好奇心：“所以这个信念是，你的正常生活自我只是个冒牌货——多有意思啊……可那怎么可能呢？即便你是在‘装样子’，那也仍然是你。如果是我来装样子，我装的方式会不一样。”

I underscore the courage and instinctive drive to seek normal even in an abnormal environment: “Think of it this way: the normal life self is the part of you that keeps trying to be OK even when all the other parts are freaking out, and that takes a lot of courage and determination—to ‘keep on keeping on’ when your parts are freaking out. A ‘false self’ wouldn’t have to work that hard!”

我着重强调那种即便身处反常环境中也要寻求正常的勇气与本能驱力：“这样想吧：正常生活自我就是你身上那个即便在所有其他部分都惊慌失措时仍不断努力让自己安好的部分，而这需要极大的勇气和决心——在你的各部分都惊慌失措时还要‘坚持不懈地撑下去’。一个‘假自我’可不必那么费劲！”

Selves-Acceptance

对诸自我的接纳

“Befriending” one’s parts is not simply a therapeutic intervention: it also contributes to developing the practice of self-acceptance, one part at a time. When clients pause their emotional reactivity to “befriend” themselves, to be curious and interested rather than dismissing or judgmental, they slow time. Autonomic arousal settles; there is a relaxing of the sense of urgency to do or be anything different. With their bodies in a calmer state, they can be more at peace, and, as a result, their parts feel more at peace. Self-alienation (i.e., disowning of some parts and identifying with others exclusively) does not contribute to a sense of peace or well-being, even when it is absolutely necessary in order to survive. Self-alienation creates tension, pits part against part, communicates a hostile environment (often much like the traumatic environment), and diminishes the self-esteem of every part.

与自己的各部分“为友”不仅仅是一种治疗性干预：它也有助于发展自我接纳的实践，每次一个部分。当来访者暂停他们情绪上的反应性来与自己“为友”，以好奇和感兴趣的态度而非回避或评判的态度时，他们让时间慢了下来。自主神经的唤醒趋于平静；那种要去做任何不同的事、或成为任何不同的人的紧迫感得以放松。当他们的身体处于一种更平静的状态时，他们能够更加安宁，结果，他们的各部分也感到更加安宁。自我疏离（即否认某些部分而专门认同其他部分），即便在为了生存而绝对必要时，也无助于产生安宁或幸福之感。自我疏离制造紧张，使部分对抗部分，传递出一种充满敌意的环境（往往很像那个创伤性环境），并降低每个部分的自尊。

As I made my case for accepting and welcoming her parts to a young graduate student, Gaby, she grew thoughtful. “These are good ideas. What about having a daily meditation circle?” she asked. “I could sit and invite them to join me in the circle. They wouldn’t have to talk, but if they wanted to tell me about things they were worried or upset about, they could. It would be a safe place for all of us.” The next week, she reported back. “It was amazing to see them all there—to know they came to meet me and to see if I would really listen. A lot of them were upset about how stressful my job is and the memories it brings back. I told them I’d talk to you about how to make it easier for them.” [See Appendix B, Meditation Circle for Parts.]

当我向一位名叫Gaby的年轻研究生陈述接纳并欢迎她各部分的理由时，她陷入了沉思。“这些都是好主意。搞一个每日的冥想圈怎么样？”她问。“我可以坐下来，邀请它们到圈子里和我相聚。它们不必说话，但如果它们想告诉我它们所担忧或心烦的事情，是可以的。那会是一个属于我们所有人的安全之地。”第二周，她回来汇报。“看到它们全都在那里真是太奇妙了——知道它们前来与我相会，是想看看我会不会真的倾听。它们当中有许多对我的工作压力有多大、以及它所带回的记忆感到心烦。我告诉它们，我会和你谈谈如何让它们更轻松一些。”〔参见附录B，为各部分而设的冥想圈。〕

“Befriending our parts” means that we “radically accept” (Linehan, 1993) that we share our bodies and lives with “roommates” and that living well with ourselves requires living amicably and collaboratively with our all of our selves, not just the ones with whom we feel comfortable. As Gaby’s Meditation Circle taught her, the more we welcome rather than reject our owned and disowned selves, the safer our internal worlds will feel.

“与我们的各部分为友”意味着我们“彻底接受”（Linehan，1993）我们与“室友们”共享我们的身体和生命，而与自己好好生活，需要与我们所有的诸自我和睦、协作地共处，而不仅仅是那些让我们感到舒服的自我。正如Gaby的冥想圈教给她的，我们越是欢迎而非拒绝我们所认领的和被否认的诸自我，我们的内部世界就会感到越安全。
