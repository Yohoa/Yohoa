Strategy One
Know Yourself

10 策略一 了解你自己

THE ATTITUDES AND BEHAVIOR exhibited by Narcissists can stir up many different kinds of feelings in the people whose lives they touch. When you interact with these individuals, their distortions of reality can cause you to question yourself and doubt your own perceptions. Their shamelessness, arrogance, and sense of entitlement can make you angry and resentful. The way they deal with envy may cause you to feel diminished, and their rage, exploitation, and poor interpersonal boundaries may leave you feeling frightened, helplessly vulnerable, or even violated.

自恋者所表现出的态度和行为，能在那些被他们触及生活的人心中激起种种不同的情绪。当你与这些人打交道时，他们对现实的扭曲会让你怀疑自己、质疑自己的感知。他们的厚颜无耻、傲慢自大和特权感会令你愤怒和怨恨。他们处理嫉妒的方式可能让你感到被贬低，而他们的暴怒、剥削以及糟糕的人际界限，可能让你感到害怕、无助脆弱，甚至被侵犯。

How you react to a Narcissist is largely determined by your previous encounters with such characters. If one or both of your parents were very narcissistic, if you were subjected to significant taunting or shaming by a sibling, if you were a shy or passive child who was exploited or abused by other children, or if you’ve had intimate relationships or job circumstances wherein your trust has been violated or you’ve felt used by Narcissists, then you may be more sensitive than people who have not had these experiences. Our social history from the very beginning teaches us what to expect from others and how we are to feel about ourselves. That is why our number-one tool for dealing with the Narcissist is to examine our own experiences and recognize how our reactions contribute to our discomfort. The goal is to understand what is happening and interrupt the process to protect ourselves.

你对一个自恋者的反应，在很大程度上取决于你过去与这类人打交道的经历。如果你父母中的一方或双方非常自恋，如果你曾遭受兄弟姐妹严重的嘲弄或羞辱，如果你曾是一个被其他孩子剥削或欺侮的羞怯或被动的孩子，又或者你曾在亲密关系或工作处境中信任遭到背叛、感到被自恋者利用，那么你可能比没有这些经历的人更为敏感。我们的社会经历从一开始就教会我们对他人抱有怎样的期待，以及我们该如何看待自己。这正是为什么我们应对自恋者的头号工具，便是审视自己的经历，认清我们的反应是如何加剧了自身的不适。其目标在于理解正在发生的事，并打断这一过程，以保护自己。

This is easier said than done. Our reactions to Narcissists are often very complex, and not always negative. Sometimes we are drawn to their larger-than-life qualities and the special way they make us feel when we are included in their grandiosity and omnipotence in some way. If being part of their lives makes our own seem fuller or more exciting, we may choose to pay the price or deny that there even is one. When this happens, we may end up sacrificing ourselves to an illusion that leaves us ultimately empty and bruised. When you enter the web of the Narcissist, you leave yourself behind.

这说起来容易做起来难。我们对自恋者的反应往往十分复杂，且并不总是负面的。有时我们会被他们那种超凡脱俗的特质所吸引，被他们以某种方式把我们纳入其浮夸与全能时所带给我们的那种特殊感受所吸引。如果置身于他们的生活会让我们自己的生活显得更充实或更精彩，我们可能会选择付出代价，或者干脆否认存在什么代价。一旦如此，我们最终可能会为一个幻象牺牲自己，而这个幻象到头来只会让我们空虚和遍体鳞伤。当你踏入自恋者的网，你便把自己抛在了身后。

The Narcissist engages us via our own narcissistic vulnerabilities. This is particularly true if we have unfinished business with a narcissistic parent. These early experiences leave us with unrealistic expectations of ourselves and a need for repair of the shame we felt when we couldn’t measure up to theirs. When someone like our narcissistic parent comes along and smiles on us, it’s a natural human tendency to respond to this unconsciously as an opportunity for healing. By attaching ourselves to these individuals, we get immediate gratification of our need to feel special by basking in their glow, and when we go out of our way to please them in order to hold their interest, we signal them that we can be used to meet their needs. The door is then open to the exploitation and shame-dumping that is characteristic of the way they operate. The most treacherous ones will keep us bound to them by pumping us up between the put-downs and the manipulations.

自恋者是通过我们自身的自恋脆弱性来拉拢我们的。如果我们与一位自恋型父母之间还有未了结的心结，情况尤其如此。这些早年经历给我们留下了对自己不切实际的期望，以及一种修复羞耻的需要——那是当我们达不到他们标准时所感到的羞耻。当一个像我们自恋型父母的人出现并向我们微笑时，把这无意识地当作一次疗愈的机会去回应，是人之常情。通过依附于这些人，我们沐浴在他们的光芒里，从而立刻满足了想要感到自己特殊的需要；而当我们刻意去取悦他们以维系其兴趣时，便向他们发出了信号：我们可以被利用来满足他们的需要。于是，剥削与羞耻倾倒之门——他们运作方式的典型特征——就此敞开。最阴险的那些人，会在贬低与操纵之间穿插着给我们打气，借此把我们牢牢拴在他们身边。

Teri is a young woman who has had more than her share of narcissistic friends and lovers, and she has also been drawn into narcissistic work environments, which you will learn more about in Chapter 17. Teri has long had a deep hunger to be loved based on growing up with not one but two narcissistic parents, and she has also tried very hard to be someone whom others would admire. Like the Narcissist, Teri has been easily deflated and often depressed, and she has sought idealized relationships with exciting others in order to feel more interesting and important herself. Unlike the Narcissist, however, Teri is aware of her deep shame about never being good enough and can talk about it and take corrective action. When she realized why she was attracted to grandiose and omnipotent people who ultimately used her without regard for her needs or feelings, she began to make better choices for herself. In order to do this, however, she had to resist the pull of the narcissistic characters who have populated her life. It has been almost like saying no to a drug dependency, because the very people she has been using to make herself feel better are actually obstacles to her real self-esteem.

Teri是一位年轻女子，她身边自恋的朋友和情人多得超出常情，她还被卷入过自恋的工作环境，这一点你将在第十七章中了解更多。Teri长久以来都有一种被爱的深切渴求，这源于她成长在不是一个、而是两个自恋型父母的家庭里，她也极力想成为一个会被他人钦羡的人。与自恋者一样，Teri容易泄气，常常陷入抑郁，她寻求与那些令人兴奋的人建立理想化的关系，以便让自己感到更有趣、更重要。然而，与自恋者不同的是，Teri意识到了自己那份对“永远不够好”的深深羞耻，并且能够谈论它、采取纠正的行动。当她明白了自己为何会被那些浮夸而全能、最终不顾她的需要或感受而利用她的人所吸引时，她开始为自己做出更好的选择。然而，为了做到这一点，她不得不抵抗那些充斥于她生命中的自恋型人物的拉力。这几乎就像戒除毒瘾一样，因为那些她一直用来让自己感觉更好的人，实际上正是她真正自尊的障碍。

Michael came to see me because he was having panic attacks just as he started to drift off to sleep. It turned out that this happened mostly on nights when he anticipated an encounter the next morning with his boss, who sounded like a particularly toxic narcissistic personality. Michael, who held a management position that put him in the middle between the boss and several other employees, had a rescue fantasy that went back to his childhood with an alcoholic mother, and his self-esteem depended on protecting his staff from their tyrannical boss. It was an impossible task, but failure nevertheless made Michael feel weak and vulnerable. This conundrum opened the door to fears that had been locked away for many years, which Michael defended against by going into a hypervigilant, ready-to-fight mode. When he tried to sleep, his body jerked him awake in a most urgent fashion. When Michael accepted that he could not protect his staff and that the only way he could protect himself was to look for another job elsewhere, his panic attacks stopped.

Michael来找我，是因为他每当快要入睡时就会惊恐发作。结果发现，这大多发生在他预料第二天早上要与老板打照面的那些夜晚，而他的老板听起来是一个尤其有毒的自恋型人格。Michael担任一个管理职位，使他夹在老板和其他几名员工之间，他有一种可追溯到童年的拯救幻想——那时他有一个酗酒的母亲——而他的自尊系于保护手下员工免受暴君般老板的伤害。这是一项不可能完成的任务，但失败仍然让Michael感到软弱和脆弱。这一困境打开了一扇门，放出了被尘封多年的种种恐惧，而Michael通过进入一种高度警觉、随时备战的状态来加以防御。当他试图入睡时，他的身体会以最为急迫的方式把他猛地惊醒。当Michael接受了自己无法保护手下、而保护自己的唯一办法就是另谋他职这一事实后，他的惊恐发作便停止了。

Both Teri and Michael were able to disengage from their own narcissistic tendencies (the need to be perfect or in control at all times, the need to inflate a deflated self by clinging to unrealistic fantasies such as “being with this person will make me special” or “slaying this dragon will prove how powerful I am”), face their underlying shame, and make changes in their attitudes and behavior that allowed them to distance from the Narcissists who plagued them. If you find that you are frequently doing battle with or feeling seduced by such people, look to see what old problems you are trying to resolve by allowing yourself to engage in what are, essentially, impossible relationships.

Teri和Michael都得以从自身的自恋倾向中抽身（那种时刻都要完美或掌控的需要，那种通过紧抓不切实际的幻想来给泄了气的自我重新打气的需要，比如“和这个人在一起会让我变得特别”或“斩除这条恶龙将证明我有多强大”），直面他们底下的羞耻，并在态度和行为上做出改变，从而得以与折磨他们的自恋者拉开距离。如果你发现自己频繁地与这类人交锋，或感到被他们诱惑，那就看一看：你究竟是在试图解决哪些旧有的难题，才让自己一再投入这些本质上根本不可能成立的关系。


Prisms and Projections

棱镜与投射

The Narcissist, too, has had experiences that explain how he or she behaves. We all view life through the lens of these experiences, but the Narcissist has something more, not just a lens but a prism that refracts and distorts incoming messages to avoid the intolerable feeling of shame. This means that you are never in control of how these people perceive you or when you will be assaulted with some defensive maneuver that deflects their shame, prevents their deflation, or rein-flates them after a narcissistic injury.

自恋者同样有着能解释其行为的经历。我们都透过这些经历的镜片来看待人生，但自恋者还多了一样东西——不只是一片镜片，而是一面棱镜，它折射并扭曲传入的讯息，以避开那难以忍受的羞耻感。这意味着，你永远无法掌控这些人如何看待你，也无法掌控自己何时会遭到某种防御性手段的攻击——那种手段会偏转他们的羞耻、阻止他们泄气，或在一次自恋性创伤之后让他们重新膨胀起来。

Narcissists constantly dump—or project—unwanted parts of themselves onto other people. They then begin to behave as if others possess these unwanted pieces of themselves, and they may even succeed in getting others to feel as if they actually have those traits or feelings. This is an unconscious process for both the dumper and the dumpee, but what it means is that you end up being treated like the dirt they’ve brushed off their own psyches, or feeling the humiliation, the anger, the vulnerability, and the worthlessness that they cannot tolerate themselves. They lob it onto you, you suck it in, and for an icky while, it’s yours. If you are young, dependent, or otherwise vulnerable, their “disowned” parts may stick around and become part of your own self-image. This, in fact, is the very process by which you may have acquired a vulnerability to the Narcissist in the first place.

自恋者不断地把自己身上不想要的部分倾倒——或者说投射——到他人身上。然后他们开始表现得仿佛别人才拥有这些他们自己不想要的部分，他们甚至可能成功地让别人真的觉得自己具备那些特质或情绪。对倾倒者和被倾倒者而言，这都是一个无意识的过程，但它意味着你最终会被当作他们从自己心灵上拂去的尘土来对待，或者去体验那些他们自己无法忍受的羞辱、愤怒、脆弱和一文不值之感。他们把这些抛向你，你把它吸进去，于是在一段令人不适的时间里，它就成了你的。如果你年纪尚轻、处于依赖地位，或在别的方面比较脆弱，他们那些被“弃绝”的部分可能会赖着不走，成为你自我形象的一部分。事实上，这正是你当初可能因此而对自恋者产生易受伤害之处的那个过程。

Imagine for a moment that you are driving slowly through a parking lot at the mall when a teenager on a skateboard suddenly appears in your path, causing you to slam on your brakes. You manage to avoid hitting him, but as he sails past, he snarls and yells an obscenity at you. It is he who has been aggressive, showing by his behavior that he fails to accept that others have a right to use the parking lot, but he seems unaware of his own aggression and instead “projects” it onto you, reacting as if you are the one who has violated his entitlement to free and unobstructed access.

不妨想象一下，你正缓缓驶过商场的停车场，突然一个踩着滑板的少年冒到你车前，吓得你猛踩刹车。你设法没有撞到他，但他从一旁飞掠而过时，却朝你龇牙咧嘴，骂了一句脏话。具有攻击性的明明是他，他的行为表明他不能接受别人也有使用停车场的权利，可他似乎对自己的攻击性浑然不觉，反而把它“投射”到你身上，那反应就好像是你侵犯了他自由无阻通行的特权。

If you find yourself wanting to go after this young man, giving him a piece of your mind, or possibly pounding him into the pavement-and this is uncharacteristic of your usual behavior—you may have taken in and owned as yours the aggressive feelings he projected onto you. This is a common occurrence in relationships with narcissistic people, making it difficult to know sometimes why our reactions to certain situations are unusually intense.

如果你发现自己很想去追这个年轻人，狠狠教训他一顿，甚至想把他按在地上痛揍——而这并不符合你平日的为人——那么你很可能已经把他投射到你身上的攻击性情绪接收下来，并当成了自己的。这在与自恋者打交道的关系中十分常见，也使得我们有时很难弄清，为什么自己对某些情境的反应会异常强烈。

Now imagine yourself at a dinner party, engaged in a stimulating intellectual conversation with the guest seated next to you. You seem to have much in common, and this encourages you to open up to someone who appears to be a kindred spirit. Suddenly, the temperature drops between you and she asks you where you earned your degree. Then she turns away and begins a different conversation with the person on her other side, leaving you startled and somehow embarrassed. Your kinship has come to an abrupt end, and you have no idea what happened. Chances are that something you did without even being aware of it deflated her, and you “caught” her deflected shame. Most of us have our own vulnerabilities to shaming experiences such as this, but when we cooperate with the projection and take ownership of someone else’s shame, there is often a sense of unreality about what has just happened. We may realize that we have been treated with contempt, but we generally don’t recognize that we have absorbed the shame of the person who is humiliating us, a shame that has been “bypassed” and kept from awareness.

现在再想象一下，你在一场晚宴上，正与邻座的客人进行一场令人愉快的思想交流。你们似乎有许多共同之处，这鼓励你向这位看似志同道合的人敞开心扉。突然间，你们之间的气氛冷了下来，她问你是在哪里取得学位的。然后她转过身去，与另一侧的人攀谈起别的话题，留下你惊愕而又莫名地尴尬。你们之间的亲近戛然而止，你却完全不知道发生了什么。很可能是你在毫无察觉之间做了某件事，让她泄了气，而你“接住”了她偏转出来的羞耻。我们大多数人对诸如此类的羞辱性经历都有各自的易感之处，但当我们配合这种投射、把别人的羞耻揽为己有时，往往会对刚刚发生的事生出一种不真实之感。我们或许会意识到自己受到了轻蔑的对待，但通常不会认识到，我们吸收的是那个正在羞辱我们之人的羞耻——一种被“绕过”、被挡在意识之外的羞耻。

These are the processes that make encounters with Narcissists so uncomfortable and confusing. It truly is difficult to know what is about the other person and what is about you. The healthier you are, the more you are able to examine your own shame, and the more you will be able to figure out which of your buttons have been pushed. If you have difficulty accessing your own feelings or accurately reading others, you may wish to consult with a therapist about this. You cannot control what others do, but you can learn to contain your own reactions once you understand what is going on. Understanding where your feelings originally came from and accepting them as your own is the first step in protecting yourself against the toxic effects of narcissism. When you become comfortable with your own feelings, you will be able to deflect the shame that is triggered by the Narcissist’s internal prism.

正是这些过程，使得与自恋者的相遇如此令人不适、令人困惑。要弄清哪些是对方的事、哪些是你自己的事，确实很难。你越是健康，就越能审视自己的羞耻，也就越能辨明自己的哪些“按钮”被人按下了。如果你难以触及自己的感受，或难以准确解读他人，不妨就此向一位治疗师咨询。你无法控制别人的所作所为，但一旦理解了正在发生的事，你就能学会约束自己的反应。理解你的感受最初从何而来，并将其接纳为你自己的，是保护自己免受自恋之毒害的第一步。当你能够安然面对自己的感受时，你就能偏转那由自恋者内在棱镜所激起的羞耻。


Guidelines for Survival

生存指南

1. Be aware of your feelings when in the company of someone who repeatedly evokes shame, discomfort, anger, and especially idealization in you or other people. These feelings can be excellent indicators that you are in the presence of a Narcissist. Once you have recognized whom you are dealing with, you will be in a better position to defend yourself.

1. 当你身处某个反复在你或他人心中唤起羞耻、不适、愤怒、尤其是理想化的人身边时，要留意自己的感受。这些感受可以成为绝佳的指标，提示你正与一个自恋者在一起。一旦你认清了自己面对的是什么人，你就能更好地保护自己。

2. When you have uncomfortable or intense feelings in the presence of a Narcissist, ask yourself what buttons of yours are being pushed. Remember times past when you have felt this way and, from this more emotionally distant perspective, consider why you respond as you do. Don’t be afraid to look at your own narcissistic vulnerabilities, because this is exactly what will make you stronger.

2. 当你在自恋者面前产生不适或强烈的情绪时，问问自己：是哪些“按钮”被按下了。回想过去你有过这种感受的时刻，并从这种在情感上更为抽离的视角出发，思考自己为何会做出这样的反应。不要害怕审视你自身的自恋脆弱性，因为这恰恰正是会让你变得更强大的东西。

3. Once you’re pretty sure you’ve identified the piece of the action that is yours, think about how your feelings help the Narcissist manage shame in some way. Try not to personalize what is happening. Although it couldn’t feel more personal, it really is not. You are just a means to an end.

3. 一旦你颇有把握地辨认出这件事中属于你的那一部分，就想一想：你的感受是如何以某种方式帮助自恋者来处理羞耻的。尽量不要把正在发生的事往自己身上揽。尽管它给人的感觉再个人化不过，但它其实根本不是针对你个人的。你不过是达成某个目的的手段而已。

4. You need to find a way to detach from the feeling of diminishment the Narcissist evokes in you. Sometimes it helps to think of this person as being two years old on the inside.

4. 你需要找到一种办法，让自己从自恋者在你心中唤起的那种被贬低之感中抽离出来。有时，把这个人想象成内在只有两岁，会有所帮助。

5. When deflecting the shame projected by the Narcissist, resist the urge to retaliate. Don’t try to challenge or enlighten this person either. The Narcissist has a lot at stake in keeping unconscious processes unconscious. If you try to tamper with this, you may escalate the situation to your own detriment or discomfort.

5. 在偏转自恋者投射过来的羞耻时，要抵住报复的冲动。也不要试图去挑战或点醒这个人。让无意识的过程始终留在无意识之中，这对自恋者而言利害攸关。如果你试图去触动它，可能会使局面升级，最终损害的或让自己难受的还是你自己。

6. It needs to be enough for you to know that you have put the projections back where they belong in your own mind, regardless of how the Narcissist sees the situation. If you have trouble letting that be enough, you may need more personalized assistance to work on this in greater depth. A competent therapist can help.

6. 你需要做到：只要在自己心里知道你已经把那些投射放回了它们本该归属的地方，这就够了，无论自恋者如何看待这一情形。如果你难以让“这就够了”成立，那么你或许需要更具针对性的协助，以便更深入地处理这一问题。一位称职的治疗师能够帮上忙。
