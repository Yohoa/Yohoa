7
Bad Boundaries

7 糟糕的边界

WE ARE BY NATURE SOCIAL CREATURES who thrive on meaningful affiliations with family, friends, and community. We all need to “belong” to something, someone outside of ourselves. We are also, however, unique individuals who have private thoughts, personal feelings, and a body that belongs only to ourselves. Although we were not meant to live as islands, completely self-involved and cut off from other people, we are programmed from birth to evolve into separate beings with an autonomous Self. The soundness of the boundary between Self and others will affect not only how we perceive ourselves but how we experience other people and to a considerable extent how we are treated by them. Good boundaries, the recognition of separateness, make for healthy relationships.

我们天生就是社会性的生物，依靠与家人、朋友和社群之间富有意义的联结而蓬勃生长。我们都需要“归属”于某种事物、归属于自身之外的某个人。然而，我们同时又是独一无二的个体，拥有私密的想法、个人的感受，以及一具只属于我们自己的身体。尽管我们并非注定要像孤岛一样、完全沉溺于自我并与他人隔绝地生活，但我们从出生起就被设定为要发展成拥有自主自我的独立存在。自我与他人之间这道边界是否牢固，不仅会影响我们如何看待自己，也会影响我们如何体验他人，并在很大程度上影响他人如何对待我们。良好的边界，即对彼此独立的承认，造就了健康的关系。

The Narcissist suffers from a deep character flaw in the development of a sense of Self. This flaw prevents such individuals from being able to recognize that they have boundaries and that others are separate and not extensions of themselves. Others either exist to meet their needs or may as well not exist at all. Those who offer the possibility of some sort of gratification will be treated as if they are a part of the Narcissist and will be expected, automatically, to live up to that person’s expectations. In the mind of a Narcissist, there is no boundary between Self and other.

自恋者在自我感的发展上有着深层的性格缺陷。这一缺陷使他们无法认识到自己拥有边界，也无法认识到他人是独立的存在、而非他们自身的延伸。在他们看来，他人要么是为满足其需求而存在的，要么根本就不该存在。凡是有可能提供某种满足的人，都会被当作自恋者的一部分来对待，并被理所当然地期望去达成此人的种种期待。在自恋者的心中，自我与他人之间不存在任何边界。

The reason why Narcissists function this way goes back to their experiences in early childhood. As anyone who has ever spent any time around small children knows, they go through an egocentric stage when they seem to feel very important and invincible. This begins to occur about the same time they start to walk and normally continues into the twos. Their egocentric behavior is usually viewed by adults as anywhere from endearing to exasperating, but it has a very important function. The child in this stage is actually beginning to form the first sense of himself as separate from his primary caregivers, and an inflated sense of self-importance allows him to explore the world without overwhelming fears and doubts.

自恋者之所以以这种方式运作，要追溯到他们幼年早期的经历。任何曾与幼儿相处过一段时间的人都知道，孩子会经历一个以自我为中心的阶段，那时他们似乎觉得自己极其重要、不可战胜。这通常在他们开始学步的同时出现，并一般持续到两岁左右。成年人对孩子这种以自我为中心的行为的看法不一，从觉得惹人怜爱到觉得令人恼火都有，但它其实有着十分重要的功能。处于这一阶段的孩子，实际上正开始形成把自己与主要照料者区分开来的最初自我感，而一种膨胀的自我重要感，使他能够在探索世界时不被压倒性的恐惧和怀疑所困扰。

Before this point, the child has experienced himself psychologically as indistinguishable from the person he is most attached to, the one who has been most responsible for his care. He sees this person as wonderful and powerful and himself as the same because, in his infant mind, they are one. When he begins to get the picture that he is small and helpless, that the power resides in the other person and not himself, a part of him wishes to stay in this fantasy of union with the powerful caregiver. If there are no boundaries between them, he can continue to feel control over the one who meets his needs.

在此之前，孩子在心理上把自己体验为与他最依恋的那个人——也就是最主要承担照料他的人——无法区分的存在。他把这个人看作美好而强大的，并因此把自己也看作同样美好而强大，因为在他婴儿般的心智里，他们二人本是一体。当他开始意识到自己渺小而无助、力量存在于对方而非自己身上时，他内心的一部分却渴望停留在与这位强大照料者融为一体的幻想之中。如果他们之间没有边界，他就能继续感到自己掌控着那个满足他需求的人。

It is one of the jobs of parents to ease the small child through this time of emotional vulnerability by helping her to accept that, while she is separate and relatively powerless, she is valuable for who she really is. If parents allow the child to feel too important or to have too much control, the child maintains her infantile fantasy that her power comes from controlling her powerful parents. This stands in the way of her developing an appreciation of her own real capabilities and the confidence to stand alone. Instead, she hones skills of scanning the environment for others who can give her what she lacks or needs. If she can convince them that she is special, they will admire her and she can share in their power. This is a rather desperate quest, and those who cannot be used will be discarded. Those who are useful, however, are perceived, like the parents, as extensions of the Self and treated as if they had no separate existence.

父母的职责之一，就是帮助幼小的孩子平稳度过这段情感脆弱期——帮助她接受这样一个事实：尽管她是独立的、相对无力的，但她因真实的自己而有价值。如果父母让孩子感到自己过于重要、或掌握过多的控制权，孩子就会维持那种婴儿式的幻想，以为自己的力量来自对强大父母的掌控。这会妨碍她去欣赏自己真实的能力，妨碍她培养独自站立的信心。结果，她转而磨炼出一套本领，专门扫描周围环境，寻找那些能给予她所缺乏或所需之物的人。如果她能让这些人相信自己很特别，他们就会仰慕她，而她便可以分享他们的力量。这是一种相当孤注一掷的追寻，凡是无法被利用的人都会被抛弃。然而，那些有用的人，则会像父母一样被视为自我的延伸，被当作没有独立存在的对象来对待。

I recall the story of a patient of mine who came home one day to find that her mother-in-law had replaced her living room furniture. Without consulting my patient, the mother-in-law had selected a beautiful new sofa and matching chairs and arranged to have them delivered, and the old furniture disposed of, as a “surprise.” My patient was indeed surprised—and dismayed. True, she had been planning to replace the old furniture, but she had been looking forward to shopping and making her own choices. That she might want to do this never crossed the mother-in-law’s mind. Her son and his wife had purchased her old home, so she knew what would look good in the living room, and besides, she felt like giving them a gift. The younger couple also both worked for the mother-in-law, so she was used to having control in their lives. There were many boundary violations in this family.

我记得我的一位患者讲过这样一件事：有一天她回到家，发现婆婆把她客厅里的家具给换掉了。婆婆事先没有和我的患者商量，就挑选了一套漂亮的新沙发和配套的椅子，安排人送货上门，并把旧家具处理掉，作为一个“惊喜”。我的患者确实大吃一惊——而且十分沮丧。诚然，她原本就打算更换那套旧家具，但她一直盼着自己去逛店、亲自挑选。她可能想要这样做这一点，从未在婆婆的脑海中闪过。儿子和儿媳买下了她的旧宅，所以她知道客厅里摆什么才好看；何况，她正想送他们一份礼物。这对年轻夫妇还都在婆婆手下工作，所以她早已习惯了掌控他们的生活。这个家庭里存在着许多边界侵犯。

People who tolerate boundary violations are generally those who, like the Narcissist, have not formed a strong sense of separate Self, usually because they have been trained to accept intrusions while growing up in their own families and have not been given support for autonomy. Others from such backgrounds are very sensitized to these intrusions and erect rigid boundaries to protect themselves. These are the people who have difficulty trusting and allowing intimacy in close relationships. They develop an anxious, apprehensive attitude toward others, as if they expect to be violated in some way. Sometimes, however, their lack of experience with healthy boundaries makes them confused or unsure when an intrusion is occurring. Such was the case with my patient whose mother-in-law replaced her furniture. She knew that she was unhappy about this gift but blamed herself for being unappreciative. Who wouldn’t want beautiful new furniture? she reasoned. It took quite a bit of discussion before she was able to understand why she felt so uncomfortable.

能够容忍边界被侵犯的人，通常和自恋者一样，没有形成牢固的独立自我感，这往往是因为他们在自己原生家庭中成长时被训练成了接受侵入，而从未获得过对自主的支持。来自这种背景的另一些人，则对此类侵入极为敏感，于是竖起僵硬的边界来保护自己。正是这些人在亲密关系中难以信任他人、难以允许亲密。他们对他人发展出一种焦虑、戒备的态度，仿佛预料着自己会以某种方式被侵犯。然而，有时由于缺乏与健康边界打交道的经验，他们在侵入发生时会感到困惑或不确定。我那位被婆婆换掉家具的患者就是如此。她知道自己对这份礼物并不高兴，却又责怪自己不知感恩。谁会不想要漂亮的新家具呢？她这样推想。经过相当一番讨论，她才得以明白自己为何会感到如此不适。

The Narcissist is often equally unaware when violating the personal boundaries of others. Mail and personal journals or diaries are read; closed bathroom and bedroom doors are ignored; purses and wallets are rifled through; clothing, toiletries, and other possessions are “borrowed”; conversations are eavesdropped on; nosy questions are asked; unsolicited opinions are offered; ideas are stolen; confidences are not kept; hugs and kisses and other kinds of touch are forced on someone whose resistance is overlooked. Many of my patients report being told, “That’s not how you feel,” or, “This is what you think,” or even, “This is who you are.” When confronted with these violations, the Narcissist is often annoyed or even mystified. In a world without fences, why should they have to knock on doors?

在侵犯他人的个人边界时，自恋者往往同样毫无觉察。他们会偷看别人的信件和私人日记；无视紧闭的浴室门和卧室门；翻找别人的手提包和钱包；“借用”别人的衣物、洗漱用品和其他物品；偷听别人的谈话；问东问西、刨根问底；不请自来地发表意见；窃取别人的点子；泄露别人的秘密；不顾对方的抗拒，强行拥抱、亲吻或施加其他形式的身体接触。我的许多患者都说，自己曾被告知“你才不是那样想的”，或者“你想的是这个”，甚至“你这个人就是这样”。当被指出这些侵犯行为时，自恋者往往感到恼火，甚至大惑不解。在一个没有篱笆的世界里，他们凭什么还得敲门呢？

When many or most of the Seven Deadly Sins are present in an individual who presents for mental health services, that person may be diagnosed with a Narcissistic Personality Disorder, a relatively rare condition. The American Psychiatric Association estimates that only about one in one hundred people meets the full criteria for this severe form of narcissism, yet there are many more individuals who exhibit these traits to some degree sufficient to cause significant distress, if not in themselves, then certainly in others with whom they come in close contact on a regular basis. Many of these individuals never come to the attention of mental health professionals because they are too shame-intolerant to recognize their own narcissism and are more inclined to blame others when they are in pain. Even when they seek help, they are more likely to be treated for depression, anxiety, relationship difficulties, or job-related stress than for the Narcissistic Personality Disorder that lies beneath their presenting problems. Many therapists miss or ignore the narcissism because it is not amenable to the short-term approaches currently favored by the insurance companies who pay for treatment. Sadly, treatment is often not effective in these cases, because the more narcissistic people are, the more rigid and resistant they are to behavioral change.

当一个前来寻求心理健康服务的人身上同时存在“七宗罪”中的许多项或大多数项时，此人可能会被诊断为自恋型人格障碍——这是一种相对罕见的状况。美国精神病学协会估计，只有大约百分之一的人完全符合这种严重自恋形式的全部标准；然而，还有远多于此的人，在某种程度上表现出这些特质，足以造成显著的痛苦——即便不是给他们自己，也必定是给那些经常与之密切接触的人。这些人中许多从未引起心理健康专业人士的注意，因为他们对羞耻过于无法容忍，以至于认不出自己的自恋，并在痛苦时更倾向于责怪他人。即便他们寻求帮助，也更可能因抑郁、焦虑、关系困难或工作压力而接受治疗，而非因潜藏在这些表面问题之下的自恋型人格障碍而接受治疗。许多治疗师之所以忽略或漠视这种自恋，是因为它无法用当下那些为治疗付费的保险公司所青睐的短期疗法来应对。可悲的是，在这类案例中，治疗往往收效甚微，因为一个人越是自恋，他就越僵化，越抗拒行为上的改变。

While a narcissistic personality that meets the full criteria for a clinical diagnosis may be relatively rare—and we should all avoid labels that trigger shame in others—there is plenty of evidence that toxic levels of narcissism are pandemic in American society and have been for some time (see Chapter 19 for a discussion of this). You are likely to recognize the Seven Deadly Sins in many people you encounter, and perhaps even in yourself. This book is designed to help you make sense of these experiences and to better protect yourself from being used in ways that undermine your healthy self-esteem.

虽然完全符合临床诊断全部标准的自恋型人格可能相对罕见——而且我们都应当避免使用那些会在他人身上引发羞耻的标签——但有大量证据表明，有毒程度的自恋在美国社会中已蔓延成灾，而且这种状况已经持续了相当一段时间（关于这一点的讨论见第19章）。你很可能会在自己遇到的许多人身上、甚至在你自己身上，认出这“七宗罪”。本书旨在帮助你理清这些经历，并更好地保护自己，免于被人以那些损害你健康自尊的方式所利用。

Don’t be surprised, however, if along the way you have to own up to some unhealthy narcissism of your own. This is to be expected if you were raised in a family where one or both parents were significantly narcissistic, leaving you vulnerable in many ways. You may share with the more flagrant Narcissist an impaired sense of interpersonal boundaries, considerable shame-sensitivity, a tendency to distort reality, and more anger than you can account for. You may feel drawn to narcissistic characters and also be more reactive to them than others are. Your strength, however, is in having enough shame tolerance to be able to learn about this problem and make changes that will improve the quality of your life. Starting with Part III, you will find out how.

然而，如果在这一过程中你不得不承认自己也有某些不健康的自恋，请不要感到意外。如果你成长于一个父母中有一方或双方都明显自恋的家庭，使你在许多方面变得脆弱，那么出现这种情况便在意料之中。你可能与那些更为明目张胆的自恋者一样，有着受损的人际边界感、相当强的羞耻敏感性、扭曲现实的倾向，以及多到自己也说不清缘由的愤怒。你可能会被自恋型的人物所吸引，也会比别人对他们反应更强烈。然而，你的力量在于：你拥有足够的羞耻容忍力，能够去了解这个问题，并做出改变，从而提升你的生活质量。从第三部分开始，你将了解到具体该怎么做。

But first, it is important to understand how a Narcissist comes to be, so that you know exactly what (or whom) you are up against. As you shall see, we all go through a stage of normal narcissism in early childhood, and the way we are parented determines how much of that we carry forward—and in what ways.

但首先，重要的是要理解一个自恋者是如何形成的，这样你才能准确地知道自己面对的是什么（或是谁）。正如你将看到的，我们每个人在幼年早期都会经历一个正常自恋的阶段，而我们被养育的方式，决定了我们会把其中多少带向往后的人生——以及以怎样的方式带下去。
