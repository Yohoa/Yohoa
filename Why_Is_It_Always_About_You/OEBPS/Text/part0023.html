Strategy Four
Cultivate Reciprocal Relationships

13 策略四 培养互惠的关系

ONE OF THE BEST WAYS TO COPE with Narcissists is to avoid becoming very involved with them in the first place. And when you can’t avoid them, try to limit your involvement and surround yourself instead with healthier people who are capable of give-and-take relationships. Along the same lines, you might also want to avoid the toxic environments in which Narcissists thrive and instead seek those in which differences between people are recognized and accepted, healthy boundaries are maintained, and expectations are clear and realistic.

应对自恋者最好的方法之一，就是从一开始就避免与他们深度纠缠。而当你无法回避他们时，尽量限制自己卷入的程度，转而让自己身边围绕着更健康、能够建立有来有往关系的人。沿着同样的思路，你或许也该避开那些让自恋者如鱼得水的有毒环境，转而去寻找那种环境——在那里，人与人之间的差异得到认可和接纳，健康的界限得以维持，期望也清晰而务实。

If you come from a narcissistic family, you will have to work especially hard to accomplish these goals. You can be sure that a parent’s narcissism has left its mark on you. You may feel unworthy of relationships in which people actually value and respect you, and you may be unfamiliar with the rhythms of reciprocity. Do not despair. With insight, effort, and self-control, you can overcome these obstacles from your childhood and live in health. You may even be able to do this while maintaining relationships with the Narcissists in your family, but this will be difficult and constantly challenging. If continuing to interact with these people while you are trying to get your own life on track seems overwhelming, you may need to distance yourself, at least for a while.

如果你来自一个自恋的家庭，要实现这些目标，你就得格外努力。你大可以肯定，父母的自恋已经在你身上留下了印记。你可能觉得自己不配拥有那种别人真正珍视、尊重你的关系，你可能对互惠往来的节奏感到陌生。不要绝望。凭借洞察、努力和自我约束，你可以克服这些源自童年的障碍，活得健康。你甚至或许能够在做到这一点的同时，仍与家中的那些自恋者保持往来，但这将是艰难的，会不断带来挑战。如果一边努力让自己的生活步入正轨、一边继续与这些人打交道令你不堪重负，那么你可能需要与他们拉开距离，至少暂时如此。

It has been said that friends are the family we choose. If narcissism describes key members of your clan, it will be especially important to choose your friends wisely—and that goes double for love relationships that include physical intimacy. The tendency is to recreate the dramas of our earlier life in an effort to write new endings, but if we approach these reparative relationships without awareness or a firm grasp on reality, and with an inability to set limits, we are likely to have the same outcomes as before. If, on the other hand, we understand our vulnerabilities, see clearly through the illusions of our desires and the distortions of our fears, and find the courage to protect ourselves from those who would use us without regard for our separateness, we can change the course of our own lives. Choosing healthy friends and lovers is a very good place to begin.

有人说，朋友是我们自己选择的家人。如果自恋正好可以用来形容你家族中的关键成员，那么明智地选择朋友就尤为重要——而对于包含身体亲密在内的爱情关系，这一点更要加倍重视。我们往往倾向于重演早年生活中的种种戏码，试图为它们写下新的结局，但如果我们带着浑然不觉、没有牢牢把握现实、又无力设立界限的状态去投入这些修复性的关系，那么很可能会重蹈覆辙。反过来，如果我们理解自己的脆弱之处，看穿自身欲望所制造的幻象和恐惧所造成的扭曲，并找到勇气去保护自己，远离那些不顾我们独立性而要利用我们的人，那么我们就能改变自己人生的走向。选择健康的朋友和爱人，是一个非常好的起点。


What Is a Reciprocal Relationship?

什么是互惠的关系？

 

1. In a reciprocal relationship, each person contributes something and each person benefits in some way. The contributions and benefits need not be spelled out or exactly equal, but it is important that each person feels he or she is receiving good value in exchange for what is offered.

1. 在一段互惠的关系中，每个人都付出一些东西，每个人也都以某种方式从中受益。所付出与所获得的不必明言，也不必完全对等，但重要的是，每个人都觉得自己以付出换得了相称的价值。

2. There is flexibility in the roles of giver and taker. Whether it is an unspoken intuitive understanding, a formal contract, or something in between, there is a mechanism by which each person knows when to give and when to receive. Over the course of the lifetime of the relationship, both parties have a sense of fairness about this aspect of their interactions.

2. 给予者与接受者的角色具有灵活性。无论是一种心照不宣的直觉默契、一份正式的契约，还是介于两者之间的某种安排，总有一套机制让每个人都知道何时该给予、何时该接受。在这段关系的整个历程中，双方对于他们互动中的这一方面都怀有一种公平感。

3. Both parties are able to feel valued for their contributions and to express appreciation for what is received.

3. 双方都能因自己的付出而感到被珍视，并能对所获得的表达感激。

4. Separateness and boundaries are valued on both sides. In the event of conflict, both parties attempt to work out their differences with respect for one another’s feelings and points of view.

4. 双方都珍视彼此的独立与界限。一旦发生冲突，双方都会本着尊重对方感受和观点的态度，努力化解分歧。

5. There is no need to “keep score.” Scorekeeping—keeping track of who’s done what and who “owes” whom—is an indicator that someone feels the relationship is not reciprocal or has difficulty with the sometimes irregular flow of give and take.

5. 没有“记账”的必要。记账——计较谁做了什么、谁“欠”谁——是一个信号，表明有人觉得这段关系并不互惠，或者难以接受那种有时并不均衡的付出与回报的流动。

If you come from a relatively healthy family, many of these considerations are probably second nature to you. While you may have some buttons that get pushed from time to time (no one’s “wonder years” were perfect), you are less likely to project and distort and probably less vulnerable to resonating with the projections of others. In short, you probably have pretty good boundaries and little need to live in a fantasy world. Where those who have been exposed to excessive narcissism in their early years may have inherited an internal prism, you may be fortunate enough to have little that obstructs you from reading or hearing others accurately. Because you have a realistic view of yourself (both strengths and weaknesses) and the ability to empathize, you can “put yourself in others’ shoes” and see them in a balanced way. Your personal life is relatively free of interpersonal strife because you tend to choose relationships with other healthy people.

如果你来自一个相对健康的家庭，那么上述许多考量对你而言大概早已是第二天性。尽管你或许时不时也会有某些“按钮”被人按到（没有谁的“黄金成长岁月”是完美无瑕的），但你较少会去投射和扭曲，也大概不那么容易与他人的投射产生共鸣。简而言之，你很可能拥有相当好的界限，几乎不需要活在一个幻想世界里。早年暴露于过度自恋之中的人，可能继承了一面内在的棱镜，而你或许够幸运，几乎没有什么东西妨碍你准确地解读或听懂他人。由于你对自己（既包括长处也包括短处）有着现实的看法，又具备共情的能力，你能够“设身处地”，以一种平衡的眼光去看待他人。你的个人生活相对没有什么人际纷争，因为你往往会选择与其他健康的人建立关系。

If you come from health but are encountering unhealthy narcissism, it is probably in the work environment or some other place where the people you deal with are not necessarily of your choice, such as in a church, club, or other organization to which you belong. Alas, they are everywhere, and if you can’t avoid them, you will have to take stock of your options, recognize any limitations and vulnerabilities you may have, be realistic about whom you are dealing with, and set those boundaries. Be grateful that you have reciprocal relationships in the rest of your life to counterbalance the narcissism that inevitably crosses all of our paths in today’s world. Others less fortunate pay dearly to achieve the self-esteem that was a gift from your healthy family.

如果你出身健康，却仍遭遇不健康的自恋，那很可能是在工作环境，或在某个你打交道的人未必由你选择的地方，比如在你所属的教会、俱乐部或其他组织里。可叹的是，这种人无处不在，如果你无法回避他们，你就只能盘点自己的选项，认清自己可能存在的任何局限与脆弱，对自己所面对的是什么人保持现实的认识，并设立那些界限。要心怀感激——你人生中其余的部分还拥有互惠的关系，可以用来抵消那种在当今世界不可避免地与我们每个人狭路相逢的自恋。那些不那么幸运的人，则要付出沉重的代价，才能换来那份本是你健康家庭赠予你的自尊。

Addictive behavior often begins in adolescence, when brain and character development are put to the test by the biological and social challenges of maturation. Those with pre-existing deficits share a unique intolerance to shame that forms an intriguing link between narcissism and addiction. In Chapter 15, you will learn how ruptures in the family and in the development of a Self can lead to addiction and may even predict a particular drug choice. Is your Narcissist also an addict? Could you be co-dependent?

成瘾行为往往始于青春期，那时大脑和性格的发育正经受着成长在生理与社会层面带来的种种挑战的考验。那些原本就存在缺陷的人，都有一种对羞耻的独特无法容忍，这在自恋与成瘾之间形成了一种耐人寻味的联系。在第十五章，你将了解到，家庭中的破裂以及自我发展中的破裂，是如何可能导致成瘾的，甚至可能预示着对某一特定毒品的选择。你身边的那个自恋者是否也是个瘾君子？你会不会是“共依存”的一方？

Our search for love and intimacy brings us face to face with the quality of our earliest attachments to the primary caregiver. Whether we felt safe and loved, how we resolved our own childhood narcissism, and whether we established psychological autonomy set the tone for intimate relationships throughout life. Sensations of being inflated, fantasies of ideal love, primitive longings to merge—is it love, or is it narcissism? Find out in Chapter 16.

我们对爱与亲密的追寻，让我们直面自己与主要照料者之间最早依恋的质量。我们是否感到安全、被爱，我们如何化解了自己童年期的自恋，以及我们是否建立起心理上的自主，这些都为我们一生中的亲密关系定下了基调。那种被吹胀膨胀的感觉、对理想爱情的幻想、想要融为一体的原始渴望——这究竟是爱，还是自恋？请到第十六章中寻找答案。

Many of today’s work environments have become stressful places where narcissistic leadership has toxic consequences for all employees. Since Narcissists seek power as a way of pumping themselves up and off-loading shame, we are most likely to encounter the boldest and most ruthless among them wherever there’s a piece of turf to be controlled. Chapter 17 explores behind the mask of omnipotence to the vulnerabilities that drive Narcissists and make life miserable for those under their thumb. 如今许多工作环境都已变成充满压力的地方，自恋型的领导对所有员工都造成有毒的后果。由于自恋者追逐权力是为了给自己打气、卸下羞耻，所以凡是有一块地盘可供掌控的地方，我们最有可能遇到他们当中最大胆、最冷酷无情的那些人。第十七章将深入探究那张全能面具的背后，揭示那些驱动着自恋者、并让受其摆布的人苦不堪言的脆弱之处。如果你想留在这场游戏里，这里有一些教你如何守住自己阵脚的建议。

If you thought a narcissistic Mom or Dad was difficult while you were growing up, just wait to see what’s in store when the aging process slowly and relentlessly robs them of their usual defenses. Chapter 18 is for every ACON (Adult Child of a Narcissist) who still holds out hope—as time runs out—of being seen as separate and valued in a parent’s eyes.
