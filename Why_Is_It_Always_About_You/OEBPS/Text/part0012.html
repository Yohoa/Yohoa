Envy

4 嫉妒

THE NARCISSIST’S NEED TO SECURE a sense of superiority encounters an obstacle whenever someone else appears to have something that he or she lacks. Pop goes the internal balloon as the other’s threat to the superiority of Me registers deep within the unconscious. “Crisis! Crisis!” sounds the alarm. “Better activate the neutralizer!” What weapon does the Narcissist choose to silence the rumblings of shame?

每当别人显得拥有某种自恋者所缺乏的东西时，自恋者确保优越感的需要就会遇到障碍。当他人对“我”之优越的威胁在潜意识深处被察觉到时，那只内在的气球便“砰”地一声破了。“危机！危机！”警报拉响。“最好启动中和器！”自恋者会选择什么样的武器来压下那隆隆作响的羞耻？

The answer is contempt. “That so-and-so isn’t such a big deal as he thinks he is.” Never mind that the “so-and-so” may be utterly humble and completely unaware of having given offense—this is a narcissistic distortion akin to shame-dumping and may have no connection to reality. Then comes the laundry list of the other’s flaws, and it can get pretty dirty. The intent, usually quite unconscious, is to soil the other enough so that the Narcissist, by comparison, is restored to the superior position. There may be awareness of feelings of disdain (always justified, of course), but the feeling of envy will be adamantly denied. To admit to envy would be to acknowledge inferiority, which no good Narcissist would ever do.

答案是蔑视。“那个谁谁谁，根本没他自以为的那么了不起。”至于那个“谁谁谁”或许极其谦逊、完全不知道自己冒犯了谁——这无关紧要，因为这是一种类似于倾倒羞耻的自恋扭曲，可能与现实毫无关系。接着便是一长串对方缺点的清单，而且会扯得相当不堪。这么做的意图——通常相当无意识——是把对方抹黑到足够的程度，好让自恋者相形之下重新回到优越的位置。他或许能意识到自己的鄙夷之情（当然，永远是理直气壮的），但嫉妒的感觉则会被坚决否认。承认嫉妒，就等于承认自己低人一等，而任何一个像样的自恋者都绝不会这么做。

Contempt is usually verbalized but may also be expressed in behavior. I once saw a married couple whose shame-sensitivity was creating serious problems in their relationship. Both were attorneys, and used to calling the shots. For their first session, the husband arrived late, and I detected a faint aroma of alcohol. “I peed in your bushes,” he announced as we introduced ourselves. For a moment, I didn’t know what to say. Then I realized how excruciating it was for him to explore his marital difficulties with a stranger whose vulnerabilities were allowed to remain hidden. Even before we met, he must have been thinking, “Who is she to sit in judgment of me?” And so he defiled my shrubbery—and made sure I knew what he’d done.

蔑视通常通过言语表达，但也可能通过行为流露。我曾经接待过一对夫妻，他们对羞耻的敏感正给彼此的关系制造严重问题。两人都是律师，习惯了发号施令。第一次会谈时，丈夫迟到了，我闻到一丝淡淡的酒味。“我在你的灌木丛里撒了泡尿，”就在我们互相介绍时他宣布道。有那么一瞬间，我不知该说什么。随后我意识到，对他来说，要在一个可以把自身脆弱隐藏起来的陌生人面前剖析自己的婚姻困境，是多么令人难以忍受。甚至在见到我之前，他想必就在心里嘀咕：“她凭什么坐在那儿评判我？”于是他玷污了我的灌木丛——而且要确保我知道他干了什么。

A bright young man named Joel, a graduate student whose family dynamics have left him with narcissistic wounds, explored his problem with envy one day. “There’s this guy in my program who just bugs me, and I don’t know why,” he told me. “He’s really pretty nice, but he’s married, and, for some reason, that bothers me. I keep thinking he’s missing out on going to bars and hitting on women. I mean, we’re the same age, and I’m out having fun and he’s married. Why should I care if he’s married or not? Why should that make me look down on him?”

一个名叫Joel的聪明年轻人，是一名研究生，家庭关系给他留下了自恋性创伤。有一天，他探讨起自己的嫉妒问题。“我们专业里有个家伙就是让我心烦，可我不知道为什么，”他对我说。“他其实人挺好的，但他结婚了，不知怎么的，这让我很不舒服。我老是想，他错过了去酒吧、去搭讪女孩的机会。我是说，我们年纪一样大，我在外面寻欢作乐，他却结了婚。他结没结婚关我什么事？为什么这会让我看不起他？”

Joel had grown up with a father who had fallen short of his own ambitions and was very critical of his son. Joel’s mother seemed to think of her husband as a failure and clearly wanted her son to do better. There was a great deal of pressure on Joel to achieve, to take the high road at all times, and when he fell short of what eventually became his own expectations, the voice that admonished him from within seemed like his own. But hearing this voice invariably made him angry. The minute his nose left the grindstone, he felt conflicted and defensive. Much of what he considered “fun” was no more than a compulsive quest to vanquish the critical inner parent. The club-hopping and weekend drinking binges and scoring with women who meant nothing to him were all part of a struggle to be just a regular guy, to defy that critical, demanding inner parent.

Joel是在这样一个父亲身边长大的：父亲没能实现自己的雄心抱负，对儿子极为苛责。Joel的母亲似乎把丈夫看作一个失败者，显然希望儿子能出人头地。Joel承受着巨大的压力，要去取得成就，要时时刻刻走正道；而当他没能达到那些最终内化为他自己期望的标准时，那个从内心训斥他的声音听起来就像是他自己的。但每次听到这个声音，都无一例外地让他恼火。一旦他不再埋头苦干，就会感到矛盾和戒备。他所谓的许多“乐子”，不过是一种想要击败那个挑剔的内在父母的强迫性追求。泡夜店、周末的酗酒狂欢，以及和那些对他毫无意义的女人上床，全都是这场挣扎的一部分——只为做个普普通通的家伙，以此对抗那个挑剔、苛求的内在父母。

But when Joel encountered a peer who was happily married and settled down, the bar was raised on what could be expected from a man in his twenties, and Joel didn’t measure up. The inner parent began to carp: “Why can’t you grow up and be a responsible adult?” But Joel wasn’t listening. He was too busy doing damage control. “There’s nothing wrong with me,” his inner defender cried out. “I’m just doing what any red-blooded twenty-four-year-old is supposed to do. It’s that married guy who’s weird. Imagine that, married at twenty-four!”

但当Joel遇到一个婚姻幸福、安定下来的同龄人时，对一个二十多岁男人的期望标准被抬高了，而Joel够不着这个标准。内在父母开始唠叨：“你怎么就不能长大、做个负责任的成年人呢？”可Joel根本没在听。他正忙着进行损害控制。“我一点毛病都没有，”他内心的辩护者喊道。“我只不过是在做任何一个血气方刚的二十四岁小伙子都该做的事。是那个结了婚的家伙才奇怪呢。想想看，二十四岁就结婚！”

Joel at first resisted the idea that what he was experiencing was envy, but when we put the pieces together, it all began to make sense to him. He certainly didn’t envy the guy for being married, but he did covet his colleague’s appearance of maturity, which made his own weekend revelries seem adolescent by comparison. Envy knows intuitively that the best defense is a good offense, so he began to look down on his colleague.

起初Joel抵触“他所体验的其实是嫉妒”这个说法，但当我们把这些片段拼凑到一起时，一切便开始对他说得通了。他当然不是嫉妒那个家伙结了婚，但他确实觊觎同学身上那副成熟的模样，相比之下，自己周末的狂欢就显得幼稚不堪。嫉妒凭直觉懂得，最好的防守就是主动进攻，于是他开始看不起自己的同学。

Sometimes the haughty face of envy is disguised beneath a mask of excessive praise or admiration, often followed by a self-disparaging remark. “This is the best cheesecake I’ve ever eaten! I so admire people who can bake. You know, I’m all thumbs in the kitchen. How do you do it and still run your own business? You’re just so talented!” Your cheesecake has exposed the Narcissist’s culinary inadequacy, for which there is no ready defense. So in a grand gesture, she cedes the kitchen to you and relocates her superiority in the moral realm. “I may not be able to bake, but no one could be more appreciative or generous than I. Nice little cheesecake, but I’m still better than you.” Watch how quickly the conversation shifts once the Narcissist reconnects to her superiority. There won’t be any more talk about your baked goods, or any of your other talents either.

有时，嫉妒那副高傲的面孔会藏在一层过分夸赞或钦佩的面具之下，后面往往还跟着一句自我贬低的话。“这是我吃过最好吃的芝士蛋糕！我太佩服会烘焙的人了。你知道吗，我在厨房里笨手笨脚的。你怎么能一边做这个一边还经营自己的生意？你简直太有才了！”你的芝士蛋糕暴露了这位自恋者在厨艺上的不足，而对此她毫无现成的防御。于是，她以一个大度的姿态把厨房让给你，转而把自己的优越搬到道德领域。“我也许不会烘焙，但论懂得欣赏、论慷慨大方，没人比得过我。小蛋糕做得不错，但我还是比你强。”看看一旦自恋者重新接通了自己的优越感，话题转换得有多快。再不会有人继续谈论你的烘焙作品，也不会谈论你别的任何才能了。

Obsequiousness toward those in power is another version of envy in disguise. Someone else’s power is very threatening to a Narcissist and evokes not only envy and contempt but fear of retaliation. Ingratiating behavior helps suppress dangerous thoughts and impulses while at the same time attempting to control the other person. By pleasing the one in power, the Narcissist may even be able to acquire some power of his or her own or “bask in the glow” of connection to someone perceived as superior.

对权势者卑躬屈膝，是嫉妒的另一种伪装形式。对自恋者来说，别人的权力极具威胁，它唤起的不仅是嫉妒和蔑视，还有对报复的恐惧。讨好巴结的行为有助于压制那些危险的念头与冲动，同时又试图控制对方。通过取悦掌权者，自恋者甚至可能为自己捞到一些权力，或者“沐浴”在与某个被视为高人一等之人相连结的光芒里。

Envy is such a common human foible that it made the biblical list of deadly sins. It is one of the reasons why we are fascinated when the rich, the famous, the brilliant, and the beautiful come to an untimely or ignominious end. “The bigger they are, the harder they fall” is more than a warning against hubris. There is something dark within us that gloats when the grand and glorious are toppled from their pedestals. We love their flaws and misfortunes, because it means that there is not so much distance between us.

嫉妒是人类如此常见的一种弱点，以至于它被列入了《圣经》中的诸宗罪。这也是为什么，当那些富有的、出名的、聪慧的、美丽的人遭遇英年早逝或身败名裂的结局时，我们会为之着迷的原因之一。“爬得越高，摔得越惨”不仅仅是对狂妄自大的警告。我们内心深处有某种阴暗的东西，会在那些尊贵显赫之人从神坛上被掀翻时暗自窃喜。我们喜爱他们的缺陷与不幸，因为这意味着我们彼此之间的距离并没有那么遥远。

Narcissistic envy, fueled by the desperate need to be superior, is something far darker. Like so much else that goes on within the Narcissist, it is unconscious or denied, which makes it that much more dangerous. Unaware of either envy or the need for superiority, these individuals may feel only self-righteous contempt. And that, dear reader, is just another word for hate.

而自恋性的嫉妒，由那种不顾一切想要高人一等的需要所驱动，则是某种远为阴暗的东西。和自恋者内心发生的许多其他事情一样，它是无意识的，或被否认的，而这恰恰使它危险得多。这些人既意识不到嫉妒，也意识不到自己对优越的需要，他们能感受到的，或许只有一种自以为是的蔑视。而那，亲爱的读者，不过是“恨”的另一种说法罢了。
